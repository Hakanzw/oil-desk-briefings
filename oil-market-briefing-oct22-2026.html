Top Story

⚡ Escalation — Washington Pulls Staff From Saudi Arabia as Houthi Strikes Reach Aramco's Shaybah Field

Brent Climbs Back Above $110 After US Orders Non-Emergency Staff Out of Saudi Arabia and a Drone Attack Hits Shaybah — Gasoil Gains 1.3%

Crude pushed higher on Thursday as the Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia took a more dangerous turn. Trading Economics showed Brent at $110.98, up about 1.96%, after Wednesday's settlement at $108.82, while early Asian quotes had put it near $110.11 and WTI near $98.12. The trigger was a State Department order, announced Wednesday by spokesperson Tommy Pigott, for non-emergency US government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities said two people were killed when air defences intercepted a drone aimed at a facility at Aramco's Shaybah oilfield, and an attack on Dammam's King Fahd airport killed one and injured eight. Fox News reported that President Trump is weighing direct strikes on the Houthis.

The physical picture remains contested, which is why volatility is high. Reuters, citing Kpler, put Hormuz flows at roughly 8.5 million barrels a day last week with at least 18 vessels attacked in two weeks, while a WSJ estimate cited by Oilprice.com put Hormuz and Gulf of Oman flows near 14 million. Diesel is again firm: Investing.com's London gasoil page showed the November contract at 1,450.75 a tonne, up 1.34% from 1,431.50; the page carried no date stamp, so treat it as an indicative early-session quote rather than a settlement. US distillate stocks rose 1.7 million barrels last week but remain about 9% below the five-year average, so the cushion is thin.

Diplomacy offers a partial offset. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi wrote in the Financial Times that Tehran wants a deal but is ready to defend itself, and President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran has submitted a peace plan, while Mohsen Rezaei said no enriched uranium would leave the country. PVM analysts noted that "neither of the two geopolitical conflicts underpinning the market's bullish sentiment has shown any signs of de-escalation." The risk is two-sided: a confirmed hit on Saudi export capacity could push Brent toward $115, while a credible Qatar- or Pakistan-brokered opening could take $5 or more off quickly.

Saudi / Houthis

US Orders Non-Emergency Staff to Leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi Attacks Intensify

The State Department on Wednesday ordered non-emergency US government employees and their families out of Saudi Arabia, with spokesperson Tommy Pigott stressing the order applies only to the kingdom, not the wider Gulf. Fox News reported that Saudi authorities said an attack on Dammam's King Fahd International Airport killed one person and injured eight, and that two people died when air defences intercepted a drone targeting Aramco's Shaybah oilfield. Britain said it would send Typhoon jets to help defend Saudi cities, and Trump is reportedly weighing direct strikes on the Houthis. Oil rose about 1.2% in early Asian trade. For crude, the concern is Saudi export capacity, including the Red Sea outlet; for diesel, it is Saudi refined-product flows.

Brent Hits $110.85 Midday Wednesday as Attacks Mount; PVM Sees No De-escalation

RTÉ reported that Brent rose $2.06, or 1.9%, to $110.85 on Wednesday and US WTI gained $2.13, or 2.2%, to $99.95, after prices had eased earlier in the week on mediation talk. Brent touched a five-month high of $111.99 on Tuesday before settling at $108.79. At least 18 vessel attacks were recorded over two weeks, per Kpler, and two US Energy Department officials said they are working with refiners worldwide to boost fuel output. PVM analysts said neither conflict supporting bullish sentiment had shown signs of de-escalation. Brent ultimately settled Wednesday at $108.82, so intraday swings of over $2 show how headline-driven the tape has become, with every dip so far attracting buyers.

03
Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Fall 3.1M Barrels to 412.5M; Distillates Build 1.7M but Stay 9% Below Average

Oilprice.com, citing EIA data released Wednesday, reported commercial crude inventories fell 3.1 million barrels to 412.5 million in the week ended October 16, about 3% below the five-year average. Gasoline stocks fell 3 million barrels after a 3.5 million-barrel gain the week before, while distillates rose 1.7 million barrels as production climbed to 5.2 million bpd; they remain about 9% below normal. Total products supplied averaged 21.1 million bpd over four weeks, up 3.2% year on year, though distillate demand was down 2.4%. API had flagged a 4.93 million-barrel crude draw. The report is mildly supportive for crude, but the distillate build is a small bearish note for diesel.

04
Hormuz Flows

Conflicting Flow Data: Kpler Says 8.5M bpd Through Hormuz, WSJ Cites Wider Gulf Flows Above Pre-War

Oilprice.com reported that Reuters, citing Kpler, estimates Hormuz flows at 8.5 million barrels a day because of intensified Iranian attacks, while a WSJ report put Middle East flows above pre-war levels at 24 million bpd in the week to October 18 and Hormuz plus Gulf of Oman flows near 14 million. Ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman, likely meant to reduce strike risk, may explain part of the gap and are pushing Persian Gulf freight rates to records; ING said this reflects "less efficient use of tankers." DBS's Suvro Sarkar called the recent dip a mix of profit-taking and hopes of a Qatar- or Pakistan-brokered deal, adding any deal remains distant.

05
Saudi Supply

Saudi Crude Exports Hit Five-Month High in August While Production Falls to a Record Low

Oilprice.com, relaying Reuters and JODI data, reported Saudi crude exports rose about 7.9% in August to 4.451 million bpd, the highest since March, while production fell to a record-low 6.24 million bpd from 8.135 million in July. Refinery throughput dropped 181,000 bpd to 2.297 million. UBS's Giovanni Staunovo said Saudi Arabia was able to further restore export volumes via terminals on both coasts. The data predate the latest Houthi strikes, but they underline why Yanbu and the Red Sea route matter. The Future Investment Initiative conference planned for October 26-29 has been postponed to 2027, and Riyadh agencies reportedly told staff to work from home. Lower refinery runs also tighten Saudi product supply for diesel.

06
Demand / Retail Fuel

US Gasoline at $4.28 as GM Pivots to Hybrids; Retail Diesel Above $6 Since September

Oilprice.com reported US gasoline averaging $4.28 a gallon against $3.04 a year earlier, with retail diesel above $6 since September, citing Reuters. GM chief Mary Barra said on the October 20 earnings call, "We will have hybrids," and CFO Paul Jacobson said that with these fuel prices "EV penetration may continue to go up." Toyota's electrified sales rose 28.5% in the third quarter. Resources for the Future estimated about 56,000 households shifted to hybrids or EVs between February and May. This is a slow demand response, but it shows pump prices are changing behaviour, which over time caps upside for crude and gasoline, even as heavy-duty diesel remains inelastic.

Natural Gas

Henry Hub Falls to Roughly $2.75, a Two-Month Low, on Mild Weather

Trading Economics showed Henry Hub natural gas near $2.75 per MMBtu on Thursday, down about 1.4%, its lowest in roughly two months as mild weather weighed on demand. LNG feedgas to US export terminals rose to 18.2 bcfd in October from 17.9 bcfd in September, while Lower 48 output averaged 111.4 bcfd, below the 113.3 bcfd record set in August and September. Storage is 1.3% above the five-year average, though the surplus is shrinking, and traders await the EIA storage report, expected to show another below-normal build. US gas remains decoupled from the Gulf crisis, so it eases pass-through costs for power and industrial users rather than moving crude.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, October 22, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent $106–$115 with a slightly upward bias, and that is where we are: Wednesday settled at $108.82 after trading above $110 intraday, and Thursday quotes sit near $110.98. The range held, but the quality of the news has deteriorated, because Saudi territory is now directly involved and Washington is reducing its diplomatic footprint there.

My read on today: Diesel remains the sharper instrument. Gasoil at roughly $1,450.75 a tonne, up about 1.3%, is firm, and US distillate stocks, despite a 1.7 million-barrel build, remain about 9% below average. With retail diesel above $6 a gallon, Russian and sanctions-relief barrels look small next to global demand. I expect cracks to stay wide unless Gulf product exports recover materially.

The bigger picture: The data conflict is as important as the headlines. Kpler says Hormuz flows are near 8.5 million bpd; another estimate says 14 million. Saudi exports recovered in August but production hit a record low, leaving little spare buffer. A Fed hike next week and positioning that is stretched after a $20-plus rally mean a diplomatic opening could produce a fast $5 to $8 drop. Natural gas at $2.75 is a quiet sideshow.

My call: Brent trades $107–$116 today, with an upward bias while Saudi security risks dominate, and sharp downside on any verified reopening of lanes or a credible US-Iran channel. The premium is large and fragile, but only safe passage and quiet over Saudi skies can truly remove it.
