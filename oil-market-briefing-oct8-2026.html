⚡ Thursday Open — A Pre-Midterm Iran Strike Option Puts the Risk Premium Back Into Brent

Brent Jumps 2.2% to $102.42 on Report the White House Wants Iran Strike Options Before the Midterms — as US Crude Stocks Draw 3.2 Million Barrels

Brent crude rose 2.22% to $102.42 a barrel on Thursday, according to Trading Economics, reversing Wednesday's losses and pushing back above the $100 handle that has defined the week. The proximate trigger was an Atlantic report, carried by the Jerusalem Post and Iran International, that the White House has asked the Pentagon to develop strike options against Iran that could be executed before the November 3 midterm elections. US Central Command is drafting the options, no decision has been taken, and a White House official said only that Trump "has all options available at any time." Trading Economics also pointed to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, which prompted fresh coalition strikes in Yemen, and to a tropical storm in the Gulf of Mexico that reportedly shut in more than 510,000 barrels a day of US crude output.

The physical picture remains a tug-of-war. OilPrice.com, citing Vitol via Reuters, reported roughly 12 million barrels a day of crude and 2 million of fuels leaving the Persian Gulf, and Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said the East-West pipeline had rebounded to 5.8 million bpd. Yet the Saudi aviation authority confirmed attacks on two airports, including Jazan, home to a 400,000 bpd Aramco refinery. ING described "a clear tug-of-war" between improving regional supply and lingering threats, while Sparta Commodities' June Goh warned that supertanker rates now exceed $1 million per day, a cost that is spreading to smaller vessels and ultimately lands in delivered product prices.

Washington's own signals are contradictory, which is what keeps volatility high. Supporters of limited strikes reportedly concede they would not secure a deal, reopen Hormuz or lower gasoline prices before Election Day, and some advisers favour waiting until after the vote. Meanwhile, indirect US-Iran contacts continue, with Vice President Vance demanding "concrete reductions" in enrichment capacity and a senior Iranian official calling enrichment rights a red line. For crude the asymmetry is plain: returning Gulf barrels and the G7 stock release cap the downside narrative, but a market with stockpiles Aramco's Amin Nasser has called "scarily thin" must price a non-trivial chance that the next headline is a strike, not a settlement.

The inventory data added a mild bullish note. The EIA reported US commercial crude stocks fell 3.2 million barrels to 424.1 million in the week ended October 2, against a Reuters poll expecting a 1.7 million barrel build, while gasoline rose 0.4 million barrels and distillates held near 105.1 million, a level that leaves diesel-producing economies with little cushion.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Geopolitics

White House Asks Pentagon for Iran Strike Options Before the Midterms, The Atlantic Reports

The Atlantic reported on October 7 that the White House has asked the Pentagon to prepare strike options against Iran that could be carried out before the November 3 midterms, citing two administration officials. US Central Command is developing the plans; targets and scope remain under discussion and no decision has been made. Officials tie the effort to Trump's desire to show progress in the eight-month war and to lower energy prices. The Pentagon has also presented post-election options up to a broader campaign that could include Iran's energy industry. For crude, the report revived a strike-risk premium: Brent rose above $102, and any move on Iranian energy infrastructure would hit a market with little inventory buffer, particularly in diesel.

02
Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Fall 3.2 Million Barrels as Distillates Hold Near 105 Million

US crude inventories fell 3.2 million barrels to 424.1 million in the week ended October 2, the EIA said, against a Reuters poll for a 1.7 million barrel rise. Stocks at the Cushing, Oklahoma hub rose 444,000 barrels. Gasoline stocks rose 0.4 million barrels to 204.7 million versus an expected 1.7 million draw, while distillates were little changed at 105.1 million versus an expected 2.1 million drop. Refinery runs rose 223,000 bpd and utilisation edged up 0.2 points to 92.7%, and net crude imports fell 53,000 bpd. The draw is supportive for crude, but flat distillates when a draw was expected show demand rationing at $6-plus diesel rather than genuine comfort in supply.

Brent Back Above $100 as Houthis Hit Saudi Infrastructure

OilPrice.com's Irina Slav reported that crude reversed Tuesday's dip, with Brent near $101.50 and WTI near $90 on Wednesday, after Saudi aviation authorities confirmed attacks on two airports, including Jazan, home to a 400,000 bpd Aramco refinery. Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said East-West pipeline flows had rebounded to 5.8 million bpd, countering reports of another shutdown. Vitol put Gulf exports at about 12 million bpd of crude and 2 million of fuels. ING called it a tug-of-war, and Sparta's June Goh flagged supertanker rates above $1 million a day. The takeaway: export volumes are recovering, but attacks on Saudi assets keep a premium in refined products and freight.

Policy / Stocks

IEA Weighs G7's 100-Million-Barrel Oil and Diesel Release; Details Still Missing

The IEA held an informal meeting Wednesday on releasing 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, EU diplomats told Reuters, after the G7 announced a four-month release including a front-loaded diesel tranche within the first 20 days. No volumes or timelines have been agreed, and it is unclear whether the barrels overlap with the 400 million barrel spring action, of which IEA chief Fatih Birol says about 325 million (over 80%) has been delivered. Washington has pressed France and Germany to release diesel ahead of the midterms. Analysts call the plan a temporary fix that further drains depleted stocks, so the release may flatten diesel spreads briefly without changing Brent's direction.

05
Refining / Diesel

Shell Flags Record $42 Refining Margin as Trump Defers Federal Tax on Dyed Diesel

Shell expects its indicative Q3 refining margin at $42 a barrel, up from $24 in Q2, citing limited Middle East fuel flows and a Russian diesel export ban that the report says has removed an estimated 7-8 million bpd of refined products from the market. Equinor also flagged unusually strong European margins. Separately, Trump signed an order deferring the federal tax on dyed diesel through year-end; AAA put US diesel at $6.32 a gallon on October 5, below the $6.53 record in late September. The tax saving is about 24 cents a gallon, which analysts doubt will matter much. Margins at these levels confirm product tightness is the binding constraint, not crude.

OPEC+ / Blockade

OPEC+ Holds November Quota at 31.01 mb/d While 50 Iranian Tankers Sit Stuck Behind the US Blockade

OPEC+ kept its November quota unchanged at 31.01 million bpd for the eight members covered, as expected, though actual August output was only about 25 million bpd. UBS's Giovanni Staunovo said output remains well below quota and the market stays tight. Kpler put Middle East flows at 18.5 million bpd for the week to October 1. Separately, United Against Nuclear Iran counted at least 50 laden Iranian tankers stuck in the Gulf, up to 16 near Kharg Island, since the US reinstated its blockade on July 14, and Bloomberg estimated Iran's crude loadings fell to zero in September. Quotas are largely paper; the blockade and attacks decide physical supply.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, October 8, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent a range trade, capped by returning barrels and the G7 pledge, with tanker attacks as the upside skew. Today the skew showed up: Brent is $102.42, up 2.22%, and the catalyst is political rather than physical. A report that the White House is seeking Iran strike options before the midterms is a reminder that this war has repeatedly produced sudden policy headlines, and that the market's cushion against them is stocks, not spare capacity. Nothing has been decided, and some advisers prefer waiting, so I treat it as a premium rather than a new regime.

My read on today: The data are mixed but lean supportive. US crude drew 3.2 million barrels against an expected build, Gulf exports are near pre-war levels per Vitol and Kpler, the East-West line is at 5.8 million bpd, and OPEC+ is holding quotas that it is not even meeting. I would not chase above $105 without a confirmed escalation, because the G7 release and Gulf flows limit follow-through, but I also would not fade a dip toward $99 given how thin inventories are.

Diesel remains the stronger story. ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil is about $1,427.50 per tonne, up 0.46% over 24 hours, with a second source roughly in line. Shell's record $42 refining margin, US diesel at $6.32 and distillates stuck near 105 million barrels all say the same thing: product tightness exceeds crude tightness. The front-loaded diesel tranche of the G7 release is the main bearish catalyst, so details on timing matter more for gasoil than for Brent.

The bigger picture: Midterm politics now sit on both sides of the trade: a strike could spike prices, while the push for stock releases and tax relief aims to lower them. My call: Brent trades $98–$106 today, biased higher while the strike story is live, with a break below $96 requiring a credible Hormuz and ceasefire timetable. Gasoil should stay bid on cracks unless the diesel release is large and quick.
