Top Story

⚡ Friday — Brent Holds Above $105 and Heads for a Weekly Gain as Hormuz Attacks, Saudi Fighting and a Huge US Crude Draw Outweigh Sanctions Relief Talk

Brent Edges Down 0.64% to $105.24 and Gasoil Falls 1.82% to About $1,402 Even as EIA Reports the Biggest US Crude Draw Since February

Oil ended the week close to its one-month highs. Trading Economics showed Brent at $105.24, down 0.64% from a prior close of $105.92, while HDFC Sky, citing futures data, put Brent at $105.3 (−0.6%) and WTI at $97.3 (−0.7%). Crude is on course for a weekly rise, up roughly 71% year on year. London gasoil for November delivery (LGOX6, our ULS1! proxy) showed $1,402.50 a tonne, down 1.82% from $1,428.50 on Investing.com, though that page carried no date stamp, so treat the level as approximate. Thursday's session saw WTI settle more than 3% higher while Brent finished marginally lower, narrowing the spread as cheaper freight lifted demand for American barrels.

The inventory data were the hardest bullish datapoint of the week. The EIA reported US crude stocks fell 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million in the week ended October 9, the largest drop since February and far above the 288,000-barrel decline analysts expected. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve remains near its record low. Against that, Washington is weighing relief for shadow-fleet tankers that have carried Russian, Iranian and Venezuelan oil, aimed at record freight rates, and the IEA agreed to release more than 20 million barrels of diesel plus the remainder of the March emergency pledge. Prices rose after the IEA announcement, suggesting traders doubt its scale.

Underneath it all the security picture keeps worsening. A Singapore-flagged container ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz after what US Central Command said was an Iranian projectile strike, vessels have been attacked across the region for ten days, and Houthi missiles hit the presidential palace complex in Aden while the Saudi-led coalition reports dozens of attacks on Saudi infrastructure. Iran says it is stepping up strikes in response to the US blockade of its ports. The dip in gasoil looks like policy-driven profit-taking, not a loosening of physical tightness, and the IEA itself warns that tight diesel markets could persist into next year.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Plunge 8.6 Million Barrels, the Largest Draw Since February

The US Energy Information Administration reported that commercial crude inventories fell 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million in the week ended October 9, against analyst expectations for a decline of just 288,000 barrels, according to HDFC Sky and Trading Economics. It was the biggest weekly drop since February, and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is still hovering near its record low, leaving the government with little buffer. The surprise came a week after the API-reported 11.1 million barrel draw and confirms that US barrels are being pulled abroad as buyers chase supply elsewhere. For crude the print is clearly supportive and helps explain why WTI outperformed Brent this week. Lower freight on US cargoes is strengthening export demand.

02
Sanctions / Freight

US Considers Lifting Sanctions on Some Shadow-Fleet Tankers to Ease Record Freight Rates

A US official said the Trump administration is considering lifting sanctions on some shadow-fleet tankers that have carried oil from Russia, Iran and Venezuela, with the aim of easing record-high global freight rates. Washington recently lifted sanctions on Russian diesel temporarily, a move criticised by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, European governments and some Republican senators. Analysts quoted earlier in the week by Energy Aspects expect only limited relief because mostly older vessels would be freed and European insurance rules would still bind. For crude and diesel, the headline trims expected delivered costs at the margin and capped gains on Friday, but the physical tonnage released looks small relative to the disruption in Hormuz.

03
Hormuz / Iran

Container Ship Sinks in Hormuz After Iranian Projectile Strike, Tanker Rates at Records

Trading Economics reported that a Singapore-flagged container ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz earlier this week, and US Central Command said an Iranian projectile hit it. The incident added uncertainty about the recovery of physical oil flows, which had nearly returned to prewar levels in September, and pushed tanker rates to record highs. Reuters noted that SEB analyst Bjarne Schieldrop said the sense of some kind of normality of exports from the strait was shattered. Iran says it is intensifying strikes in response to a US blockade of its ports and tougher sanctions imposed last month. Higher war-risk premiums and convoy delays matter most for diesel cargoes heading to Europe, so further hits on merchant shipping are an upside catalyst for Brent and gasoil.

Saudi Arabia / Houthis

Houthi–Saudi Fighting Escalates: Aden Palace Struck, Dozens of Attacks on Saudi Infrastructure Reported

HDFC Sky reported that Houthi forces fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden, killing two guards, while the Saudi-led coalition reported dozens of attacks on Saudi infrastructure. Earlier this month Reuters reported a Houthi strike on Riyadh's international airport that killed 13 people and wounded more than 300, and CNBC covered the strike on an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran. A separate report said the attacks forced Saudi Arabia to shut its East-West oil pipeline. Production has not been publicly reported as lost, but the targeting is drifting toward energy assets. With spare capacity thin, a hit on an export terminal would remove barrels that the market cannot easily replace, which is why dips toward $102 keep attracting buyers.

05
IEA / Diesel

IEA Agrees to Release Remaining Emergency Stocks and 20+ Million Barrels of Diesel

The International Energy Agency agreed to release the remaining oil from the record emergency release pledged in March, plus more than 20 million barrels of diesel, after lobbying from US officials. Danske Bank noted earlier this month that the plan largely accelerates volumes already committed within the 400 million barrel total rather than adding new supply, and IEA chief Fatih Birol urged members that have not delivered their pledged volumes to speed up, prioritising diesel. Trading Economics reports the IEA also warned that tight diesel markets could persist into next year. Prices rose after the announcement, showing doubt about scale and duration. For ULS1! the release is a modest bearish offset but does not address refinery outages in Russia or shipping risk.

06
Natural Gas

Henry Hub Eases to $3.23 as EIA Reports 67 Bcf Storage Injection, Below Forecast

Trading Economics put Henry Hub natural gas near $3.23 per MMBtu, down about 0.28% but still up roughly 11% over the month. The EIA reported a 67 bcf injection for the week ending October 9, smaller than the 76 bcf expected and the 90 bcf five-year average, leaving inventories at 3.567 trillion cubic feet, 3.9% below a year earlier and 1.3% above the seasonal norm. Lower 48 output averaged 110.8 bcfd in October, below the record 113.3 bcfd, and LNG feedgas averaged 17.5 bcfd versus 17.9 bcfd in September. Forecasts call for milder weather through October 29, which limits heating and cooling demand and caps rallies. Gas is a secondary driver for oil, but firmer power-sector fuel costs add to the energy inflation backdrop.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, October 16, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called a $101–$108 Brent range with a mild upward bias after profit-taking. That held: Brent is at $105.24, down 0.64% on Trading Economics and set for a weekly gain. The EIA data then arrived at 8.6 million barrels below the prior week, against a consensus near 0.3 million, which is exactly the sort of surprise that justifies buying dips rather than selling rallies in this tape.

My read on today: The market is balancing a hard physical signal against soft policy headlines. Shadow-fleet relief and the IEA diesel release are real but small, and Energy Aspects' view that they add little tonnage still stands. Hormuz attacks and the Saudi–Houthi fighting are getting worse, not better. I would respect support near $102 and not chase above $108 without a new escalation.

Diesel remains the sharper instrument. London gasoil for November is near $1,402.50 a tonne, down 1.82%, though the quote lacks a verified timestamp, so I treat the level as approximate. Even so, the move is a policy pullback, not an easing of tightness: the IEA warns of tight diesel into next year, Russian refineries are under fire, and Goldman sees only $1 to $2 per barrel from extra Russian cargoes. Next Wednesday's distillate figure is the real test.

The bigger picture: Goldman's $90 December forecast and its risk premium estimate of just over $20 per barrel mean the path over months is lower, and the EIA now sees Q4 Brent near $105, so today is roughly fair on its numbers. None of that helps into a weekend where a single strike can reprice the curve. My call: Brent trades $102–$109 today, with a mild upward bias and an upside tail if Saudi export assets or tankers are hit.
