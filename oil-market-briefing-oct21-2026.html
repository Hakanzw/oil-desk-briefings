Top Story

⚡ Premium Persists — Brent Holds Near $110 as Gulf Flows Falter and Houthis Hit Saudi Arabia

Brent Hovers Near $110 at a Five-Month High as Hormuz Tanker Attacks and Houthi Strikes on Saudi Arabia Keep the Risk Premium Alive — Gasoil Jumps 2%

Crude opened Wednesday near the highest levels since mid-May. LiveOilPrices showed Brent at $110.32, up about 0.7% on a day range of $108.87 to $110.43, after Monday's settlement at $109.55 and a Tuesday print as high as $111.30 reported by The National. Brent first touched $110 on Friday for the first time since May. The rally is being driven less by policy than by physical danger: Kpler, cited by Reuters, counts at least 18 tanker attacks in two weeks, and the seven-day average of oil flowing out of the Persian Gulf has dropped to roughly 8.5 million barrels a day from more than 20 million only two weeks earlier, erasing the recovery that had taken exports back toward pre-war levels.

Diesel is again the sharper signal. Investing.com's London gasoil page showed the November contract at 1,478.63 per tonne, up 2.01% from a previous close of 1,449.50; the page carried no explicit date stamp, so treat it as an indicative early-session quote rather than a settlement. Wholesale diesel has crossed $200 a unit in Singapore and about $220 in Europe, and the International Energy Agency agreed last week to release more than 20 million barrels. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Monday that Washington will let sanctioned dark-fleet tankers ship crude legitimately, but Kpler estimates only 24 of nearly 160 OFAC-listed vessels could return to mainstream trade and says easing would barely dent record freight rates.

The macro overlay is turning hostile. Higher energy costs are feeding inflation, and analysts expect the Federal Reserve to deliver a second consecutive rate hike at next week's meeting; Duncan Oil analysts said Brent near five-month highs has "reignited fears of sticky inflation." ING's Warren Patterson and Ewa Manthey noted the market is pricing a "significant risk premium" and that there are "no signs" yet of it fading. The setup is two-sided: supply risk is real and rising, but a Fed shock or a de-escalation headline could unwind crowded length quickly.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Hormuz Shipping

Gulf Flows Collapse to 8.5M bpd as Iranian Strikes Hit Tankers Again

Oilprice.com, citing Reuters and shipping-data firm Kpler, reported that Iranian strikes on tankers have again sharply reduced flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Kpler counts at least 18 tanker attacks in the last two weeks, and the daily average of oil leaving the Persian Gulf fell to about 8.5 million barrels, against more than 20 million barrels per day just two weeks earlier. Kpler had said earlier this month that exports had recovered to pre-war levels, so the reversal is abrupt. For crude it removes the most important bearish argument of the past month, namely normalising Gulf supply. For diesel it is worse, because refined-product exports from the Gulf were already lagging crude recovery.

02
Saudi / Houthis

Houthis Claim Six Attacks on Saudi Arabia, Including Riyadh's King Khalid Airport

The National reported that on Monday the Houthis claimed six attacks on Saudi Arabia, among them strikes on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, a military airbase near Abha International Airport, a naval task-force camp and a radar at Bir Askar. The attacks pushed Gulf equity markets lower. Brent rose 1.46% to $111.20 and WTI climbed 2.3% to $100.80 in Tuesday trading, levels last seen in mid-May. Earlier Houthi claims have named Aramco's Yanbu facility and the Shaybah oilfield, though damage has not been independently confirmed in the sources reviewed. Yanbu matters because it is the Red Sea outlet for Saudi crude that bypasses Hormuz, so any damage would remove the main workaround.

Sanctions / Dark Fleet

Wright Says Sanctioned 'Dark Fleet' Tankers Can Ship Crude — Kpler Sees Little Relief

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said on Monday that Washington will allow sanctioned dark-fleet tankers to begin legitimately shipping crude, an effort to ease tight supply. Analytics firm Kpler responded that only 24 of the nearly 160 vessels sanctioned by the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control could realistically return to mainstream trade, and that easing sanctions would have little effect on record freight rates. The gap between the policy headline and the practical effect explains why prices have repeatedly dipped on such announcements and then recovered. With attacks on ships rising, the binding constraint is security and insurance, not sanctions paperwork, so the barrels freed by the move look small relative to the Gulf shortfall.

Diesel / Russia

Russian Diesel Pledge: 300,000 Tonnes Promised, but Skepticism Lingers

Oilprice.com reported earlier this month that President Trump said Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel, about 2.25 million barrels, with more to follow, while Moscow partially lifted diesel export restrictions to allow another 500,000 tons, roughly 3.7 million barrels, onto world markets. Trump also said Russia and Ukraine agreed to stop attacking each other's energy infrastructure, but Zelensky accused Russia of violating the deal. Against global diesel demand near 29 million barrels per day, these volumes are small. Wholesale diesel above $200 in Singapore and near $220 in Europe shows the market is not yet convinced, which is why gasoil, at about 1,478 per tonne, remains close to its highs.

05
Inventories / OPEC+

EIA: US Crude Draws 8.6M Barrels to 415.6M; OPEC+ Holds November Targets

The latest EIA data available at press time, for the week ended October 9, showed commercial crude inventories falling 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million, about 3% below the five-year average, while total motor gasoline stocks rose 3.5 million barrels; the American Petroleum Institute had estimated a larger 11.12 million-barrel draw. The following week's EIA report is due today. Separately, OPEC+ kept November production targets unchanged, with the seven core members producing about 25 million bpd in August, roughly 5 million bpd below February levels, and the group meets again on November 1. For crude, the quotas matter less than the fact that war-limited exports leave real output below targets.

Natural Gas

Henry Hub Slips to a Four-Week Low Near $2.95 on Mild Weather

Trading Economics put Henry Hub natural gas at $2.95 per MMBtu, down 0.45% on the day and at its lowest since September, with prices down 14.5% year on year. Mild weather is limiting heating and cooling demand and expectations of a strong El Niño weigh on the winter outlook. LNG feedgas to the nine major US export terminals rose to 18.2 bcfd in October from 17.9 bcfd in September, while Lower 48 output averaged 111.4 bcfd, below the record 113.3 bcfd of August and September. Storage is 1.3% above the five-year average. US gas is decoupled from the Gulf crisis, but it limits the cost pass-through to US power and industrial users.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, October 21, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent $105–$114 with a slightly upward bias, and the tape complied: Brent has held a $109–$111 band and is quoted at roughly $110.32 this morning, against a Monday settlement of $109.55. The Russian-diesel and dark-fleet headlines produced brief dips, but each was bought. That repeating pattern tells us policy announcements are being treated as small relative to the physical problem in the Gulf.

My read on today: Diesel remains the leader. Gasoil at roughly $1,478.63 a tonne, up about 2%, is back near the top of its recent range, and the Russian and sanctions relief measures amount to a few million barrels against 29 million barrels a day of demand. With wholesale diesel above $200 in Singapore and near $220 in Europe, I expect cracks to stay wide into winter unless Gulf product exports recover from their roughly 60% of pre-war level.

The bigger picture: The data conflict is notable. Official commentary points to substantial Gulf recovery in September, but Kpler's latest seven-day average of about 8.5M bpd says that recovery has reversed in October. Add Houthi claims against Saudi sites and a Fed expected to hike again, and the risk is two-sided. Positioning is stretched, so a diplomatic opening could produce a fast $5 to $8 drop. Natural gas at $2.95 is a quiet sideshow.

My call: Brent trades $106–$115 today, with a modest upward bias while tanker attacks and Saudi risks dominate, and sharp downside on any verified reopening of lanes. The premium is large and fragile, but only safe passage can truly remove it.
