⚡ Breaking — Iran Strikes Kuwait, Hormuz Approaching Zero: The Supply Crisis Escalates Again

Iran Attacks Kuwait Power and Water Plant Overnight — Brent Surges 4.6% to $88.10 as Hormuz Transits Collapse to Five Per Day

The conflict expanded its geographic footprint overnight. Iran's armed forces struck a power generation and water desalination facility in Kuwait, triggering fires and knocking out a significant portion of the emirate's generating capacity. The attack is the most direct Iranian strike on Kuwaiti infrastructure since the war began in late February, and came in the immediate aftermath of US Central Command announcing its sixth consecutive night of strikes on Iranian military targets — logistics infrastructure, maritime capabilities, and command nodes. The market read the expansion of the conflict swiftly: Brent crude surged +4.59% to $88.10 per barrel in the July 18 session, the front-month contract's largest single-day gain since June and a move that pushes it toward the critical $90 psychological threshold traders have been watching since the July 7 Hormuz tanker attacks.

Beneath the headline strike, the structural deterioration in Strait of Hormuz transit traffic continued without interruption. Lloyd's List Intelligence data places daily crossings at approximately five vessels on Friday — down from the roughly ten per day flagged in yesterday's briefing and a fraction of the ~130 per day that characterized normal pre-war traffic. At five transits per day, the world's most critical oil chokepoint is not disrupted; it is functionally closed. The Strait carries an estimated 20% of global seaborne crude and 20% of global LNG. Iran also reportedly directed Houthi forces in Yemen to prepare to disrupt traffic at Bab el-Mandeb — adding the Red Sea's second critical choke point to an already extreme risk landscape.

The supply shock is now a multi-front event. Ukraine's armed forces struck the Omsk refinery — Russia's largest, with capacity exceeding 21 million metric tons annually — in one of Kyiv's deepest strikes on Russian territory, halting operations. A follow-on strike on the Saratov refinery compounded the damage; Ukrainian sources estimate that 42.7% of Russia's total refining capacity is now offline. Simultaneously, the IEA's July 2026 Oil Market Report confirmed that global supply remains 9.4 million barrels per day below pre-war February levels even after a partial Hormuz recovery in June. That recovery is now reversing. China responded to the week's 12% crude surge by raising retail gasoline prices 300 yuan per tonne — the first concrete demand-side signal, though analysts note meaningful demand destruction at these price levels remains weeks away.

OPEC+

Fifth Consecutive Monthly Hike Approved — 188k bpd for August, But Gulf Delivery Blocked by Hormuz

Seven OPEC+ member states — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman — approved a 188,000-barrel-per-day output increase effective August 2026 on July 6, the group's fifth straight monthly hike as it continues unwinding 2023-era cuts. The decision was taken when Brent was trading near $72 following the June ceasefire. The group now has approximately 379,000 bpd of original cuts still to return. Analysts note the increase is largely theoretical for Gulf producers: total OPEC+ output has fallen from 42.77 million bpd in February to 33.13 million bpd in May, with Hormuz closures blocking barrel delivery regardless of paper quota levels.

Ukraine Drones Strike Omsk Refinery — Russia's Largest Halted; 42.7% of Refining Capacity Now Offline

Ukrainian drones struck the Omsk oil refinery in western Siberia on July 6-7, halting operations at Russia's largest facility — with capacity exceeding 21 million metric tons per year — in one of Kyiv's deepest-range strikes on Russian infrastructure, covering approximately 2,500 km from Ukrainian territory. A follow-on strike on the Saratov refinery on July 8 further disabled Russian processing capacity. Ukrainian sources estimate that combined strikes have taken offline an estimated 42.7% of Russia's total refining capacity, adding a secondary supply shock to the primary Middle East disruption.

China Raises Retail Gasoline and Diesel Prices — 300 Yuan/Tonne on Fuel as Crude Surges 12% in a Week

China's National Development and Reform Commission announced fuel price hikes effective July 18 — gasoline up 300 yuan per tonne (~$44) and diesel up 290 yuan per tonne (~$43) — following international crude prices surging more than 12% in a single week due to Middle East escalation. The adjustment follows China's standard pricing formula tied to international benchmarks. Analysts warn that China's gasoline consumption could contract 5.5% in 2026 if elevated prices persist, introducing a demand-destruction dynamic that partially counteracts the supply-disruption narrative.

IEA

IEA: Global Oil Supply 9.4 mb/d Below Pre-War Levels — Demand to Fall 1 mb/d in 2026

The IEA's July 2026 Oil Market Report found that global supply rebounded 4.1 million barrels per day in June to 98.8 mb/d as some Hormuz flows temporarily resumed, but output remained 9.4 mb/d below pre-war February levels. Gulf exports of refined products and LPG stayed below 50% of pre-war levels even as crude flows briefly recovered to nearly three-quarters of February rates, reflecting that key Gulf export refineries have not resumed operations. The IEA projects global oil demand declining 1 mb/d in 2026 before recovering 2 mb/d in 2027, with annual demand contractions easing from 4.8 mb/d in Q2 to 1.7 mb/d in Q3.

The situation as of this morning: Iran has now attacked a GCC member state's critical infrastructure directly. Hormuz is at five transits per day and trending toward zero. The June ceasefire framework is not merely paused — it is structurally finished. Ukraine has taken offline 42.7% of Russia's refining capacity. China is raising retail fuel prices today, printing the first observable demand-response data point in a market that has been supply-shock-dominated for months. This is a synchronized multi-theater supply crisis, and $88 is not the ceiling — it may not even be the midpoint.

The one caveat worth holding onto from the physical data: China's price hike is the market's internal circuit-breaker showing its first spark. A 5.5% contraction in Chinese gasoline demand — if it materializes at sustained prices above $85 Brent — would represent meaningful demand destruction at scale. The distillate build reported in last week's EIA data (+4.6 million barrels) is a second data point in the same direction. Demand-side pressure will not arrest today's move, but it is the structural release valve the market will need eventually. Watch China's July demand data in early August for the first real read.

My call: Brent trades $85–$97 in the sessions ahead, with the Kuwait escalation and Hormuz near-closure establishing the structural floor around $85–$87. The upside scenario — Hormuz at confirmed zero transits, further Gulf state infrastructure attacks, or Bab el-Mandeb closure — puts $97–$102 in range, with $100 no longer the extreme tail it appeared last week. The downside case remains the same binary: a credible back-channel diplomatic signal via Qatar or Oman is the only mechanism capable of taking more than $5/bbl off this market before fundamentals catch up. The ceiling has moved. This weekend's events will determine how much.
