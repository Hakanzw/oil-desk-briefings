⚡ Monday Open — US Strikes Iranian Tankers; Scenario 2 Materialises at $97 as Kharg Island Spared — For Now

US Military Strikes Three Iranian Crude Tankers After IRGC Targets US Carrier — Brent Opens at $97.30; Iran Vows Escalated Retaliation and Announces Expanded Maritime Zone; Kharg Island Spared — For Now

The tail risk flagged in Sunday's briefing materialised overnight — but at the lesser end of the escalation spectrum. The United States struck three Iranian crude oil tankers near Kharg Island and in the Gulf of Oman on Saturday, permanently disabling two vessels and sinking a third, after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired ballistic missiles at a US aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer in the Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM confirmed the strikes early Sunday, with Rear Admiral Brad Cooper stating: "If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost." Critically, Kharg Island itself — which handles roughly 90% of Iran's crude exports — was not directly struck, sparing the market the $110–$120/bbl Brent scenario outlined in Sunday's column. Brent opened the ICE London Monday session at approximately $97.30, up 2.2% from Friday's $95.23 close, with CME closed for the US Labor Day holiday adding a liquidity premium to intraday volatility.

Iran's response hardened the strategic picture considerably. Tehran launched its own retaliatory strikes Sunday, attacking three additional tankers and three US-linked vessels in the Strait, before Iran's Parliament Speaker issued what analysts are reading as the most consequential threat of the entire conflict: "the era of proportionate responses has come to an end." Tehran simultaneously announced it would declare a restricted maritime zone beyond the Strait of Hormuz within days — a move that, if implemented, would extend Iran's exclusion perimeter into the broader Arabian Sea, threatening the UAE's Fujairah terminal, which has functioned as a partial alternative crude loading hub since Hormuz throughput collapsed to roughly 3.29 million barrels per day from its pre-war baseline near 20 million bpd. Multiple bank analysts flagged Monday morning that Tehran's announced zone expansion raises the probability of a Kharg Island strike within 48–72 hours — the one escalation step that would push Brent well above $110.

The physical market backdrop entering this session is the tightest since early May. US retail diesel stands at a record $5.85 per gallon. Distillate inventories are 14% below the five-year seasonal average — entering the northern hemisphere heating season at a critical deficit. The Low Sulphur Gasoil September contract (ULSU2026) expires Wednesday, September 10, with the October roll now pricing winter distillate replenishment through a Hormuz corridor still running at approximately 13% of pre-war throughput. ICE London is open today despite the US Labor Day holiday, but thinner overall liquidity could amplify intraday price swings on any new headline. The OPEC+ JMMC issued its Q4 2026 production-freeze communiqué Sunday — already priced by the market, directionally neutral — but notably absent of any explicit force-majeure language addressing the Israeli energy-infrastructure threat, leaving that variable open as Iran's escalation calculus evolves.

Strait of Hormuz Disruption Now Twice the Scale of the 1973 Oil Crisis — World Bank Analysis

A World Bank analysis found that the 2026 disruption to global oil supplies has exceeded twice the magnitude of the 1973 Arab oil embargo — the largest supply shortfall on record. Kuwait, Qatar, and Bahrain have no alternative export route around the Strait, concentrating the shock in ways 1973 did not. Middle East crude exports in August ran 39% below the January–February baseline — a 7.2 million bpd deficit — as Hormuz throughput fell to approximately 3.29 million bpd from roughly 20 million bpd before February 28. The IEA's 400-million-barrel SPR release has partially offset the shock but has not reversed price pressure. Iran's announced maritime zone expansion would further degrade even this residual throughput.

The Guru's Verdict — Monday, September 7, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Sunday's briefing framed three scenarios for Monday's open: no strike ($93–$94), limited strikes ($98–$103), Kharg Island ($110–$120). The market woke up to a version of Scenario Two: real strikes, real escalation, real tankers sunk — but Kharg Island still standing. Brent opening at $97.30 is precisely what the limited-strike scenario predicted. It is a rational price. It is also, as of Monday morning in Tehran, an already-outdated one. The Parliament Speaker has declared proportionate retaliation over. The maritime zone announcement is not a rhetorical gesture — it is operational preparation. The question for this session is not whether the weekend's strikes were priced. They were. The question is whether the next round will be.

My read on today: The market enters Monday with a risk premium that fits a world in which Iran retaliates proportionately and Kharg Island remains undisturbed. That world may have ended Sunday. Every rally above $99 is the market buying a Kharg Island scenario. Every sell below $96 is the market trusting that cool heads have prevailed again. In a session with CME closed for US Labor Day and ICE London carrying the full weight of price discovery alone, there is less liquidity to absorb any shock that arrives. The morning wires from Tehran, Tel Aviv, and CENTCOM matter more than any inventory number or OPEC communiqué published this week.

The structural picture: Nothing in the weekend's events has improved the physical market. Distillates remain 14% below the five-year seasonal average entering the heating season. Hormuz throughput is still roughly 13% of pre-war capacity — and the announced Iranian maritime zone expansion threatens the residual Fujairah workaround that has kept some Atlantic Basin supply flowing. The ULS1! September contract expires Wednesday; the October roll will price winter distillate supply replenishment through whatever corridor Iran's next move leaves open. US retail diesel is at a record $5.85 per gallon. Freeport LNG returned to full operations, easing the US domestic gas market at the margin — it does not address the global LNG disruption premium. The Russia-Ukraine ceasefire track is diplomatically active for the first time in years but months from translating into barrels. The OPEC+ Q4 freeze is priced and already absorbed. There is no bullish supply narrative in this session — only varying degrees of unpriced tail risk.

My call: Brent $94–$100 today, with the range entirely hostage to headline risk around Iran's maritime zone declaration and any further military action in the coming hours. The $97.30 open is the equilibrium for a session in which nothing more happens before London close. Given that this session opens with three sunken tankers, a parliamentary declaration that proportionate responses are over, and an imminent Iranian restricted-zone announcement, the assumption that nothing more happens is the market's most exposed position. Watch the wires, not the chart.
