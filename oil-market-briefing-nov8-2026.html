⚡ Hormuz Under Fire — Brent Holds Near $98 Into the Weekend as Iran Reports More Tanker Fires and Exports Slip

Brent Holds $98.32 and Gasoil $1,364.50 as Iran Reports Further Tanker Fires in Hormuz and Gulf Oil Exports Fall in October

Crude enters Sunday with little changed on the quotes retrieved this morning. Brent (Jan '27) stands at $98.32 per barrel, up 0.03% or $0.03, while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is $1,364.50 per tonne, up 0.55% or $7.50. The weekend leaves the market with the same shape as Friday: a sharp political sell-off earlier in the week, a partial rebound, and no physical improvement underneath. WTI last settled at $90.52 on Thursday after a 2.7% gain, and Brent remains about 17% below the late-October peak near $118, according to CNBC's earlier reporting.

The shipping picture is the dominant story. Al Jazeera reported that Iranian forces say at least five oil tankers and a tugboat have caught fire in several incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, with the IRGC claiming a large tanker struck a mine south of the strait and the army saying three more hit mines. Oilprice.com reported the IRGC saying four tankers were destroyed in an area Iran has declared off-limits, and by November 7 Iranian media announced a fire on a second tanker east of the strait. These are largely Iranian claims and independent confirmation is thin, but UKMTO says most ships now refuse to report attacks, so the true count is likely higher.

The flow data show the strain. Vortexa figures cited by Reuters put Middle East crude, condensate and fuel exports at 17.1 million bpd in October, down from 18.9 million in September, and Kpler's provisional crude-and-condensate estimate fell to 16.1 million from 17.09 million. Vortexa's George Morris said exports have "pulled back slightly from recent post-war highs." Add Pentagon officials telling the New York Times that thousands of US troops are ready to resume major combat on Trump's orders, against Qatari mediation and Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei's insistence on no "submission," and the two-sided risk is clear. For diesel, thin distillate stocks make gasoil the more sensitive instrument.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Hormuz / Shipping

Iran Says Tankers Struck in Hormuz Amid Fears of a New US Attack

Al Jazeera reported that Iranian forces say at least five oil tankers and a tugboat have caught fire in several incidents in the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC said a large tanker hit a mine south of the strait, the army said three more tankers struck mines, and a later statement reported two more around noon. Separately, UKMTO said a tanker was hit by two projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash-Shamal, Qatar, with no group claiming responsibility. UKMTO added that most ships now refuse to report attacks. The strait normally carries about one-fifth of global oil consumption. For crude and diesel, each new incident raises war-risk insurance and rerouting costs, and keeps a physical premium under prices even when diplomacy dominates headlines.

02
Supply Data

Four Tankers Destroyed by Mines, IRGC Claims, as Middle East Exports Slip to 17.1M bpd

Oilprice.com's Tsvetana Paraskova reported that the IRGC said four oil tankers were destroyed after hitting naval mines south of the strait, an area Iran has declared off-limits, and that two other vessels were seized. Citing Vortexa via Reuters, Middle East crude, condensate and fuel exports fell to 17.1 million bpd in October from 18.9 million in September, while Kpler's provisional crude and condensate estimate dropped to 16.1 million bpd from 17.09 million. Analysts increasingly rely on satellite imagery because vessels switch off transponders. The decline suggests flows had recovered from spring lows but security is deteriorating, a trend that supports Brent near $98 and tightens product markets, particularly diesel.

03
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds December Targets for a Third Month; Full Group to Set 2027 Quotas on November 29

investingLive reported that seven core OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — kept December output targets unchanged at a Sunday videoconference, the third straight month on hold, after finishing the unwind of 1.65 million bpd of 2023 voluntary cuts in September. About 2 million bpd of 2022 cuts remain until the end of 2026. Most producers are pumping below quota because of Hormuz restrictions. The full 21-member group meets November 29 to set 2027 quotas after a Q4 capacity review, and one analyst called Sunday's decision the easy one. The IEA and others warn of a possible 2027 surplus if Gulf flows normalize, which caps the long-dated outlook.

Brent Rebounds 3.8% to $98.29 After Fourth-Day Slide Ends; WTI Settles at $90.52

CNBC reported that Brent rose 3.8% on Thursday to settle at $98.29 and WTI gained 2.7% to $90.52, after both benchmarks fell more than 8% on Wednesday to their lowest settlements since August 31, ending a four-day losing streak. Citi warned that with the midterms over, the White House may lean on executive foreign-policy powers, raising escalation risk. The sell-off reflected political repricing, with Democrats winning Congress, rather than a physical improvement. That is why rebounds can be sharp when shipping headlines return, and it frames the $93 area as the key support for Monday's open.

Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Fall to 409.8M Barrels Against Expectations of a Build; Distillates Draw 3.6M

The latest EIA weekly report showed US commercial crude inventories declining by roughly 0.9 million barrels to 409.8 million, against market expectations of an increase of about 877,000 barrels. Distillate stocks fell 3.6 million barrels, and one report described them as the lowest since 2003, while Gulf News said demand reached a one-year high and distillates sit about 13% below the five-year average. This thin cover explains why Low Sulphur Gasoil has been rising faster than Brent: any Gulf or Russian disruption lands on a market with little buffer. The next EIA report, covering the week to November 6, may be delayed by the Veterans Day holiday on November 11.

Qatar Mediates as Pentagon Readies Forces; Khamenei Rejects "Submission"

Al Jazeera reported that Qatar is mediating between Washington and Tehran and both sides are reviewing proposals, while unnamed Pentagon officials told the New York Times that thousands of US forces are ready to resume major combat operations on Trump's orders. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said there should be no "even the slightest inclination towards submission," and IRGC Ground Force commander Mohammad Karami warned that continued hostile actions would bring more Iranian "victories." Eurasia Group, per CNBC, puts deal odds at 10% and renewed US strikes at 60%, with Brent at $110–$125 on escalation. The market therefore carries a fat upside tail even as talks continue, which favors owning protection in diesel.

The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, November 8, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I wrote that the tape had stopped falling but nothing physical had improved. Nothing in the weekend flow changes that. Brent at $98.32, up 0.03%, is a Sunday print with thin conviction, and I would not read it as a verdict. The pivot remains $100, and the weekend headlines tilt toward the upside because they are about ships and barrels rather than politics.

My read on today: The political sell-off earlier in the week priced peace, but the physical data point the other way. Middle East exports fell to 17.1 million bpd in October from 18.9 million, Iran says five tankers and a tugboat have burned, and UKMTO admits attacks are going under-reported. Eurasia Group's 10% deal odds make Wednesday's low near $93 look like a market that priced too much settlement.

The diesel angle: Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,364.50 per tonne gained 0.55% while Brent was flat, extending diesel's outperformance. US distillates fell 3.6 million barrels to levels last seen two decades ago, and Ukrainian drones continue to threaten Russian refineries. Heading into winter, diesel has the sharper upside tail and the thinner buffer, so gasoil strength is the cleaner expression of this market's risk than crude.

My call: Brent trades $94–$103 into Monday's open, with $100 as the pivot and a mild upward bias on shipping risk. Natural gas remains a sideshow, last sourced near $2.98. OPEC+ is on hold until November 29, so the next move belongs to Hormuz. I would fade a drop below $93 absent a verified US–Iran meeting.
