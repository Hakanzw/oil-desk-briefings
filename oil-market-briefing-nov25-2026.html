⚡ Wednesday — Brent Eases to $87.47 as Traders Position Ahead of the OPEC+ Ministerial and Iran Tightens Its Grip on Hormuz

Brent Slips 1.48% to $87.47/bbl While Gasoil Firms 0.41% to $1,106.25/mt as OPEC+ Decision and Hormuz Tanker Attacks Collide with Surplus Forecasts

Brent (Feb '27) is quoted at $87.47/bbl, ▼ −1.48% (−$1.31), while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is at $1,106.25/mt, ▲ +0.41% (+$4.50). The divergence between the two is the first thing to notice: crude is giving back ground while diesel feedstock edges higher. Reuters-based reports carried by Business Today said Brent fell 43 cents, or 0.5%, to $90.97 early Wednesday on the January contract, with WTI down 47 cents to $81.58, after Tuesday's rebound. Rigzone reported that on Tuesday Brent for January settled up 2.3% at $91.40 and WTI up 2.4% at $82.05, in thin volumes ahead of the US Thanksgiving holiday, which exaggerated the moves. Contract roll and timing explain the gap to the Feb '27 quote above.

The driver is the interplay of a loud security story and a quiet surplus story. Iran said it will tighten control of the Strait of Hormuz until its demands are met — lifting the US naval blockade of its ports, easing sanctions and releasing frozen assets — and Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaee said Tehran is "firmly determined" to defy the blockade. More vessels were reported hit near the strait. Against that, the IEA says Persian Gulf crude exports have rebounded to 74% of pre-war levels and expects the market to swing into surplus this quarter, Goldman Sachs has flagged a glut risk next year, and a Dubai timespread flipped into a bearish structure this week. Rigzone noted the 3-2-1 crack spread is at its weakest since March.

The near-term catalyst is the OPEC+ ministerial on Sunday, November 29, which sets 2027 output policy. Iraq has rejected the OPEC+-commissioned assessment of its production capacity, setting up a potential clash over baselines. For diesel, the message is nuanced: broad refining margins are soft, yet gasoil is holding near $1,106/mt, and EIA data show US distillate stocks 7% below the five-year average. Traders should treat Wednesday's EIA report, due today, as a test of whether the glut narrative extends to products, and treat the thin pre-holiday tape as a reason for caution on any single-day move.

01
Market Reaction

Oil Dips After Rally as Traders Look Ahead to OPEC+ Output Decision

Business Today, carrying Reuters copy, reported that Brent crude futures fell 43 cents, or 0.5%, to $90.97 a barrel by 01:37 GMT on Wednesday, while US West Texas Intermediate fell 47 cents, or 0.6%, to $81.58. The pullback follows Tuesday's rebound, when Rigzone reported Brent for January settled up 2.3% at $91.40 and WTI up 2.4% at $82.05 in thin pre-Thanksgiving trade. WTI has still fallen by more than a fifth this month, though global benchmarks remain more than 40% higher this year. Different data feeds report different contract months, so treat single-day figures cautiously. For crude and diesel, the market is waiting on OPEC+ before committing to a direction.

02
Hormuz / Iran

Iran Vows to Tighten Hormuz Control Until Demands Met as More Vessels Are Hit

Rigzone reported that further tanker attacks overshadowed diplomacy on Tuesday, with more vessels hit in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran says it will tighten control of the waterway until its demands are met: Tehran wants the US naval blockade of its ports lifted, sanctions eased and frozen assets released before fully reopening the strait. Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaee said Iran is "firmly determined" to defy the blockade. US Vice President JD Vance met Qatar's prime minister, and NBC reported officials are weighing whether to target Iran's top leaders. Trump has held off on renewed strikes and reportedly pushed the deadline until after the New Year. The market reads this as a stalemate with a fat upside tail.

03
Supply / IEA

IEA Sees Surplus This Quarter as Gulf Exports Recover to 74% of Pre-War Levels

Persian Gulf crude exports have rebounded to 74% of pre-war levels, according to the International Energy Agency, which predicts the market will swing to a surplus this quarter. Goldman Sachs has flagged the risk of a glut next year, and a Dubai timespread flipped into a bearish structure this week, a sign that prompt supply is running ahead of demand. Onyx Capital Group's Will Cunliffe said the market remains risk off, with time-spread structure signaling oversupply, while TD Securities' Ryan McKay called the status quo unsustainable for the medium to long term. Rigzone noted observed inventories rose last month but mostly in barrels at sea, while onshore stockpiles remain at their lowest in a decade. That keeps the flat-price downside real yet fragile.

04
OPEC+

Iraq Rejects OPEC+ Capacity Assessment, Setting Up Clash at Sunday's Ministerial

Iraq has rejected an OPEC+-commissioned assessment of its oil production capacity, raising the risk of a clash at the group's key meeting on November 29, where ministers are due to set 2027 output policy and fix how much members can pump. Capacity baselines determine quota allocations, so a dispute with one of the group's largest producers could complicate consensus or delay the framework. Reports indicate the market is already bracing for the group to hold off on further increases. A cautious outcome would put a floor under Brent, while a messy or loosening outcome would feed the surplus narrative and push prices toward the high $70s that JPMorgan has forecast for the first half of 2027.

05
Refined Products

Crack Spreads at Weakest Since March, but Distillate Stocks Stay Tight

Rigzone said the 3-2-1 crack spread, which approximates the margin from turning crude into gasoline and diesel, was at its weakest since March, a sign that product demand is not keeping pace with crude availability. Yet EIA data for the week to November 13 showed US distillate stocks rose 1.6 million barrels but remain 7% below the five-year average, while gasoline rose 5.2 million barrels to 3% above average, and refiners ran at 95.7% of capacity. Gasoil's slight gain to $1,106.25/mt this morning while Brent eased suggests Europe's diesel market is firmer than the broader complex. For diesel buyers, tight onshore distillate cover argues against complacency heading into winter.

Why Prices Fall

Why Are Oil Prices Falling Despite Fresh Attacks in the Strait of Hormuz?

Invezz asked the question the whole market is wrestling with: why does crude keep sliding when ships are being attacked near the world's most important chokepoint? The answer from analysts is that surplus forecasts, recovering Gulf flows, fading odds of a fresh US strike and a risk-off tone across assets have outweighed the incident-by-incident headlines. Arab News earlier reported Brent near three-month lows as rising supply offset the Hormuz threat. The practical takeaway for traders is that the market now demands confirmed loss of barrels, not just threats, before adding premium, which makes sharp reversals more likely when verified disruption does appear.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, November 25, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I wrote that surplus was the story and Hormuz the tail. Today's tape, with Brent at $87.47, down 1.48%, is consistent with that: a modest pullback after Tuesday's pre-holiday bounce rather than a fresh leg lower. The market is neither buying the attack headlines nor chasing the surplus calls, which is what an undecided tape ahead of an OPEC+ meeting looks like.

My read on today: Thin liquidity ahead of Thanksgiving makes every move look bigger than it is. Tuesday's 2.3% rebound and today's dip are two sides of the same noise. The signal is in the structure: a bearish Dubai timespread, the weakest 3-2-1 crack since March and an IEA surplus call all say the physical market is loosening, even as Iran vows to tighten its grip on Hormuz.

The bigger picture: Diesel remains the relative strength. Gasoil edged up 0.41% to $1,106.25/mt while Brent fell, and US distillate stocks are still 7% below their five-year average. If crude keeps drifting while gasoil holds, the diesel crack widens, which helps refiners but is unwelcome for freight and heating costs this winter. Onshore inventories at decade lows also mean any confirmed disruption hits harder.

My call: Brent trades $83–$91 today, with a flat-to-lower bias into Sunday's OPEC+ ministerial and a fat upside tail on Hormuz. Surplus sets the drift; Iran and OPEC+ set the jumps. Keep sizing modest into a holiday-thinned market.
