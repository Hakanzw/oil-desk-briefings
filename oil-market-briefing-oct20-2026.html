Top Story

⚡ Policy Relief Fades, Physical Risk Persists — Brent Holds Above $109

Brent Steadies Near $109.9 as Houthi Strikes on Saudi Energy Sites and Hormuz Ship Attacks Outweigh the Russia Sanctions Waiver — Diesel Futures Rebound 1.4%

Crude held its ground on Tuesday after a choppy Monday. Trading Economics put Brent near $109.87, up about 0.3%, after Reuters reported Monday's settlement at $109.55, down 31 cents, having recovered from a session low more than $3 lower. That recovery matters: the first reaction to President Trump's Russia sanctions waiver was a sharp dip, yet buyers returned as Houthi militants claimed strikes on an Aramco facility at Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield and Riyadh's King Khalid airport. WTI settled at $103.66, up 2.2% and its highest close since September 15, in thin trade ahead of the November contract's expiry today. The benchmark is up more than 75% year to date, and European diesel futures have more than doubled.

The diesel complex told a sharper story. Investing.com data show ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,469.63 per tonne, up 1.42% from Monday's $1,449.00, reversing the waiver-driven slump of last week's close near $1,413. The Russian diesel arrangement is facing resistance: US lawmakers have introduced a bill to block it, and some experts say any Russian release would offer limited or no price relief given damaged Russian refining and a small initial volume against global demand near 29 million barrels per day. Dated Brent, meanwhile, traded near $117 Monday, though almost $16 below a week earlier, showing the physical premium is easing from extremes.

Mixed signals define the physical picture. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Persian Gulf flows have recovered to about 80% of pre-conflict levels, and Iraq has exported over 3.4 million bpd this month, near pre-war levels. Yet Kpler's seven-day average showed Gulf crude flows near 8.5 mb/d against more than 20 mb/d the week before, and UKMTO counted at least ten vessels attacked in or near Hormuz in a week, the most since the war began in February. Technical positioning adds fragility: Kpler says trend-following advisers are at maximum long in Brent and WTI and the 9-day RSI is overbought, leaving the market vulnerable to a sharp pullback on any de-escalation headline.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Saudi / Houthis

Houthis Claim Strikes on Aramco's Yanbu, Shaybah Oilfield and Riyadh Airport

Iran-aligned Houthi forces claimed attacks on an Aramco facility in Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield, Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and Abha airport, according to Reuters-sourced reports carried by Nation Thailand. Saudi authorities had not immediately confirmed the claims, and they could not be independently verified. CBS News reports Houthi attacks have killed about 20 people in recent weeks, including 13 in a strike on the Riyadh airport, and that the group has blockaded Bab el-Mandeb to Saudi-flagged tankers. Yanbu is a key Red Sea export hub that has become more important as Hormuz is constrained, so even claimed hits carry a risk premium for crude and diesel flows.

02
Hormuz Shipping

UKMTO: 10+ Vessels Attacked Near Hormuz in a Week; Supertanker Hits Mine

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said at least ten vessels were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz over the past week, the highest weekly count since the war began in February, with 23 attacks since the start of October, nearly double the average. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels hit a naval mine on October 17 and caught fire, while Iran's navy reported another tanker ablaze in the Gulf of Oman. For markets, insurance costs and voluntary transit reductions matter as much as physical blockage, and they explain why flow estimates diverge so widely between official and tracker data.

03
Diplomacy

Araghchi: Hormuz Would Reopen on Day Six of Seven-Day Plan if Washington Complies

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran's conditions still stand: under a seven-day plan shared via Qatar, the strait would reopen on the sixth day and negotiations would begin on the seventh if the US complies, including lifting its blockade of Iranian ports. Iran's army spokesman separately said the strait would reopen if US forces leave the region, and new defense minister Mehrdad Akhlaghi warned of enormous damage if Washington resumes its campaign. CBS also reported contingency plans for heavy bombs on Iranian sites, though Trump has said no attack before the midterms. The talks offer the main bearish tail for crude, but the conditions remain unresolved.

04
Russia / Diesel

Russia Waiver Meets Resistance: US Lawmakers Move to Block Diesel Deal as Experts Doubt Relief

Trump's executive order waiving certain sanctions and tariffs on Russian energy is intended to lower fuel prices before the November 3 midterms, but implementation is uncertain. Reuters reported US lawmakers have introduced a bill to block the proposed diesel export arrangement, and some experts say any Russian release would offer limited to no relief. Ukraine and Russia also resumed attacks on each other's energy infrastructure, per Trading Economics. The administration is also letting sanctioned tankers ship crude legitimately. With gasoil rebounding 1.4% to about $1,470 per tonne, the market is signalling that the waiver cannot fix a distillate shortage on its own, particularly with US stocks near record-low seasonal levels.

05
Inventories / OPEC+

EIA: US Crude Stocks Fell 8.6M Barrels, Distillates 2.5M; OPEC+ Holds Targets Until Nov 1

For the week ending October 9, EIA data showed commercial crude inventories down about 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million, gasoline up 3.5 million to roughly 208 million, and distillates down 2.5 million to about 102.6 million, some 12% below average. Refinery utilisation was 93.5%. The next weekly report is due Wednesday for the week ending October 16. OPEC+ core members left November targets unchanged at about 31.01 million bpd and meet again November 1; Gulf producers are pumping below quota because of export disruption. Thin distillate cover keeps diesel cracks wide and magnifies the effect of any transatlantic supply news.

06
Natural Gas

Henry Hub Slips to About $3.02, a Two-Week-Plus Low, on Mild Weather and El Niño Outlook

US natural gas fell roughly 1.2% to 1.4% to about $3.02 per MMBtu on Tuesday, according to Trading Economics, extending Monday's drop to $3.08 and reaching its lowest in more than two weeks. Forecasts for mild temperatures through late October are limiting heating and cooling demand, while El Niño-related expectations weigh on the winter outlook. Rising LNG export flows and lower output offer some support and the storage surplus keeps narrowing. Gas is up about 6.4% over the month but down about 13% year on year, and remains largely decoupled from the Middle East oil risk premium for now.

The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, October 20, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent mildly lower on the Russia waiver with upside tail risk. The tape split the difference: Brent settled at $109.55, only 31 cents lower after a $3 intraday dip, and is nudging back toward $109.87 today. The waiver shaved the premium for an hour; Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure restored it. That is a market telling us policy headlines are secondary to physical risk.

My read on today: Diesel is leading again. Gasoil at roughly $1,469.63 a tonne, up 1.42%, has erased much of the waiver selloff, and with a bill in Congress to block the Russian arrangement and Russian refineries under fire, the relief looks smaller and slower than the headline suggested. US distillate stocks at 102.6 million barrels leave little room for error as winter nears.

The bigger picture: Official and tracker data conflict, with Secretary Wright citing Gulf flows near 80% of pre-war levels while Kpler's weekly average shows far less. Either way, attacks on ships are at war-time highs and Red Sea routes are now in play. Positioning is the counterweight: CTAs are maximum long and RSI is overbought, so a diplomatic breakthrough could produce a fast $5 to $8 drop. Natural gas at $3.02 stays a quiet sideshow.

My call: Brent trades $105–$114 today, with a slightly upward bias while Saudi and Hormuz risks dominate, but with violent downside if Washington engages Araghchi's seven-day plan. The premium is crowded and fragile, yet only reopened lanes can truly remove it.
