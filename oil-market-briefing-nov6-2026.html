Top Story

⚡ Hormuz Flare-Up — Tanker Fires and Mines Drag Brent Back Toward $100 After the Midterm Rout

Brent Climbs to $99.93 as Iran Reports Tanker Fires and a Mine Strike in Hormuz — Gasoil Rebounds 1.9% to $1,383

The post-midterm sell-off has stalled. Per the ICE quote retrieved this morning, Brent (Jan '27) trades at $99.93 per barrel, up 1.67% or $1.64, while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is $1,383.25 per tonne, up 1.93% or $26.25. The bounce follows Wednesday's near-9% collapse to a two-month low around $93, and Thursday's recovery: The National reported Brent up 2.47% at $96.96 and WTI up 2.02% at $89.90 as buyers returned. Both benchmarks remain roughly 14% lower on the week, but the slide has clearly lost momentum.

The catalyst is physical, not political. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in separate incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, with one tanker striking a naval mine while moving south and another igniting as it left the eastern strait; the tug that approached to help also burned. The UKMTO separately reported a tanker hit by two unidentified projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, and Oilprice.com notes the IRGC said four tankers were destroyed by mines on Wednesday. Tehran says the ships used routes it has not authorized. Gulf News counts at least five vessel incidents since Sunday.

Analysts see a market exposed to binary outcomes. Ole Hansen of Saxo Bank said that with no US–Iran agreement, prices are "vulnerable to renewed volatility," noting that recovering Middle East crude exports have not been matched by refined-fuel supply. Eurasia Group cut its odds of a deal to 10% from 20% and of renewed US strikes to 60% from 65%, warning escalation could send Brent to $110–$125. UBS's Paul Donovan, by contrast, sees postwar fair value near $80–$85. The distance between those views explains why $100 is again the pivot for both crude and diesel.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Hormuz / Shipping

Iran Reports Two Tankers and a Tugboat on Fire in Hormuz as Mine and Projectile Incidents Mount

Oilprice.com reported that the IRGC said two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday. One tanker hit a naval mine heading south; a second caught fire leaving the eastern strait, and the tugboat that came to assist also ignited. The UKMTO reported a further tanker struck by two unidentified projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, with crew status unconfirmed. On Wednesday the IRGC said four tankers were destroyed by mines in a zone Iran has banned. Tehran argues the vessels used unauthorized routes. For crude and diesel the point is that flows have recovered from spring lows, but security is deteriorating, and insurance costs and rerouting keep a floor under physical prices.

02
Markets

Oil Rebounds From Two-Month Low as Uncertainty Lingers Over Iran War

The National reported that Brent rose 2.47% to $96.96 on Thursday and WTI gained 2.02% to $89.90, after both hit two-month lows on Wednesday when Brent briefly touched $93. Weekly losses were pared to about 14%. Ole Hansen of Saxo Bank said the absence of a peace agreement leaves markets exposed to binary geopolitical risk, while UBS's Paul Donovan argued oil has further to fall once fighting ends, estimating postwar fundamental value at roughly $80–$85. The Democratic sweep of Congress has added uncertainty over Trump's approach, and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi spoke separately with his Saudi and Qatari counterparts about diplomacy. The implication is that the downside case rests on a deal that has not materialised.

Analysis

Eurasia Group Cuts Deal Odds to 10% and Warns Escalation Could Send Brent to $110–$125

Gulf News, citing Eurasia Group, said the consultancy lowered its probability of a US–Iran deal to 10% from 20%, and trimmed the odds of renewed US strikes to 60% from 65%, leaving a large residual of continued stalemate. It warned that escalation could lift Brent to $110–$125 per barrel, against a late-October peak near $118. Gulf News adds that major Gulf shipping corridors are operating but not safe, with insurance costs sharply higher since the war began on February 28, and that Brent rose to $98.29 in Asian trading, up 3.88%. The market is therefore pricing a wide distribution rather than a central case, and tail risk to the upside is larger than the sell-off implied.

Inventories

EIA: US Crude Falls 0.9M Barrels to 409.8M; Distillates Draw 3.6M and Sit 13% Below the Five-Year Average

The latest EIA weekly report, for the week ended October 30, showed US commercial crude stocks down 900,000 barrels to 409.8 million, roughly 4% below the five-year average, while distillate inventories fell 3.6 million barrels to about 13% below average. Gulf News notes crude, gasoline and distillate stocks all declined as demand reached a one-year high. Oilprice.com's sidebar flags a steep decline in US crude inventories and further Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases. Thin distillate cover is the key reason gasoil can rebound faster than crude: any Gulf disruption hits a diesel market with little buffer, and pump diesel remains elevated. The next EIA report on November 11 tests whether the draws persist.

Physical Flows

Middle East Exports Slip to 17.1M bpd in October as Dubai Slides Into Contango and Murban Premium Vanishes

Citing Vortexa data reported by Reuters, Oilprice.com said Middle East exports of crude, condensate and fuels fell to 17.1 million bpd in October from 18.9 million bpd in September, a post-war peak, though Vortexa cautioned that the figure may be revised higher as shuttle shipments are tallied. Trackers increasingly rely on satellite imagery because vessels switch off transponders in the strait. Sidebar headlines add that the Dubai benchmark slipped into contango for the first time since July and that Rystad Energy believes Hormuz flows are unlikely to return to pre-war levels. Murban physical crude is quoted at $95.89, down 4.96%, so the roughly $7 premium over Brent seen yesterday has now disappeared, a sign the physical squeeze eased even as shipping risk rose.

06
OPEC+

OPEC+ Core Members Hold December Targets Near 31 Million bpd; 2027 Quotas Await the November 29 Meeting

The seven core OPEC+ members kept December production targets unchanged at roughly 31 million bpd, according to The National, with Saudi Arabia at 10.478 million bpd, Russia 9.949 million, Iraq 4.431 million and Kuwait 2.676 million. Most members are producing below target because of the war and the Hormuz disruption, so quotas are largely theoretical. The group finished unwinding 1.65 million bpd of 2023 voluntary cuts in September and keeps a further 2 million bpd of 2022 cuts through year-end, while reviewing member capacity to set 2027 quotas at the November 29 meeting. With Houthi missile attacks on Saudi Arabia continuing, the group has little ability to add barrels, and the debate between defending price and recapturing share only starts once Hormuz is secure.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, November 6, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that the war premium was deflating faster than barrels were returning and that I would not chase the sell-off below $90. That has held. Brent is back at $99.93, up 1.67%, only two sessions after printing a two-month low near $93. The midterm result shifted political odds, but it did not change the geography: mines and projectiles are still hitting tankers in the strait, and the screen is reacting accordingly.

My read on today: The market has swung from pricing a deal to pricing a stalemate. Eurasia Group's cut in deal odds to 10% is stark, and Saxo's Ole Hansen is right that binary risk leaves prices vulnerable. The counterweight is UBS's $80–$85 postwar value, which caps how much a settlement headline can add. Murban's premium to Brent vanishing suggests physical conditions are less acute than last week.

The diesel angle: Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,383.25 per tonne rose 1.93%, outpacing Brent's 1.67%, and distillate stocks remain 13% below the five-year average after a 3.6 million barrel draw. Refined supply has not kept pace with crude exports, so diesel retains the sharper upside if another tanker is hit. Heating-oil futures near $4.38 per gallon show no relief priced in for winter.

My call: Brent trades $95–$104 today, with $100 as the pivot and a mild upward bias into the weekend given the shipping incidents. Natural gas near $2.98 is simply drifting. The premium is rebuilding on the water, not in the diplomats' offices. I would fade moves above $104 without a confirmed casualty event.
