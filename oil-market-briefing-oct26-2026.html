⚡ Monday Brief — Brent Eases From Six-Month High as Qatari Mediators Reach Tehran, but Saudi Attacks Continue

Brent Slips About 2% to the Mid-$114s on Profit-Taking and Qatari Mediation, While Saudi Defences Keep Intercepting Houthi Missiles and Murban Surges Above $118

Crude gave back part of last week's rally on Monday, though the move looked more like consolidation than a change of regime. Per Oilprice.com figures carried by Gulf News, Brent traded near $114.60, down about $2.15, and WTI near $101.10, down 2.1%. Reuters reported early trading with Brent around $115.79 and U.S. crude near $102.28 as the market opened lower. Both benchmarks had settled almost $3 higher on Friday, with Brent at $116.72, its highest close since April 29, and finished the week up roughly 6%. A caution on the data: Brent's December contract has been rolling to January in some vendor feeds, and one aggregator showed a figure near $110, so we quote the cluster of consistent intraday readings rather than any single tick.

Two forces explain the pullback. GoodReturns, citing Reuters, notes that Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday working to de-escalate, lifting hopes that the feared US-Israeli strike on Iran might be avoided, and traders banked profits after the biggest weekly gain in a month. Kotak's analysts observed that the escalation markets feared did not materialise over the weekend. Yet the underlying threat has not eased: the Saudi-led coalition reported intercepting a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh plus drones and a cruise missile over the weekend, and Gulf News stressed that no hit on oil infrastructure has been independently confirmed, including at Yanbu, the Red Sea terminal of the 7-million-barrel-a-day East-West Pipeline that replaced Hormuz exports.

The most telling signal came from physical grades. UAE Murban, loaded at Fujairah outside the Strait of Hormuz, rose about 3% to above $118, roughly $3.40 over Brent, after gaining nearly 4% on Friday. Barrels that avoid Hormuz are scarce, and Hormuz transits were counted at only about four on October 18 against roughly 85 a day before the war. That scarcity is most acute in products: GoodReturns cites diesel trading more than $90 a barrel above Brent. We could not retrieve a Monday ULS1! quote, so the gasoil read is directional rather than numerical. With Trump saying he will soon decide between a deal and renewed military operations, the downside from here looks limited.

Market Reaction

Oil Slips Almost $1 in Early Trading From Near Six-Month Highs

Reuters reported that oil lost nearly $1 after Monday's open, with Brent around $115.79 and U.S. crude near $102.28, falling back from its highest in almost six months. By Asian midday, Oilprice.com data carried by Gulf News had Brent near $114.60 and WTI near $101.10, a drop of about 2.1% for WTI. Friday's settlements were nearly $3 higher, so the market is giving back roughly one session of gains. The dip came with no confirmed damage to Saudi export assets, which supports the profit-taking reading. For crude and diesel the point is that the weekly uptrend is intact: a retreat toward $110 would still leave Brent far above the EIA's $105 fourth-quarter average forecast reported by FinanceFeeds.

Diplomacy

Qatari Mediators in Tehran Spur De-escalation Hopes as Trump Weighs Deal Versus Strikes

GoodReturns, citing Reuters, said Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday seeking to calm tensions, which helped pull Brent below $115. Trump said he would soon decide whether to pursue a deal or resume military operations, while Iran and the Houthis have threatened Americans in the region and Iran could reportedly retaliate against Israel and the UAE. Kotak's analysts said the feared escalation did not happen over the weekend but the risk remains, putting Brent support at $109-$111 then $106, with resistance at $120. FinanceFeeds adds that Iran's president is due to meet Putin in Moscow this week and that the IAEA says it is technically ready to resume verification under a political settlement. A credible channel is the only development that could strip several dollars quickly.

Saudi / Houthis

Saudi Coalition Intercepts More Missiles and Drones; Oil Hits Remain Unconfirmed

FinanceFeeds, drawing on Reuters and others, reports that on Sunday the Saudi-led coalition intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, with explosions heard in the capital, plus a drone, a cruise missile aimed at Khamis Mushait and later ten ballistic missiles fired toward southern Saudi Arabia. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed strikes on Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery and the East-West Pipeline, but no Saudi or independent source has confirmed a hit on oil infrastructure. Two terminals at King Khalid airport have resumed operations. The key is Yanbu: in September a Houthi drone attack shut the pipeline and Aramco suspended loadings on September 15, the template traders use if damage is confirmed. Aramco loading notices on Monday are the tell.

Physical Market

Murban Jumps 3% to $118 as Barrels That Avoid Hormuz Command Record Premiums

Gulf News, citing Oilprice.com, reports UAE Murban rose about 3% to above $118 a barrel, roughly $3.40 over Brent, after gaining nearly 4% on Friday to $114.40, even as Brent and WTI slipped. Murban loads at Fujairah outside the Strait of Hormuz, and in mid-September it traded above $127 while Brent was above $107. The article also notes CENTCOM said U.S. forces had helped partners export more than 1.5 billion barrels since the summer. A Murban premium shows that the benchmark dip is financial while the physical market stays tight; it also matters for diesel, since Gulf-sourced middle distillates are the barrels most exposed to Hormuz risk, keeping product cracks elevated even when Brent consolidates.

05
EIA / Inventories

EIA: Crude Stocks Fall 3.1M Barrels, Distillates Rise 1.7M but Stay 9% Below Average

For the week ended October 16, EIA data reported by the BOE Report showed commercial crude inventories excluding the SPR fell about 3.1 million barrels to roughly 412.5 million, about 3% below the five-year average, against an expected draw of 820,000 barrels. Distillate stocks rose about 1.7 million barrels but remain roughly 9% under the five-year average, with output near 5.2 million bpd. Gasoline fell about 3.0 million barrels, Cushing rose 987,000 and refineries ran at about 93.4% of capacity. Total commercial petroleum stocks fell about 8.5 million barrels. The distillate build is modestly bearish for diesel, but with stocks still 9% short, it does little to relieve the tightness driving gasoil. The next report is due Wednesday.

Analysis / Outlook

ING: $80 Fourth-Quarter Brent View "Unattainable" as Aramco Orders Dhahran Evacuation, Libya Pipeline Shuts

Oilprice.com's Irina Slav reported on October 23 that Aramco ordered staff to leave its Dhahran headquarters, suggesting the Houthis may have longer-range capabilities than previously seen. The Guru's Verdict — Monday, October 26, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday's call was a Brent range of $112–$122 around the open with upside bias. Monday has so far delivered the lower half of that range: Brent near $114.60, down about 1.8%, after Friday's $116.72 settle. Profit-taking after a 6% weekly gain and Qatari mediation in Tehran explain the dip, while the Saudi coalition's interceptions continued and no oil-infrastructure hit has been independently confirmed. That is a consolidation, not a reversal.

My read on today: The pullback is financial, not physical. Murban above $118 and about $3.40 over Brent, Hormuz transits near four a day on October 18 against roughly 85 before the war, and diesel more than $90 over Brent all describe a physical market still short of barrels. I could not retrieve a Monday ULS1! quote, so I will not put a number on gasoil; the direction is firm.

The bigger picture: The forecasts show how little anyone knows. ING calls its $80 Q4 view unattainable, the EIA sits at $105, HSBC at $90 with a $120 stalemate case, and Goldman at $90 for December. The Fed on Wednesday adds a demand-side counterweight with a December hike near-certain among economists. Against that, the SPR is at its lowest since 1982, so Washington's ability to cap prices is limited, and the November 3 midterms mark the date the market fears for a return to strikes.

My call: Brent trades $110–$118 today, with a modest downward bias on diplomacy headlines but a hard floor from physical tightness. Confirmed damage at Yanbu or a suspension of Aramco loadings sends it through $120; a real diplomatic channel tests $108. Buy the dips cautiously, and respect two-way headline gaps.
