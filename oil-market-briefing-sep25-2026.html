⚡ Phased Diplomacy — US and Iran Explore Gradual Hormuz Reopening as Brent Holds Above $105

US and Iran Explore Phased Hormuz Deal in New York — Brent Eases to $105.68 After $106.60 Surge as Gasoil Crack Begins to Contract

US and Iranian negotiators advanced what may be the most structured diplomatic overture since the collapse of the June 17 memorandum of understanding — a phased path to reopening the Strait of Hormuz. According to sources close to the talks reported by Reuters on Thursday, officials meeting on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York are exploring an arrangement in which Tehran gradually allows shipping through the strait in exchange for a staged easing of Washington's naval blockade of Iranian ports. Qatar is mediating the sessions, building on its role from last week's three-hour Witkoff–Araghchi shuttle diplomacy. Brent crude surged to an intraday high of $106.60 per barrel on the news — its highest print since the five-day losing streak reversed September 23 — before easing back Friday to $105.68 (-0.86%) as Iran's signalling grew more ambiguous.

The critical friction point remains sequencing. Iran wants the US naval blockade lifted before allowing increased Hormuz traffic; Washington insists on verifiable strait access first. Neither side wants to be the first to surrender its primary leverage in a war now seven months old. Iranian official statements have oscillated — state media partially denied the deal's scope overnight, a pattern familiar from the March and June negotiating cycles, both of which ultimately collapsed into renewed hostilities. Traders are discounting the residual mistrust, but not ignoring it: the pullback from $106.60 to $105.68 captures precisely that tension. Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!), which rolled to the October front-month contract, moved lower as traders began pricing in some narrowing of the extreme product premium — the gasoil crack compressing from roughly $93/bbl to approximately $86/bbl.

Compounding the diplomatic uncertainty is a dual data signal from this week. The EIA's weekly report posted a surprise crude build of 2.97 million barrels for the week ending September 18 — sharply above market expectations for a draw of 0.6 mb — raising the first serious question about demand softening at triple-digit prices. At the same time, the IEA's September Oil Market Report documented that 507 mb of global inventories have been drained since February, averaging 2.8 mb/d across seven months, underscoring that whatever demand destruction is occurring has not yet offset the supply shock. The Saudi Petroline, restarted September 22 but still flowing at minimal throughput, has zeroed out October European allocations via Yanbu — meaning no physical relief yet for buyers awaiting Red Sea barrel alternatives. OPEC+ confirmed its Q4 production freeze at unchanged September levels.

EIA Inventory

EIA: US Crude Stocks Post Surprise 2.97 mb Build — Cushing Rises; First Bearish Data Point in Weeks

The EIA's Weekly Petroleum Status Report for the week ending September 18 showed US commercial crude inventories rose 2.97 million barrels to 426.4 million barrels — sharply above market expectations for a draw of approximately 0.6 mb. Stocks at the Cushing, Oklahoma delivery hub rose 2.27 million barrels to 23.7 million barrels. The build was driven by a combination of softening implied demand at $100+ prices and a modest uptick in import volumes. Distillate inventories remained well below seasonal averages, and the distillate deficit versus the five-year average continues to widen. Analysts noted the single-week data point does not reverse the structural tightness documented by the IEA — which logged global inventory draws of 507 mb cumulatively since February — but it is the first bearish demand signal in weeks and suggests extended triple-digit crude prices may be starting to curb consumption at the margin.

04
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds October Output Steady — Q4 Freeze Confirmed; Next Meeting October 4

OPEC+ confirmed at a virtual meeting September 6 that it would hold October oil production at September 2026 levels, completing the Q4 production freeze following the final 188,000 bpd voluntary-cut rollback in September. The seven core members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman — cited continued Middle East supply uncertainty and limited real spare capacity in justifying the hold. The group's next monitoring meeting is scheduled for October 4. While OPEC+'s official quota level now implies full voluntary-cut restoration, analysts note the group's effective spare capacity is largely theoretical: Saudi Arabia is at near-35-year production lows, Iranian output remains below 2 mb/d, and Gulf infrastructure damage limits any rapid output ramp. An analyst briefed on the meeting told Argus that 'OPEC+ currently has very limited power over the physical oil market' because it cannot guarantee barrels will reach markets amid geopolitical disruptions.

IEA September Report

IEA: 507 mb of Global Inventories Drained Since February — Demand Cut 2.5 mb/d for 2026; Recovery Deferred to 2027

The International Energy Agency's September 2026 Oil Market Report documented a further 95 million barrel plunge in global observed oil inventories in August, bringing the cumulative draw since the start of the US-Iran war in February to 507 million barrels — equivalent to a sustained drain of 2.8 mb/d over seven months. Non-OECD inventories drew by 52 mb, led by China, while OECD stocks rose by just 23 mb. The IEA cut its 2026 global oil demand forecast by 2.5 mb/d, citing high-price demand destruction concentrated in middle distillates and petrochemical feedstocks in Asia. Total oil supply is forecast to fall 5.7 mb/d to 100.7 mb/d in 2026. The Agency noted the continuing impasse in US-Iran negotiations delays normalisation of flows into 2027 at the earliest, while projecting a recovery of 2.6 mb/d in demand in 2027 if a settlement emerges.

Houthi / Saudi Supply

A Week of Houthi Strikes on Saudi Oil: Yanbu Now the Primary Target as Red Sea Route Remains Closed

The Houthis have maintained a relentless pace of strikes against Saudi oil infrastructure throughout September, forcing Riyadh to rely almost exclusively on the East-West Petroline for exports after Red Sea maritime blockades and direct attacks on Yanbu-bound shipping made the southern export route untenable. The campaign has evolved from maritime harassment to direct strikes on land-side energy facilities: attacks on September 8 wounded at least 73 people and damaged installations at Abha, Jizan, and Najran. The Houthis targeted a Saudi vessel near Yanbu as recently as August 24 — signaling the pipeline's northern outlet is now the primary threat focus. The Petroline's 12-day shutdown (September 10–22) following a drone attack demonstrated the fragility of Saudi Arabia's only remaining export corridor, and with October Yanbu allocations zeroed out, European buyers face another month of acute supply uncertainty.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, September 25, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

The phased Hormuz deal story is doing exactly what diplomatic signals do in war-premium markets: it introduced optionality without resolving it. Thursday's 3.4% surge to $106.60 on the Reuters report was the market buying the possibility of a framework. Friday's pullback to $105.68 is the market remembering that Iran still controls the strait, neither side wants to yield first, and the June 17 memorandum already collapsed once before. This is not skepticism about the phased approach per se — it is a rational discounting of a deal type that has a 0-for-2 track record in 2026.

My read on today: The phased approach is structurally more achievable than a grand settlement, and that makes it both more interesting and more dangerous to trade. A phased reopening could deliver partial barrel relief without the full premium unwind of a comprehensive deal. If Tehran allows, say, 30–40% of normal Hormuz traffic in exchange for an initial blockade easing, Brent could settle in the high $90s rather than the catastrophic unwind to the low $80s that a full deal would bring. The gasoil crack's compression from $93 to $86/bbl today captures the market trying to price exactly this partial outcome. The crack is contracting — but from a level so extreme that even $86/bbl represents a historically unparalleled product market stress.

The bigger picture: Three data points from this week form the structural mosaic. The EIA's surprise +2.97 mb crude build is the first flicker of demand destruction appearing in US stock levels. The IEA's 507 mb cumulative inventory draw since February explains why, despite that demand destruction, prices remain well above $100. And OPEC+'s Q4 freeze locks the group's output at a level that contributes almost nothing to physical supply given the Gulf disruption — their formal quotas are now a paper exercise. The market is simultaneously seeing demand cracks and supply deprivation, and the resulting price sits uneasily in the middle.

My call: Brent holds $103–$109 today and into next week, biased sideways as the phased deal narrative introduces genuine two-way risk. The upside scenario — talks collapse and Iran signals entrenchment — pushes us toward $112+. The downside scenario — a credible phased framework with a concrete Hormuz opening timeline — takes us toward $100 or below. The October 4 OPEC+ meeting and September 30 EIA report are the next scheduled price anchors. Watch whether Araghchi makes any statement before leaving New York — this market moves $2 on a quote.
