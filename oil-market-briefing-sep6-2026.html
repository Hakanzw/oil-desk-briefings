⚡ Sunday Analysis — Strike This Weekend? US-Israel Prepare Campaign on Iran Energy Infrastructure; OPEC+ JMMC Convenes

US and Israel Reportedly Preparing Strikes on Iranian Energy Infrastructure — Discussions Included Timing Before Monday's Market Open; OPEC+ JMMC Meets Against the Backdrop of the Sharpest Single Escalation of the War

The oil market enters Monday's open carrying the highest acute tail risk of the entire US-Iran conflict. CBS News and Daily Sabah reported Friday evening, citing people familiar with the matter, that the United States and Israel are preparing what sources described as "one of the harshest yet" campaigns against Iranian energy infrastructure — a campaign that could include power plants, refineries, and petroleum export terminals. Critically, discussions reportedly included explicit deliberations about timing the operations to conclude before financial markets open Monday, reflecting White House awareness of the market impact. President Trump had not given final authorization as of Friday's reporting, but the disclosure itself — that both governments are in active strike-planning mode with market timing as an explicit coordination variable — is unprecedented in the seven-month history of this conflict. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz set the rhetorical framework on September 4 when he explicitly threatened to return Iran to "the Stone Age and darkness" by striking civilian and military infrastructure including energy assets, warning Tehran not to take "desperate measures" as the Jewish New Year approaches September 11.

What a strike on Iran's energy infrastructure would mean for markets cannot be overstated. Kharg Island — which handles roughly 90% of Iran's crude exports — the Abadan refinery complex, or the South Pars gas installations represent targets that, if struck, would constitute the single largest supply disruption since the conflict began on February 28. Analysts surveyed by Bloomberg following Katz's September 4 statement estimated an instantaneous Brent spike of $15–25/bbl on a confirmed Kharg Island strike, with gasoil crack spreads potentially widening by $30–50/mt — from a market that already enters this weekend with distillate inventories 14% below the five-year seasonal average and the northern hemisphere heating season now underway. US retail diesel surpassed $5.85 per gallon on September 4, setting an all-time record. At Friday's $95.23 settle, the market is priced for the continuation of Hormuz disruption — not for the physical obliteration of the infrastructure behind Iran's remaining crude and product export capacity. The gap between those two scenarios is the defining trade of this weekend.

OPEC+ provided the day's other major structural catalyst: the Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee convened September 6 to review market conditions, with multiple delegate sources telling Reuters and CNBC the group was set to confirm a production freeze through Q4 2026 following September's final 188,000 bpd increase — the last step in the full phased rollback of the 2023 voluntary cuts. A clean Q4 freeze communiqué is already priced and would be directionally neutral. Two complications loom: Venezuela is in advanced discussions with US officials about a formal OPEC exit following the announcement of a historic US-Venezuela 100-year oil field lease deal, and any JMMC communiqué language that explicitly flags Israeli energy-infrastructure threats as a force-majeure scenario would itself add $3–5/bbl to the forward curve independently of the output headline. A parallel diplomatic track emerged this weekend: Russia and Ukraine declared a mutual three-day ceasefire beginning September 5, with US special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Moscow presenting a Trump peace framework and planning Sunday travel to Kyiv. A Ukraine settlement that eventually lifts Russian oil sanctions is the largest medium-term downside catalyst for crude — but at current negotiations pace, that scenario is weeks to months away at best.

Supply / Demand

US Diesel Hits All-Time Record $5.85/Gallon — Exceeds June 2022 Peak as Iran War Squeezes Global Distillates

US retail diesel prices hit a national average of $5.85 per gallon on September 4 — surpassing the June 2022 post-Ukraine-invasion record and setting a new all-time high. GasBuddy attributed the surge to the combination of Iran-war supply disruption, reduced global refining capacity, tight distillate inventories (14% below the five-year seasonal average per EIA), and elevated crude prices. Gasoline also hit a Labor Day record of $4.14 per gallon. EIA economists estimate the current diesel level is the equivalent of a $50 billion annualised household tax, with transportation and logistics sectors absorbing rising operating costs that are beginning to feed through to consumer prices across the supply chain.

OPEC / Venezuela

US Secures 100-Year Lease on 17 Venezuelan Oil Fields — Caracas in Active OPEC Exit Talks With Washington

The White House and Caracas released details of a historic US-Venezuela oil deal giving the United States a 100-year operating lease across 17 Venezuelan oil fields containing an estimated 65 billion barrels of reserves, with the Department of Defense Office of Strategic Capital holding a 35% stake. Venezuela is simultaneously in active discussions with US officials about a formal OPEC departure. Trump, when asked, said 'It's up to them.' Venezuela is already quota-exempt at ~1.16 million bpd output — roughly a third of its 1990s peak — so the immediate supply impact of an exit would be limited. But a second founding-member departure after the UAE's May exit would materially damage OPEC+ credibility at the worst possible moment for the cartel's market-setting authority.

Natural Gas / LNG

Freeport LNG Returns to Full Operations — Henry Hub Ticks to $2.98; US Feedgas Demand Up 6.4% Month-on-Month

Freeport LNG in Texas returned to full operations following maintenance that ran from July 10 through late August, restoring approximately 0.8 Bcf/d of feedgas demand to the Gulf Coast. Average flows to nine major US LNG export facilities rose to 18.3 Bcf/d in early September from 17.2 Bcf/d in August — a 6.4% month-on-month increase. Henry Hub prompt prices ticked up from the approximately $2.87/MMBtu August average to near $2.98/MMBtu in early September. Record US natural gas production at an estimated 109 Bcf/d continues to limit upside, but any further escalation of the Iran conflict constraining Hormuz-transiting LNG would quickly remove the current production-buffer ceiling on US gas prices.

The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, September 6, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Sunday's dominant question is the same one that has driven every session since September 4: will US and Israeli forces strike Iranian energy infrastructure before oil markets open Monday morning? Friday's CBS News and Daily Sabah reports — which explicitly cited discussions about timing strikes to conclude before markets open — transformed that question from speculative tail risk to active planning scenario. The market closed Friday at $95.23 without pricing that scenario anywhere near full. The gap between $95 and $110+ is not priced. It is the trade the weekend has handed the market, and it will resolve at Sunday midnight, one way or the other.

My read on today: Three scenarios frame Monday's open. Scenario one — no strike: markets open near Friday's $95.23 close, drift lower on JMMC Q4 freeze confirmation, test $93–$94 on partial tail-risk relief. Scenario two — strikes reported but limited scope (power infrastructure, not core export terminals): Brent opens $98–$103, gasoil cracks widen sharply, market re-prices the conflict's scope upgrade. Scenario three — confirmed strike on Kharg Island or Abadan refinery: Brent opens $110–$120, gasoil crack premium detonates from its already critical 14%-below-norm baseline, and ULS1! October prints prices not seen since the April war spike. The JMMC Q4 freeze outcome is directionally neutral and already priced. The JMMC communiqué language is the only OPEC-related variable with real information content today, and even that is secondary to the overnight strike question.

The structural picture: Whatever happens overnight, the physical market that greets any Monday outcome is the tightest it has been since early May. Distillates sit 14% below the five-year seasonal average with the northern hemisphere heating season underway. Hormuz transits average 13 per day against a pre-war baseline of ~100 per day. US diesel is at an all-time high of $5.85 per gallon. Freeport LNG returning to full operations eases the US domestic gas market at the margin — it does not address the global LNG disruption premium from Hormuz. The Brent-WTI spread of ~$4.11 reflects an Atlantic Basin that cannot replenish from Gulf sources at pre-war rates. And the Russia-Ukraine ceasefire track — though now active — is months away from translating into barrels even in the most optimistic scenario.

My call: Brent Monday open $93–$98 absent a strike confirmed overnight; $110–$120 if strikes on energy infrastructure are confirmed. The Friday close at $95.23 is the equilibrium in the no-strike scenario — a clean JMMC freeze confirmation pushes it mildly lower to $93–$94. Any confirmed US-Israel action against Iranian energy assets breaks $100 in the opening minutes. The most consequential oil market event since February 28 may occur while most traders sleep. Watch the wires closely.
