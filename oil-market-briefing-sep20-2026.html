⚡ Sunday Outlook — Two Chokepoints, One Summit: The Week That Could Reset Oil Markets

Houthis Bar Saudi Ships From Bab el-Mandeb on Eve of UNGA Summit — Oil Faces Its Most Consequential Week Since June as Both Gulf Choke Points Are Now in Play

The geography of this crisis just got more complicated. On Friday afternoon, Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree announced that Saudi-flagged vessels are now barred from transit through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — the strategic narrow connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden — following the faction's seizure of Perim Island on September 12. Houthi forces now control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, Perim Island, and the Hanish Islands, giving Ansar Allah operational dominance over both approaches to the strait. Commercial vessel transits through Bab el-Mandeb fell from 29–30 per day at the start of last week to just 12 on Thursday as cargo operators began rerouting via Cape of Good Hope. The knock-on implication is serious: the ship-to-ship transfer operation that has been Saudi Arabia's primary crude export workaround — moving barrels through the Strait of Hormuz, transferring off Sohar and Fujairah in the Gulf of Oman — now runs through a southern corridor that Iran-backed forces have effectively bracketed from both ends.

Saudi Arabia is not without options. Loadings at Ras Tanura and Juaymah — the kingdom's Gulf coast export complex — have risen sharply to approximately 4 million barrels per day combined, with Aramco conducting short-haul runs through Hormuz under US naval escort before transferring cargo to long-haul VLCCs staging off Oman. But that architecture has its own ceiling: a growing queue of very large crude carriers is forming in the Gulf of Oman as the transfer volume outpaces berth capacity off Sohar, and the southern routing of outbound cargoes toward Asia and Europe now adds weeks and tens of millions of dollars per voyage in costs routed via the Cape. For the market, the arithmetic is simple: the East-West pipeline is down for 3–5 weeks, the Bab el-Mandeb corridor is newly hostile to Saudi ships, and Hormuz remains under Iranian operational tension. There is no clean bypass left.

Against that backdrop, President Trump meets the Gulf Cooperation Council leaders in New York on Monday, September 21 at the UN General Assembly — the highest-stakes diplomatic event for oil markets since the June memorandum of understanding. Axios reported the meeting will focus on an Iran war "endgame" and a US-led "day-after" postwar reconstruction strategy expected to be finalised ahead of November midterms. The oil market is holding its breath and its range: Brent $103.59, WTI $101.21, both pinned in the same corridor they have traded since Wednesday's retreat from $108.75. The range resolves on Monday's communiqué. Both tails — a diplomatic breakthrough and a re-escalation signal — are live.

Chokepoint Escalation

Houthis Bar Saudi Ships From Bab el-Mandeb After Completing Perim Island Seizure

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree announced Friday that Saudi-flagged vessels are barred from transit through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait following Ansar Allah's completion of its campaign to seize Yemen's Red Sea coast and strategic islands. The faction captured Perim Island on September 12, one day after taking the port city of Mocha, giving it operational control over both the northern and southern approaches to the 29-kilometre-wide strait. Commercial maritime data showed daily transits dropping from roughly 30 vessels to 12 in the span of five days, with major shipping lines rerouting to the Cape of Good Hope. Bab el-Mandeb handles roughly 5–7% of global oil shipments; a sustained blockade of Saudi crude through the corridor eliminates the last geographically viable bypass route for Aramco's export operation.

Saudi Workaround

Saudi Aramco's Ship-to-Ship Operation Off Oman: 4 mb/d Loading, Growing VLCC Queue, and a Cape of Good Hope Premium

Saudi Aramco has sharply ramped loadings at its Ras Tanura and Juaymah export complex — the kingdom's Gulf coast hub — to approximately 4 million barrels per day combined, conducting short-haul runs through Hormuz under US naval escort before transferring cargoes to long-haul VLCCs staging off Sohar, Oman and Fujairah, UAE. Aramco has reportedly sold approximately 20 million barrels to Asian refiners available for September and October pickup from outside Hormuz. Maritime experts warn the operation has created an increasingly crowded queue of VLCCs in the Gulf of Oman, slowing transfer rates. Cargoes heading to Europe, now denied the Red Sea route, are adding up to four weeks and significant freight cost via the Cape — effectively applying an invisible supply-chain tax of roughly $3–5/bbl on delivered European crude.

Diplomacy

Trump–GCC Summit Monday at UNGA: Iran Endgame Talks and "Day-After" Strategy in New York

President Trump is expected to meet leaders from all six Gulf Cooperation Council member states — Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman — at the UN General Assembly sidelines in New York on Monday, September 21, Axios and Reuters reported. The summit is the first structured multilateral meeting since the June memorandum of understanding effectively collapsed in July. US officials describe the agenda as a "day-after" postwar reconstruction plan and Iran war endgame strategy expected to be finalised before November midterms. For oil markets, the GCC collectively represented roughly 20% of pre-crisis global supply; the degree to which this summit produces a credible roadmap toward Hormuz normalisation will set the directional tone for Brent for weeks.

Supply Picture

IEA September OMR: 10 mb/d Gulf Output Still Shut In — Global Supply Recovery Deferred to 2027

The International Energy Agency's September Oil Market Report offered its starkest assessment yet, cutting the 2026 global demand forecast by an additional 940,000 bpd to a full-year net decline of 2.5 mb/d and deferring the forecast for a meaningful normalisation of Gulf supply to 2027. Global oil production fell 1.6 mb/d month-on-month in August to 100.1 mb/d, with more than 10 mb/d of Gulf output still shut in amid heightened security risks. Total global oil supply is set to fall 5.7 mb/d this year, the agency noted, with the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve at its lowest level since 1982. The Saudi East-West pipeline outage — which the IEA estimates removed a further 4–5 mb/d of export capacity — is reflected in next month's projections.

Saudi-Houthi Front

Saudi Strikes in Yemen Intensify as Houthis Advance Along Red Sea Coast — Bab al-Mandab War Within the War

Parallel to the US-Iran diplomatic track, the Saudi-Houthi conflict has re-intensified. Saudi fighter jets conducted bombing campaigns along Yemen's Red Sea coast as of September 17, while Houthi forces launched drones and missiles at Saudi cities. US officials reportedly held quiet talks with Houthi representatives at the US Embassy in Muscat; Ansar Allah indicated it "would abide by the ceasefire agreement" with the US but maintained operations against Saudi Arabia. China's reported diplomatic intervention pressing Tehran to rein in Houthi Red Sea attacks is a potential moderating factor — but Tehran's response was non-committal.

EIA: U.S. Crude at 423.4 mb After Sixth Straight Weekly Draw — Next Report Tuesday Sept 23

The most recent Energy Information Administration weekly status report — for the week ending September 11 — showed U.S. commercial crude stocks declined 600,000 barrels to 423.4 million barrels, approximately 1% above the five-year seasonal average. The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, September 20, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Two chokepoints. One summit. The calculus going into this week could hardly be more concentrated. When this column warned last week that Brent's four-session retreat from $108.75 to $103.59 was pricing diplomatic hope, not progress, the picture was already complicated by one contested strait. It is now complicated by two. The Houthi Friday announcement barring Saudi-flagged vessels from Bab el-Mandeb is not a marginal development — it is the closure of the last viable southern bypass for an export architecture already strained by the East-West pipeline outage to the north and an active conflict zone in Hormuz to the east. Saudi Arabia has no uncontested route to market. It is running its exports through the eye of a needle, under naval escort, against a ticking clock.

My read for this week: The UNGA summit on Monday is simultaneously the most important event on the calendar and the most difficult to trade into with conviction. The setup favours a Brent range of $99–$109. A constructive communiqué — even vague, even conditional — permits the market to probe $100 and below on a continued deflation of the diplomatic premium. A re-escalation signal, a breakdown in talks, or a Houthi action against a Saudi-linked cargo in the Bab el-Mandeb approach triggers a straight re-rating toward $110+. What the meeting almost certainly cannot deliver is resolution: mine clearance, pipeline repair, and field restart are physical problems that run on weeks-to-months timelines, not diplomatic calendars.

The structural case has not changed: the IEA says 10 mb/d of Gulf output is shut in, global supply is falling 5.7 mb/d this year, and the distillate crack is running at five times its pre-crisis level because the world is burning more middle distillates to substitute for curtailed natural gas and petrochemical feedstocks. The East-West pipeline will need three to five weeks even under the best-case scenario. The ship-to-ship workaround is capped at somewhere between 2 and 4 mb/d and is now operationally exposed to Houthi enforcement decisions in the Gulf of Oman and Red Sea approaches. None of that is in Monday's communiqué.

My call: Brent $99–$109 this week, resolved by Monday's UNGA tone. Bias is volatile and two-sided — the downside scenario (constructive signal, $99 test) and the upside scenario (re-escalation or Houthi cargo interdiction, $110+) are roughly equally weighted for the first time since the June MOU unravelled. Both chokepoints are live. Both tails are open. The summit is the hinge.
