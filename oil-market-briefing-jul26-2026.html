Top Story

⚠️ Ceasefire OVER — Talks Still Live: Markets Enter a Week of Maximum Uncertainty

Trump Declares US-Iran Ceasefire "OVER" as Pakistan-China Mediation Steps In — Brent Surrenders $100 and Drifts Into a Five-Day Calendar Gauntlet: FOMC, GDP, and the Next Diplomatic Wire

The 38-day US-Iran ceasefire that had nominally capped the hot phase of the Hormuz war expired Friday in acrimony. President Trump declared the framework "OVER" in a social media post, citing repeated Iranian violations of the Strait of Hormuz terms — specifically Tehran's insistence on the right to impose fees on commercial transits. In the same breath, he said the US had agreed to continue talks at Iran's request, and simultaneously Pakistan and China separately surfaced a proposal for a formal back-channel, a diplomatic signal that pulled Brent 3.9% lower from its intra-week high above $100 to a Friday close of $96.78.

The market is now navigating a contradiction that the tape cannot hold indefinitely: a ceasefire that is officially dead but diplomatically still breathing. Three supply corridors remain simultaneously impaired — the Strait of Hormuz, running at well below pre-war transit levels; the Red Sea, where the Houthi kinetic blockade has driven Saudi crude loadings down 36% and into a second week with no tanker company willing to resume northbound transits; and the Black Sea-CPC route, offline since July 19 after Ukrainian drone attacks put four tankers out of service in four days, suspending roughly 80% of Kazakhstan's oil exports. Any deterioration in any single corridor snaps Brent back above $100.

The week ahead concentrates maximum uncertainty into five sessions. The FOMC meets July 28–29 with markets pricing one-in-three odds of a rate hike driven by oil-led inflation — a decision that would reprice the energy demand outlook and complicate already-battered supply chains. US Q2 GDP prints July 30. The EIA weekly inventory report arrives July 29. Against that macro backdrop, the diplomatic clock is also ticking: if Pakistan-China mediation produces nothing concrete by mid-week, "OVER" starts to mean what it says — and the geopolitical premium that the Friday tape partially discounted would return in full.

Houthi Maritime Blockade of Saudi Arabia Enters Second Week — Saudi Crude Loadings Down 36%, No Tanker Has Resumed

The Houthi maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia entered its second week with no tanker company willing to risk northbound Red Sea transits through the Bab al-Mandab Strait. Saudi crude loadings via the Red Sea route have collapsed 36%, rerouting volumes around the Cape of Good Hope and adding 8–12 days of transit time. Brent surged above $100 midweek when the attacks on Saudi tankers Encelia and Layla confirmed the blockade was kinetic, not rhetorical. Trump has pledged to hold Iran responsible for further Houthi escalation — a statement that makes the Pakistan-China back-channel even more load-bearing.

Black Sea / Kazakhstan

CPC Terminal Offline Into Second Week — 80% of Kazakh Exports Suspended With No Restart Timeline

The Caspian Pipeline Consortium's Novorossiysk terminal has now been offline for seven days following Ukrainian drone attacks that put four tankers out of service in four consecutive days (July 19–22). With no tanker company willing to send vessels to the facility, Kazakhstan halted piped supplies and began cutting upstream production — removing approximately 1.5 mb/d from the market on a route that handles roughly 80% of the country's exports. European refiners dependent on KEBCO-grade crude are seeking replacement barrels from the Atlantic basin, pushing differentials sharply wider.

IEA: First Annual Oil Demand Decline Since 2020 — War-Driven Destruction Cuts 1 mb/d From 2026 Baseline

The IEA's July report projected a 1 mb/d year-on-year decline in global oil demand in 2026 — the first contraction since the height of the Covid-19 pandemic. The agency described the decline as "highly skewed" in product and regional terms, driven by the Hormuz closure cutting industrial and transport fuel demand across Asia and Europe. Crucially, the IEA's forecast assumed a ceasefire and a gradual Hormuz normalization — an assumption now in doubt following the ceasefire's formal collapse. If the corridor disruptions deepen, the demand-destruction arithmetic worsens further.

Ukraine Sustains Refinery Campaign — Omsk, Yaroslavl, Syzran and Saratov Hit in Two-Week Offensive

Ukraine's campaign against Russian oil-refining capacity has intensified through July, with confirmed strikes on refineries in Omsk (Russia's largest, located 2,500 km from the Ukrainian border), Yaroslavl, Saratov and Syzran — four of Russia's five largest facilities targeted in two weeks. Russian refinery output has fallen sharply, tightening Europe's diesel market at the start of winter planning season. The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, July 26, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Saturday's briefing called for $93–$100, biased sideways to lower on the Pakistan-China diplomatic signal, with a ceiling at $100 unless the weekend produced nothing. The Friday tape delivered exactly that arithmetic: Brent fell 3.9% from $100.57 to $96.78 on the same afternoon Trump formally declared the ceasefire OVER. The market held both facts simultaneously — "OVER" and "talks continue" — and priced them as net neutral. I don't think that equilibrium survives 48 hours of silence from Islamabad and Beijing.

My read heading into Monday: The diplomatic architecture has shifted from a bilateral US-Iran framework with US-backed enforcement to a third-party mediated process through Pakistan and China. That is a materially weaker structure. The original ceasefire collapsed because Iran violated the Hormuz tolling terms and the US had no enforcement mechanism it was willing to use. A Pakistan-China back-channel has even less enforcement capacity. If it produces no concrete deliverable by Wednesday, "OVER" starts to mean what it says — and the market's Friday risk-off move will reverse.

The bigger picture: We are now running three simultaneous supply-corridor disruptions for the first time in the history of this conflict. The Hormuz recovery is stalled at below pre-war levels. The Red Sea is kinetically blockaded. The Black Sea-CPC route is offline. The IEA's 1 mb/d demand-decline forecast was built on an assumption of Hormuz normalization that is now in serious doubt. Against that backdrop, the FOMC may be about to hike into an oil shock — a combination that argues for continued volatility at the top of the range rather than a clean directional break.

My call: Brent $92–$103 on Monday — a deliberately wide range because the uncertainty is genuine and both tails are live. Bias is asymmetrically to the upside: three impaired corridors, a defunct ceasefire, a Fed meeting that may signal persistent inflation, and no sign of Houthi retreat. If Pakistan-China produces a communique, $93–$95 opens. If it produces nothing — or any corridor deteriorates — $100 returns within the session. This is not a directional call. It is a volatility call. The range is the story.
