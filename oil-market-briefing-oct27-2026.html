⚡ Tuesday Brief — Saudi Defence Minister Flies to Tehran as Brent Drifts Below $115, but Houthi Claims and Egypt's Troop Vote Keep the Risk Premium Alive

Brent Eases Another 1% to About $114.70 as Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan Open Channels With Iran — While Egypt Authorises Troops to Defend the Kingdom and Houthis Claim Pipeline Strikes

Crude extended Monday's pullback on Tuesday, though the move again looks like consolidation beneath six-month highs rather than a change of regime. Per Oilprice.com, Brent traded near $114.70, down $1.14 or 0.98%, with WTI near $100.80, down the same percentage. Brent had surged above $117 last week, its highest since April, after attacks near key shipping lanes. HDFC Sky's market note attributes the dip to diplomacy: Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks with Iranian officials in Tehran on Monday, and Iranian state media said the four countries agreed to keep discussing peace, security and regional cooperation. Investors, however, remain cautious about whether any of this produces a lasting settlement.

The diplomatic detail is notable. Oilprice.com reports that Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman led a surprise delegation to Tehran, with Riyadh saying the visit aimed to de-escalate and support mediation by Qatar and Pakistan. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said indirect talks with Washington continue through both countries, adding that Tehran is ready to resume fighting but that diplomacy is still possible. Against that, the Houthis claimed Monday strikes on pumping stations along the East-West crude pipeline and on Riyadh's King Khalid airport; Saudi Arabia had confirmed no damage as of Monday morning, and the earlier Saturday claims on Rabigh and Yanbu also remain unconfirmed. Egypt's parliament separately approved sending forces to defend Saudi territory if Riyadh asks.

The physical market is the reason the dip is shallow. Murban at Fujairah, outside Hormuz, is around $117.70, still about $3 above Brent, and the Washington Institute for Near East Policy says October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping around Hormuz since tracking began in 2017. The East-West pipeline matters enormously: it is 740 miles long, was shut for two weeks after September 10 drone attacks, and was back to 5.8 million bpd on October 6 against about 7 million capacity, with Yanbu exporting roughly 3 million bpd in October per Kpler. We could not retrieve a Tuesday ULS1! quote, so the diesel read is directional: NYMEX heating oil at $4.865 a gallon is down about 1%, in line with crude.

Diplomacy

Saudi Defence Minister Leads Surprise Delegation to Tehran; Araghchi Says Talks With Washington Continue Indirectly

For crude, this is the first Saudi-Iranian channel of the war that is visible and public, which is why Brent slipped about 1%. But with no ceasefire text and Houthi attacks continuing, the market is paying only a modest discount for it.

02
Saudi / Houthis

Egypt's Parliament Backs Troops for Saudi Defence; Houthis Claim East-West Pipeline and Riyadh Airport Strikes

Egypt's parliament voted on Monday to let President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi send forces if Riyadh requests help against the Houthis, with Sisi saying support would be limited to defending Saudi territory and borders. The Houthi parliament speaker Yahya Al Raei warned Cairo that joining would pose a grave danger to its national security. The Houthis claimed attacks on East-West pipeline pumping stations and on King Khalid airport on Monday, and on Rabigh and an Aramco facility at Yanbu on Saturday; Saudi Arabia has confirmed no damage. The coalition said it destroyed two missile launchers in Sanaa and hit five storage tunnels. The Red Sea export route is the system's critical bypass, so any confirmed damage would push Brent and diesel sharply higher.

Diesel / Russia

Russian Refiners Lift Diesel Output Toward 180,000 t/day to Supply Trump Deal, but Skeptics Doubt It Moves the Market

Two industry sources told Reuters that Russian refiners raised October diesel output after Moscow issued export quotas under its supply agreement with the US. Output fell to about 160,000 tonnes a day in early October from 175,000 in September, then began rising toward 180,000. With domestic use near 140,000, at least 40,000 tonnes a day is available for export. Trump said on October 9 that Russia would ship more than 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 in November and 1 million after that, backed by a six-month Treasury waiver. The first cargo, about 40,000 tonnes, left Primorsk on October 23 for New York on November 7; US diesel averaged $6.32 a gallon on October 23. Standard Chartered warns Ukrainian drone strikes limit upside, so the relief for gasoil is likely marginal.

Outlook / IEA

IEA's Birol: Oil Is "Flirting With $120" and Prices Won't Return to Pre-War Levels in 2027

Speaking at Singapore International Energy Week on Monday, IEA chief Fatih Birol said crude, fuel and gas prices are unlikely to return to pre-war levels in 2027. He said that "oil prices are flirting with $120 a barrel, and the prices for products are even much higher," adding that near-$120 oil is having a major impact on trade deficits across many regions, especially in Asia. The IEA's October Oil Market Report expects the diesel and gasoil crunch to extend into mid-2027, and Morgan Stanley analysts say fuel markets will stay tight until at least then, citing heavy refinery maintenance deferred from 2026 into Q2 2027. For traders, the message is that the product curve, not just crude, carries the structural premium, which supports gasoil cracks even if Brent consolidates.

Demand / Asia

India's BPCL to Cut Russian Crude Purchases in November as Premiums Collapse and Freight Rises

BPCL's head of finance, Vetsa Ramakrishna Gupta, told analysts on Monday that November Russian purchases will be significantly reduced because of higher shipping costs, with Middle East grades now commercially more attractive. Russian crude supplied 37% of BPCL's July-September intake. Russian premiums to regional benchmarks have fallen to $1-$2 a barrel for December cargoes from as high as $13 for October. Reuters tracking data show India's September Middle East imports rose 32% to 2.8 million bpd, the highest since February, while Russian shipments fell 14% to 1.8 million bpd as China, having lost Iranian supply, competes for Russian barrels. This reshuffle shows high prices rationing demand at the margin, and shifts support toward Gulf grades such as Murban and Dubai-linked crude.

06
OPEC+ / Inventories

OPEC+ Holds Targets Ahead of Nov 1 Meeting; Latest EIA Data Showed Crude Draw and Distillates 9% Below Average

OPEC+ kept November output targets unchanged at its October 4 virtual meeting, with the seven participating countries due to meet again on November 1; analysts quoted by Modern Diplomacy said actual output remains below quota, with Gulf exports running at roughly 60%-80% of normal because of the Iran conflict. The war has also delayed the capacity review needed for 2027 quotas. On inventories, the latest EIA report we retrieved (week ended October 16, via BOE Report) showed commercial crude down about 3.1 million barrels to roughly 412.5 million, distillates up 1.7 million but about 9% below the five-year average, and refinery runs near 93.4%. This week's API and EIA prints (week ended October 23) were not yet available at time of writing; the EIA report is due Wednesday.

The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, October 27, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday's call was a Brent range of $110–$118 with a downward bias on diplomacy and a hard floor from physical tightness. Tuesday has so far delivered exactly that: Brent near $114.70, down about 1%, WTI near $100.80, with the move driven by a visible Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani channel to Tehran. It is a modest, orderly pullback beneath last week's $117 high.

My read on today: The diplomacy is real but thin. A Saudi defence minister in Tehran is a meaningful signal, yet Araghchi said Tehran is ready to resume fighting, the Houthis are still claiming strikes on the East-West pipeline, and Egypt has just authorised troops for Saudi defence. None of those claims is confirmed, which is why the market is trimming rather than liquidating. I could not retrieve a ULS1! quote, so I will not put a number on gasoil.

The bigger picture: Birol's warning that prices will not return to pre-war levels in 2027, and the IEA's view of a diesel crunch into mid-2027, frame this as a structural rather than a spike story. Russian diesel under the Trump deal is a marginal relief at best, with Standard Chartered flagging refinery damage, and BPCL's pivot away from Russian crude shows demand rationing at the margin. Fed day tomorrow adds a macro counterweight.

My call: Brent trades $111–$118 today, with a mild downward bias while the Tehran channel holds, but a floor from physical scarcity. Confirmed damage on the East-West pipeline sends it through $120; a real ceasefire framework tests $108. Respect two-way headline gaps and fade neither side aggressively.
