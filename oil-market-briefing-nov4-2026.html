Top Story

⚡ Second Down Day — Diplomacy Hopes Hold Crude Below $105 While Tehran Warns of "Maximum Deterrence"

Brent Slips to $102.86 as Qatari Mediation Meets Iranian Defiance — Oil Falls a Second Session After the Midterm-Eve Rout

Crude extended its retreat on Wednesday as traders weighed a fragile diplomatic channel against an ugly security picture. Per the ICE quote retrieved this morning, Brent (Jan '27) trades at $102.86 per barrel, down 0.71% or $0.74, while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is $1,416.25 per tonne, down 0.44% or $6.25. CNBC reports Brent settled Tuesday at $103.60, down $1.17, and WTI closed at $96.09, down 37 cents after dipping below $95 intraday. That is the second consecutive decline following Monday's sell-off of more than 7%, though crude still gained more than 10% across October.

The diplomatic backdrop is genuinely two-sided. A Qatari delegation met in Tehran, according to Qatar's foreign ministry, on a draft settlement that has been under negotiation for roughly eight days. President Trump said Tehran wants "to make a deal and that could happen," and Vice President JD Vance told CNN both men "feel really good" about a negotiated outcome, with no deadline set. Yet Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said Tehran "will never accept diplomacy based on coercion," and a senior Iranian official told Reuters the sides remain far apart and that Hormuz will not reopen until Tehran's conditions are met.

The security tape is deteriorating at the same time. Al Jazeera reports Iran says a "maximum deterrence" plan is ready for any new US attack and that its military is widening target lists across US and Israeli assets, while UKMTO reports a tanker struck by two unknown projectiles near the strait after the IRGC claimed three tankers hit mines. With Hormuz — normally a fifth of global crude and LNG — still effectively shut, today's dip is premium compression on talks hopes, not a physical fix. The EIA's weekly report, due today, is the next hard data point for a US market already drawing stocks.

01
Markets

Oil Falls for a Second Session as US Voters Head to the Polls

CNBC reported that Brent settled Tuesday at $103.60, down $1.17, and WTI at $96.09, down 37 cents, after trading below $95 earlier in the session. The decline follows Monday's drop of more than 7%, the sharpest in months, yet crude still rose more than 10% in October and global prices are up roughly 45% since the war began in late February. Markets had priced in escalation risk ahead of the US midterm elections, and two Federal Reserve presidents warned last week that rates must rise further to curb inflation, a headwind for demand. For traders the key point is that this is a diplomacy-driven pullback inside a still-elevated range, so it can reverse quickly on any hostile headline.

Diplomacy

Vance Says Trump "Feels Really Good" About a Settlement as US Strikes on Qeshm Island Are Called "Defensive"

Vice President JD Vance told CNN that he and Trump "both feel really good" about reaching a negotiated settlement, calling Iranians "unpredictable" while stressing the president retains "kinetic and non-kinetic" options and that no deadline is set. A US official described the reported strike on Qeshm Island, the largest island in the Persian Gulf, as a defensive measure against Iranian surface-to-air missile fire. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that "Iran will not submit" and that nuclear talks could begin only after Hormuz conditions are met. Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran Monday night on the draft. For crude, the gap between rhetoric and positions keeps a two-way risk premium in place.

Iran Readies "Maximum Deterrence" Plan as Tanker Is Struck Near Hormuz

OPEC+

OPEC+ Core Members Hold December Targets at About 31 Million bpd; 2027 Quotas Await Nov 29

The seven core OPEC+ members kept December production targets unchanged at roughly 31 million bpd, matching September through November, with Saudi Arabia at 10.478m bpd, Russia 9.949m, Iraq 4.431m and Kuwait 2.676m, according to The National. Most members are producing below targets because of the war and the Hormuz closure, so the quotas are largely theoretical. The group finished unwinding 1.65m bpd of 2023 voluntary cuts in September and keeps a further 2m bpd of 2022 cuts until year-end. It is reviewing member capacity to set 2027 quotas, with the next meeting on November 29. The practical message is that policy offers no supply relief while the strait is shut.

Inventories

EIA: US Crude and Distillate Stocks Fall, Gasoline Builds; New Report Due Today

In the latest published EIA data, for the week ended October 23, Reuters reported crude inventories fell 1.9 million barrels to 410.6 million, against expectations of a 15,000-barrel build, with Cushing stocks down 624,000 barrels. Distillates fell 1.5 million barrels to 102.8 million, a bigger draw than the 0.9 million expected, while gasoline rose 3.9 million barrels to 209.2 million and refinery utilization slipped 0.6 points to 92.8%. Kpler disputed the export figures, saying observed loadings were about 1 million bpd lower than the EIA reported. Today's report covers the week to October 30; another distillate draw would reinforce the tightness visible in the gasoil curve.

Corporate / Refining

Aramco Q3 Adjusted Profit Rises to $31.2 Billion as Downstream Earnings Jump 234%

Saudi Aramco reported third-quarter adjusted net income of $31.2 billion, up 9% from $28.6 billion a year earlier and above the $30.66 billion consensus. Downstream adjusted EBIT surged 234% to $10.3 billion on stronger refining margins, free cash flow reached $28.2 billion, and the base dividend is $21.9 billion. CEO Amin Nasser said "geopolitical uncertainty and attacks on key energy facilities continued to amplify the global energy supply crisis," and the company is exploring more overseas storage and alternative export routes. The takeaway for diesel is that refiners are earning exceptional margins because middle distillates are scarce, consistent with gasoil holding above $1,400 per tonne.

Diesel / Macro

Diesel Futures Still Up Nearly 110% This Year; US Pump Price at $6.29 as Ukraine Hits Russian Refineries

NBC News reports diesel futures fell 5.4% on Monday, their biggest one-day drop since May 6, but remain up nearly 110% year to date, with the average US diesel price at $6.29 per gallon, up 77% since January. Supply is squeezed by the Iran war and by renewed Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries. Bank of America's Francisco Blanch wrote in September that a broader conflict damaging energy infrastructure could spike Brent as high as $150, while continued disruption through year-end implies a $95 to $120 range. Economists worry higher freight costs spread quickly through the economy. Natural gas, by contrast, is soft at about $2.87 on warm weather and ample storage injections.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, November 4, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that the rebound after Monday's rout would be capped by headlines, and that is what the tape delivered: Brent settled at $103.60 and trades at $102.86 this morning, down 0.71%. The market is behaving like a patient that has been given a sedative called diplomacy. It is calm, but nothing about the underlying condition has changed, and Hormuz remains effectively shut.

My read on today: The two-way risk is real. On one side are Qatari mediators in Tehran, Vance's upbeat tone and Trump's repeated denials that strikes will resume. On the other are Baqaei's refusal of "diplomacy based on coercion," a senior official telling Reuters that Iran "will not submit," and a "maximum deterrence" plan paired with fresh tanker attacks. Prices that fall on hope can rise equally fast on a single hostile headline.

The diesel angle: Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,416.25 per tonne fell less than Brent in percentage terms, a sign that middle distillates are tighter than crude. US distillate stocks sit at 102.8 million barrels after a 1.5 million draw, diesel futures are up nearly 110% this year, and Aramco's downstream profit jumped 234%. Refiners are earning for a reason, and today's EIA print will show whether that tightness persists.

My call: Brent trades $98–$107 today, with a slight downside bias while mediation holds but sharp upside risk on any breakdown. This is a headline market sitting on a physical shortage. I would respect the support area near $100 until a verified meeting or an easing of the blockade changes the picture.
