⚡ Surplus Arrives — IEA Says Supply Now Exceeds Demand as Iran Stalemate Keeps Risk Premium Alive

Brent Steady at $95.84 and Gasoil Jumps 3.0% to $1,287.50/mt as the IEA Declares a Supply Surplus — but Hormuz Mines and Stalled Iran Talks Keep Diesel Tight

Crude closed a choppy week almost unchanged on the day, while diesel feedstock surged. Brent (Jan '27) is quoted at $95.84/bbl, ▲ +0.02% (+$0.02), and Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is ▲ +3.02% (+$37.75) at $1,287.50/mt. The divergence tells the story: the IEA's November report, as relayed by CNBC, says global supply reached 105.4 million bpd in October, up 2.3 million bpd from September and 5.3 million bpd since August, and that supply now exceeds demand for the first time since the Iran war began. Observed global inventories rose 52 million barrels in October, or 1.7 million bpd, the first build since June. Crude is on course for a second straight weekly loss, per Oilprice, with Brent still roughly 19% below its late-October high near $118.

Yet the physical diesel market refuses to follow crude lower. The IEA notes that persistent attacks on Russian energy infrastructure are trimming Russian diesel exports on top of Middle East losses, and refinery capacity remains constrained. That explains a 3.0% gasoil gain even as Brent barely moved. Washington has tried to ease the squeeze: after the October 9 Trump–Putin understanding, OFAC issued a general license covering certain transactions in Russian-origin diesel through April 7, 2027, and the Jones Act waiver for foreign-flagged tankers was extended another 90 days on Friday. Neither measure has yet unlocked enough middle-distillate supply to compress the diesel crack.

Geopolitics remains the wild card. Trump said this week, "We don't need a deal with Iran," while Tehran says it will not allow IAEA inspectors back and reports almost daily incidents in Hormuz, including a liquefied gas tanker it says hit a mine on Friday on an "unauthorised" route. Meanwhile Gulf barrels keep returning: Saudi Arabia told OPEC it pumped 10.563 million bpd in October, Iraq reached 4.6 million bpd, its highest since 2019, and Asia's Hormuz crude imports recovered to more than 7.4 million bpd. A surplus-building crude balance and a still-fragile diesel chain argue for a market where flat-price rallies fade, but product spreads stay elevated.

Supply / IEA

IEA: Global Oil Market Returns to Surplus as Russian Output Rises to 8.73M bpd

The IEA's latest monthly report, reported by CNBC, says Russia's crude output rose 210,000 bpd to 8.73 million bpd in October, helping lift global supply to 105.4 million bpd, up 2.3 million bpd on September. Supply now exceeds demand for the first time since the Iran war began, and observed inventories rose 52 million barrels, the first build since June. The agency raised its fourth-quarter supply estimate by 3.3 million bpd and forecasts a 6.6 million bpd supply increase in 2027, with the US, Canada, Brazil, Argentina and Guyana adding 1.6 million bpd this year. For crude the message is bearish and structural; for diesel it is less clear, because the IEA says attacks on Russian infrastructure are still cutting diesel exports.

02
OPEC+ / Gulf Output

Iraq Output Soars to 4.6M bpd, a 2019 High, as Saudi Reports 10.56M bpd

Citing the IEA's November report, Oilprice says Iraq produced 4.6 million bpd in October, the highest since 2019 and up 540,000 bpd from September, about 170,000 bpd above its OPEC+ quota. Kuwait rose from 1.58 million to 2.03 million bpd, still about 650,000 bpd below its ceiling. The IEA puts Saudi output near 9.99 million bpd, 480,000 bpd under quota, while Riyadh told OPEC it pumped 10.563 million bpd, slightly above its roughly 10.5 million quota; Saudi Arabia noted that part of the jump went into storage. Flows through Hormuz are recovering largely via shuttle tankers and ship-to-ship transfers. The risk for OPEC+ is quota compliance as barrels return, adding to the surplus narrative that weighs on Brent.

Hormuz / Security

Iran Says Liquefied Gas Tanker Hit Mine in Hormuz After Four Oil Tankers Struck Thursday

A tanker carrying liquefied gas hit a mine and exploded on Friday while crossing the Strait of Hormuz by a route Iran had not approved, according to the IRGC Navy, per Oilprice. On Thursday Iran's military said four oil tankers were hit by explosions after ignoring warnings; those claims are unverified. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf told Al Jazeera that strait navigation will not return to pre-war conditions. Kpler data show 31 LNG tankers transited the strait last month, up from 23 in September, and Qatar's Ras Laffan loadings exceeded 2.7 million tons in October versus nearly 6.2 million in February. Near-daily incidents keep war-risk premiums and freight elevated even as volumes recover.

Asia Demand

Asian Refiners Turn Back to Gulf Oil as Hormuz Imports Hit 7.4M bpd

Asia's crude imports through Hormuz jumped to more than 7.4 million bpd in October, from 1.6 million bpd in April, per Reuters tanker data cited by Oilprice, pushing total Asian imports back to pre-war February levels. Chinese, Japanese and Thai refiners are favoring Middle East cargoes as freight rates ease from record highs and Gulf producers cut official selling prices to win buyers. India's Hormuz imports are about 2.1 million bpd so far in November, above the 1.8 million bpd from Russia, per Kpler via Bloomberg. Middle Eastern benchmarks have weakened for weeks, and the article suggests a glut may be forming. That is bearish for crude and for Russia's pricing power, though ship-to-ship workarounds show logistics remain abnormal.

Diplomacy / Nuclear

Iran Rules Out Return of UN Inspectors as US War Talks Stall; IAEA Board Meets Next Week

Iran continues to block IAEA access to its enriched uranium stockpile and ruled out resuming regular monitoring "under the present circumstances," Oilprice reports. IAEA chief Rafael Grossi wrote in a restricted report seen by Bloomberg that "it is more than a year since the agency lost continuity of knowledge" and that the agency "is not able to conclude that there has been no diversion of declared nuclear material." Access ended after US and Israeli attacks in June 2025. The Iran file is on the IAEA Board of Governors agenda in Vienna next week. With Trump saying the US does not need a deal, the nuclear impasse lowers odds of a negotiated sanctions and blockade relief, keeping Iranian barrels off the market and tail risk alive.

Policy / Inventories

Trump Extends Jones Act Waiver 90 Days; EIA Showed 4.7M bbl Crude and 4.1M bbl Distillate Builds

Oilprice reports Trump extended the Jones Act waiver for another 90 days, allowing foreign-flagged tankers to keep moving oil between US ports, a measure aimed at easing domestic fuel logistics. The most recent EIA weekly report, released Thursday after a holiday delay, followed a build in US crude stocks of 4.7 million barrels to 414.4 million in the week ended November 6, versus a Reuters poll for a 1.1 million barrel draw, with distillates up 4.1 million barrels to 103.3 million, per Reuters via BOE Report. Together the waiver and the builds ease US-side tightness, but Friday's 3.0% gasoil gain shows the global diesel market is being set by Russian refining disruption and Middle East losses rather than US stocks.

The Guru's Verdict — Saturday, November 14, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Earlier this week I argued that crude was caught between a bearish US balance sheet and a stubbornly risky Gulf. Today's IEA data tilts that argument: supply of 105.4 million bpd, a 52 million barrel stock build, and a market that is in surplus for the first time since the war began. Brent at $95.84, flat on the day, is trading as if that surplus is not yet real, which is why I still see more downside risk than upside in the flat price over a multi-week horizon.

My read on today: A flat Brent and a 3.0% gasoil gain is the cleanest signal available that the crude and diesel stories have decoupled. Crude is being priced on supply returning from Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Kuwait and Asian refiners buying Hormuz barrels again. Diesel is being priced on Russian refinery attrition and lost Middle East product exports. Gasoil at $1,287.50/mt is paying for scarcity that the crude market no longer shows.

The bigger picture: Geopolitics keeps the floor intact. Trump says no deal is needed, Iran refuses inspectors, and tanker incidents continue almost daily. But the market has learned that unverified strikes fade within sessions. Only confirmed physical loss, such as a hit on loaded Saudi or Iraqi export capacity, would break that pattern. Meanwhile OPEC+ members exceeding quotas hints at limited discipline into a surplus.

My call: Brent holds $92–$99 into Monday, with a mild downward bias on the IEA surplus and a sharp upside tail on any confirmed Hormuz loss. For diesel, stay respectful of the squeeze: gasoil strength can persist even if crude drifts lower.
