Top Story

Tuesday Session — Iran-Oman Hormuz Deal Nears; Brent Consolidates at $97.62 as Market Eyes Tehran's Next Move

Brent Holds Above $97 on Iran-Oman Shipping Pact Alarm — Tehran's Growing Hormuz Control Fuels Risk Premium; Saudi Aramco Jizan Hit Again; ULS1! September Expires Thursday

Crude oil entered Tuesday's session consolidating the prior week's near-10% surge, with Brent trading at $97.62/bbl (+0.47%) and WTI at $93.12/bbl (+0.64%) — both at their highest levels in roughly three months. The bid beneath $97 is not a new shock but rather the accumulation of unresolved tail risk: the US struck three Iranian crude tankers over the weekend, Iran's parliament declared 'the era of proportionate responses has come to an end,' and now Tehran says it is days away from finalising a deal with Oman to manage shipping through the Strait of Hormuz — a development that markets are reading not as de-escalation but as Iran formalising its chokepoint authority. How the United States responds to Iran institutionalising its control over the Strait is the defining variable for this session.

The Strait itself continues to function at a fraction of pre-war capacity. The most recent tracking data shows an average of 10 commodity ships per day transiting the Strait — versus the pre-February 28 baseline of vessels carrying roughly 20 million barrels of oil and LNG daily. Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery was struck for the second time in a month over the weekend, according to a Financial Times report cited by CNBC, potentially pushing back the timeline on Saudi production recovery. Saudi Arabia has been partially rerouting exports via Fujairah but volumes remain severely constrained. US retail gasoline has reached $4.15 per gallon nationally, up 7 cents week-on-week and 39% since the conflict began, while diesel stands at a record $5.90 per gallon.

The broader macro backdrop is sombre. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell below 290 million barrels — its lowest level since 1982 — as the Biden-era drawdown was compounded by emergency releases throughout the crisis. EIA data for the week ending August 28 showed commercial crude inventories at 424.5 million barrels (−4.5 mb on the week, 1% above the five-year average) while distillates sat 14% below the five-year seasonal norm heading into the northern hemisphere heating season — a deficit that the Low Sulphur Gasoil October roll (ULS1! September contract expires Wednesday) will price directly into the forward curve. The next EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report is due Thursday September 10, delayed by one day from the normal Wednesday release due to Monday's Labor Day federal closure. That print — the first post-weekend-strike inventory read — is the week's critical data event.

Geopolitics / Hormuz

Iran-Oman Shipping Deal 'Imminent' — Tehran Moves to Formalise Hormuz Control; US Response Uncertain After Weekend Tanker Strikes

Iran stated Tuesday that an agreement with Oman to manage commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is 'nearing completion,' a development traders are interpreting not as diplomatic progress but as Tehran institutionalising its authority over the world's most critical oil chokepoint. The announcement follows the US striking three Iranian crude tankers on Saturday — two permanently disabled, one sunk — in retaliation for IRGC ballistic missile strikes on US Navy warships. Iran's IRGC struck three additional tankers and three US-linked vessels in retaliation. The combined effect: Hormuz traffic averaging 10 ships per day, versus the pre-war baseline of vessels carrying roughly 20 million barrels daily. How Washington responds to an Oman-brokered Iranian corridor-management deal is this session's primary unpriced risk.

Supply / Infrastructure

Saudi Aramco Jizan Refinery Hit Second Time in One Month — Production Recovery Delayed; Murban Crude Tops $106

Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery was struck for the second time in a month over the weekend, according to reporting by the Financial Times cited by CNBC, threatening to delay what Saudi Arabia had planned as a managed production recovery in Q4 2026. Saudi Arabia has more than doubled its shipping volume since June 17 relative to the prior three months combined, routing crude through Fujairah transfers and around the Strait, but the physical supply chain remains deeply constrained. Murban crude — the UAE's benchmark, also a partial Hormuz workaround indicator — reached $106.80/bbl (+3.36%) on Monday, reflecting the premium on Gulf crude that actually clears the chokepoint.

03
OPEC+ / Production

OPEC+ Q4 2026 Production Freeze Confirmed — September's 188,000 bpd Hike Completes Full Rollback; October Quotas Held Steady

OPEC+'s Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee confirmed Sunday that the group will hold production quotas flat for Q4 2026, following September's 188,000 barrels-per-day increase — the final step in unwinding the 3.5 million bpd of voluntary cuts first announced in 2023. The seven core producers (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman) agreed on August 2 to complete the rollback and then freeze. The September increase is moot in the physical market: actual production across OPEC+ members lags quotas significantly, as Hormuz disruptions prevent Gulf exports from reaching destination markets at quota-implied rates. The group's next review of 2027 production baselines — via DeGolyer and MacNaughton capacity assessments — is expected in late September.

EIA / Inventories

EIA: Crude Draw of 4.5 mb Week Ending Aug 28; Distillates 14% Below Seasonal Norm; Next Report Due Thursday Sep 10

The most recent EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (week ending August 28, released September 2) showed: commercial crude oil inventories fell 4.5 million barrels to 424.5 million barrels (1% above five-year average); gasoline inventories declined 1.2 million barrels, now 6% below the five-year average; distillate inventories rose 0.8 million barrels but remain 14% below the five-year seasonal average — a critical deficit entering the northern hemisphere heating season. Refinery runs stood at 17.5 million bpd at 98% utilisation. The next EIA report — covering the week ending September 5, the first to capture the post-tanker-strike period — is due Thursday, September 10 at noon Eastern (delayed one day from normal Wednesday release due to Labor Day).

Demand / Strategy

China Accelerates EV Transition as Oil Shock Deepens — Over 50% of New Chinese Vehicle Sales Now Electric; SPR Release Continues

China is responding to the sustained oil price shock by leveraging its strategic petroleum reserve and accelerating its electric vehicle transition — over 50% of new vehicles sold in China are now electric, a threshold crossed earlier than most forecasts anticipated. The IEA revised down its 2026 global oil demand forecast by 1.6 million barrels per day, attributing the shortfall primarily to Chinese and European demand destruction at record fuel prices. Meanwhile, the US SPR fell below 290 million barrels, its lowest level since 1982, as emergency releases have provided only partial price relief against a physical supply shortfall that the IEA's 400-million-barrel coordinated release has not fully offset. The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, September 8, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Tuesday's session is a pause in a market that has not stopped moving. Brent at $97.62 and WTI at $93.12 represent not a resolution of any of the underlying tensions but rather a momentary equilibrium between the weekend's shock (already priced) and the next escalation step (not yet priced). The Iran-Oman Hormuz deal is the day's defining variable: if Tehran formalises its corridor management authority and Washington accepts it, the market gets a temporary ceiling. If Washington rejects it and signals another military response, $100 Brent is not a ceiling but a floor.

The structural read: Nothing in this session's setup improves the physical market's underlying condition. Distillates are 14% below seasonal norms entering the heating season. The ULS1! October roll this week prices winter supply through a corridor moving 10 ships per day versus 25 million barrels per day before February 28. Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery has been hit twice in a month. The US SPR is at its lowest since 1982. Russia's refined product exports are down a third. EIA data Thursday will tell us whether the 4.5 million barrel crude draw of August 28 continued into the first week of September. The headline risk runs entirely to the upside.

My call: Brent $95–$101 today, with the range entirely driven by the Hormuz-deal and US-response headlines. A formalised Iran-Oman deal without escalation puts a soft ceiling near $100 and a floor near $95. Any fresh military action — or Iran's announced maritime zone declaration beyond Hormuz threatening Fujairah — removes the ceiling and targets $105–$115. The distillate market does not need a new headline to widen further; the structural deficit is already doing that work. Watch the Iran-Oman deal language, any CENTCOM statements, and the crude draw print on Thursday.
