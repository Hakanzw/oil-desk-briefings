Top Story

⚡ Supply Recovers, Diplomacy Stalls — Brent Slips Under $97 While Diesel Tightens

Brent Eases to ~$97 as Hormuz Flows Climb, but a Surprise Distillate Draw Sends Gasoil Higher and Iran Talks Remain Deadlocked

Crude drifted lower Thursday. Brent traded near $96.91 a barrel, down about 1.1%, according to Trading Economics, as Middle East supply conditions kept improving. Flows through the Strait of Hormuz have climbed to roughly 13.2 million bpd, and Saudi Arabia has restored about half of its East-West pipeline capacity. Quoted levels differ by provider and contract (other feeds showed Brent in the $97–98 range; Wednesday's settlement was reported near $103 by another source), so treat levels as approximate.

Product markets told a different story. The EIA reported US crude stocks rose 922,000 barrels to 427.3 million against an expected 264,000-barrel draw, but gasoline fell 1.7 million and distillates fell 2.3 million barrels to 105.2 million — about 14% below the five-year average. Refinery utilization slipped 1.5 points to 92.5%. ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) was quoted at $1,434.75/mt, up 5.07% over 24 hours per TradingView.

On diplomacy, Iran said it received the formal US response to its proposal to reopen Hormuz in phases, days after President Trump rejected the plan. Trump has said the choice is to "blow them up or make a deal." With OPEC+ expected to hold output steady at Sunday's meeting, geopolitics remains the dominant swing factor.

EIA: US Crude Unexpectedly Builds While Gasoline and Distillate Stocks Fall

US crude inventories rose 922,000 barrels to 427.3 million in the week ended Sept 25, against a 264,000-barrel draw expected. Cushing stocks rose 553,000 barrels. Gasoline fell 1.7 million barrels to 204.4 million and distillates fell 2.3 million to 105.2 million. Refinery runs fell 554,000 bpd, with utilization down 1.5 points to 92.5%. Diesel futures jumped 4.5% to $5.1175/gal after the report.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, October 1, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called the tape a tug-of-war between returning supply and stalled diplomacy. Today's data keeps that framing: Brent is softer near $97 while the diesel complex is firming.

My read on today: crude fundamentals look comfortable — a surprise US crude build, Hormuz flows at 13.2 mb/d, Saudi pipeline flows restored — yet products are tight. A 2.3-million-barrel distillate draw leaving stocks 14% below average is what drives ULS1! higher.

The bigger picture: without a deal, Hormuz remains a bottleneck and Trump's "blow them up or make a deal" rhetoric keeps tail risk alive. Data sources also disagree on levels today, so I am leaning on direction rather than precise prints.

My call: Brent trades $93–$100 today, with a mild downward bias on supply recovery but firm support from product tightness and unresolved diplomacy. Watch diesel — it is where the tightness is hiding.
