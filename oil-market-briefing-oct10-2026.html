Top Story

⚡ Weekend Wrap — Trump Opens a Russian Diesel Valve and Gasoil Drops Even as Hormuz Stays Choked

Brent Holds $104 as Trump Says Putin Will Release Diesel — Gasoil Slides 4.7% While Crude Barely Moves and Hormuz Risk Persists

Friday's session belonged to diesel, not crude. President Trump announced after a call with Vladimir Putin that Russia would "immediately" release hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel onto global markets, and the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a temporary general license authorizing the sale, delivery and importation of Russian-origin diesel, including into the United States, through April 7, 2027. Diesel futures fell about 4% on the day, per NBC News, though they remain up more than 110% since the start of 2026. Brent, by contrast, barely moved: Trading Economics put it at $104.43, up 0.14%, while NBC described it as down about 0.3% near $104. Brent finished the week roughly 1.5% to 2% higher.

The crude market is still being held up by the Gulf. Iranian tanker attacks last week were the highest weekly total since the war began, and MarineTraffic data cited by NBC showed Hormuz traffic averaging fewer than 23 ships a day from September 28, a small fraction of prewar levels. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said Tehran is reviewing a US reply to its offer to reopen the strait within seven days in exchange for a halt to hostilities and unfrozen assets. Trump's pledge not to strike Iran before the November 3 midterms has taken some strike risk out of the tape, but freight is at records and many tankers are tied up in ship-to-ship transfers near the strait.

The product complex tells a different story. ICE-linked London gasoil (October contract LGOV6, the front-month proxy for ULS1!) fell $70.50, or 4.67%, to about $1,438.75 per tonne on Investing.com, from a prior close of $1,483.00, with a day's range of $1,436 to $1,518.88. Investing.com's report tied the drop to the Russian diesel signal and to China resuming refined fuel exports in October. Caution is warranted: the October contract is at or near expiry, so thin trading can exaggerate the percentage move, and one source put the settlement nearer $1,437.75. Even after the drop, gasoil is roughly 90% above its $752.75 level of February 27.

The caveats on Russian barrels are large. Putin gave no volume or timing, Trump's post cited tranches of up to 4.8 million tonnes with the largest tied to the condition of Russian refineries, and Russia's own diesel export ban, extended through October, must be lifted first. Zelenskyy called the easing "an obvious weakness" and denied agreeing to stop striking Russian energy plants. Earlier 30-day waivers on Russian oil this spring expired without materially changing price trends, so traders are treating this as a ceiling on diesel rallies rather than a regime change.

Russia / Diesel

Trump Says Putin Will Release Diesel; OFAC License Runs Through April 7, 2027

Trump said Putin agreed to "immediately" release hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel, which he said would help bring prices down. Treasury's OFAC issued a temporary general license authorizing the sale, delivery, offloading and importation of Russian-origin diesel, including into the US, until April 7, 2027. Putin only said he had told Trump of Russia's "readiness to supply oil and petroleum products to the US and global markets," offering no specifics. Russia's deputy prime minister, Alexander Novak, said the July diesel export ban would begin to be lifted immediately. Diesel futures fell about 4%. For crude the effect is modest, but for diesel-importing Europe and Asia it is the first large supply-side relief since the war began, even though UK and EU sanctions remain in force.

06
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds November Quota at 31.01 mb/d; Gulf Output Stays Well Below Targets

OPEC+ agreed on October 4 to keep November production targets steady, with seven core members including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman deciding. The combined requirement is 31.01 million bpd, with Saudi Arabia at 10.478 million, Russia at 9.949 million and Iraq at 4.431 million. Nairametrics notes Gulf producers are pumping well below targets because of export disruption, with exports fluctuating at 60% to 80% of normal in recent months. Sources say changes are unlikely before 2027 pending a capacity review, and the group meets again on November 1. The quotas are largely paper: the war and Hormuz transit, not OPEC+ policy, determine the barrels available to the market, so the decision is neutral for near-term prices.

The Guru's Verdict — Saturday, October 10, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent a $100–$108 range biased higher into the storm landfall. The tape landed in the middle: Brent held about $104.43, up 0.14% on the day, and the hurricane premium was offset by Trump's diesel announcement. The more interesting move was in products, where London gasoil dropped 4.67% to roughly $1,439 a tonne. Crude is still being priced off Hormuz; diesel is now being priced off Washington and Moscow.

My read on today: The weekend is the risk. Markets are closed, Iran owes Washington an answer on the seven-day reopening offer, and Isaias damage reports arrive before Monday's open. Transits below 23 ships a day, a US crude draw of 3.2 million barrels and record freight argue against any collapse in Brent. I would not fade dips toward $100 unless Tehran gives a dated reopening plan, in which case $95 comes quickly.

Diesel is the nuance. The OFAC license through April 7, 2027 is a real policy shift, and combined with the G7 and IEA release it makes a cap on gasoil rallies credible. But Putin gave no volumes, Russia's export ban must be lifted, Ukrainian drones keep hitting refineries, and the October contract is expiring, which can exaggerate the percentage move. I read the drop as roughly half signal and half expiry noise.

The bigger picture: Natural gas near $3.20 after an 85 bcf build shows this is an oil and products story, not a general energy squeeze. My call: Brent trades $100–$108 into the Monday open, with a bias to the upside on weekend headline risk. Gasoil should stay range-bound to softer while Russian cargoes are verified, but a Hormuz setback would erase Friday's drop quickly.
