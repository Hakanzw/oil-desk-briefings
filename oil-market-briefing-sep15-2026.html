Top Story

⚡ Day 9 of the Triple Supply Shock — Salalah Dead, Pipeline Dark, Hormuz Blockaded

Markets Consolidate Above $107 as Triple Chokepoint Holds Firm; Bernstein Warns of $150 Crude If Diplomatic Freeze Persists

Tuesday's session opens with Brent holding just above $107 per barrel — a narrow consolidation after Monday's session pushed as high as $109 — as the three-way supply stranglehold that has gripped markets since September 10 shows no sign of loosening. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, the only major bypass around Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, remains shut following drone strikes from Iraqi territory on September 10–11 and repair crews face a 3-to-5-week timeline before any meaningful restoration of flows. More damaging still for the diplomatic front, the Iran–GCC summit in Oman's Salalah that traders had pinned as the relief valve for Hormuz pressure collapsed before it started, postponed indefinitely after Gulf Arab states withdrew citing the escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure.

The compounding of three simultaneous disruptions — the Hormuz blockade (active since March), the Red Sea / Bab-el-Mandeb corridor restricted by Houthi drone campaigns, and now the Saudi pipeline — has drawn comparisons to scenarios analysts once called extreme tail risk. Bernstein analysts Neil Beveridge and Brian Ho warned clients Monday that their prior full-year Brent forecast of $90 per barrel had been "exceeded by reality" and placed a fresh upside scenario at $120 to $150 per barrel if transit flows through the three chokepoints do not normalise before Q4 demand season arrives. Saudi August production has already hit its lowest level in more than 30 years. Crude flow through the combined Hormuz–Bab-el-Mandeb–Suez corridor has collapsed from roughly 20 million barrels per day pre-conflict to below 7 million bpd.

The sole piece of near-term data relief comes Wednesday, when the EIA releases its weekly petroleum status report for the week ending September 12 — the first print to fully capture the pipeline-shutdown shock. East Coast distillate inventories fell to a record low of 19.3 million barrels in late August, and diesel crack spreads have hit $101/barrel; any further draw will intensify pressure on refinery margins and pump prices. US diesel has already broken through $6 per gallon for the first time in history. The IEA's September 2026 Oil Market Report, released this week, cut its 2026 global supply forecast by a further 1.3 mb/d, projecting world output at just 100.7 mb/d — down 5.7 mb/d year-on-year — and pushed its estimate for full Gulf supply normalisation into 2027.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Pipeline Attack

Saudi East-West Pipeline Stays Dark — Yanbu Port Has Just 5–7 Days of Export Inventory

Saudi Arabia's 1,200-kilometre East-West pipeline, which normally carries up to 7 million barrels per day to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, remains shut after drone strikes attributed to Iranian-backed Iraqi militias on September 10–11. With the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed by Iran, the pipeline was the Kingdom's only high-volume export route. Regional officials told PBS that repairs could take three to five weeks, and Yanbu's shore storage can cover only 5–7 days of exports at current drawdown rates. Saudi August production has already hit its lowest in more than 30 years.

Diplomacy

Salalah Talks Collapse — Iran-GCC Hormuz Summit Postponed Indefinitely After Arab States Pull Back

The Iran-GCC meeting planned for Salalah, Oman — the most substantive direct diplomatic contact since the war began — fell through just as it was due to convene, with Gulf Arab states citing the escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure as incompatible with constructive dialogue. Oman said the meeting was postponed to 'ensure appropriate conditions.' The collapse removes the single near-term catalyst that could have taken pressure off the Hormuz choke. Al Jazeera reported that a temporary shipping deal would have given Iran authority over vessel passage in exchange for resuming commercial transit; no rescheduled date has been announced.

Analyst Warning

Bernstein Warns of $150 Crude: "Triple Pressures Compounding Simultaneously — Unprecedented Strain"

Bernstein analysts Neil Beveridge and Brian Ho told clients Monday that their 2026 Brent forecast of $90 had been 'exceeded by reality' and outlined a scenario in which simultaneous blockages of Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, and the Saudi pipeline push prices to $120–$150 per barrel. The analysts described this as a market experiencing a 'long-term supply shortage' rather than a cyclical spike. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright separately warned against expecting 'a deal with Iran or a diplomatic breakthrough over the Strait of Hormuz.' BMI/Fitch Solutions cut its probability of a US-Iran deal in Q3 from 50% to 40% following the Salalah collapse.

04
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds October Output Unchanged — But Group Acknowledges It 'Cannot Guarantee Barrels Will Reach Market'

OPEC+'s seven core members agreed at a September 6 virtual meeting to maintain September 2026 production levels for October, completing the rollback of the 2023 voluntary cuts. The September 188,000-bpd increase was the final step in that reversal. However, Rystad Energy analyst Jorge Leon cautioned that 'OPEC+ currently has very limited power over the physical oil market — the group can change production targets on paper, but it cannot guarantee those barrels will reach market,' given the export route constraints. OECD commercial inventories fell 26.4 million barrels in June to 66.5 million barrels below the five-year average.

05
Fuel Prices

US Diesel Crosses $6/Gallon for First Time Ever — East Coast Distillates Hit 44-Year Seasonal Low

US retail diesel broke through $6 per gallon on September 11, reaching $6.0556 — a 63% rise from $3.70 twelve months earlier and the highest price in American history. The record comes on top of record diesel crack spreads of $101/barrel. East Coast distillate inventories collapsed to 19.3 million barrels in late August — their lowest August level since 1982 — as the triple supply shock, Russia's diesel export ban (extended to September 30), and the closure of Gulf distillate export routes combine. National gasoline averaged $4.14 by Labor Day weekend.

06
IEA Data

IEA Cuts 2026 Supply Forecast 1.3 mb/d — Gulf Recovery Pushed to 2027, August Output Hits Crisis Low

The IEA's September 2026 Oil Market Report cut its 2026 world oil supply forecast to 100.7 mb/d — down 5.7 mb/d year-on-year and 1.3 mb/d lower than August's report — as a full Middle East supply recovery slips to 2027. Global oil production fell 1.6 mb/d month-on-month to 100.1 mb/d in August, with more than 10 mb/d of Gulf output remaining shut in. World oil demand is also forecast to fall 2.5 mb/d in 2026, though the IEA expects a 2.6 mb/d recovery in 2027 as conflict disruptions normalize. The widening supply-demand dislocation is concentrated in middle distillates and petrochemical feedstocks, particularly in Asia.

The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, September 15, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called $105–$118 with an upward bias until either Salalah was rescheduled or the pipeline restarted. Both conditions remained unfulfilled as of this morning: the Saudi East-West pipeline stays dark, Salalah is in indefinite limbo, and the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively Iranian-controlled. Brent's $107 handle is not a consolidation of strength — it is a market that has absorbed an enormous supply shock over nine sessions and is now waiting for the next move.

My read on today: The price is right for the news, but the news has not stopped. Bernstein's $120–$150 call is not hyperbole — it is an arithmetic exercise in what happens when three major transit corridors are simultaneously blocked as Q4 heating demand arrives. The only substantive question is whether any of the three chokepoints opens before demand peaks in November. Right now, the probability of any of them opening appears lower today than it did last week. Salalah was the best near-term chance and it is gone. The pipeline will not restart for weeks.

The structural picture: The IEA's numbers are unambiguous. Global supply is running 5.7 mb/d below year-ago levels. OECD commercial inventories are 66.5 million barrels below the five-year average and falling. The EIA's distillate data from Wednesday will tell us how fast the buffer is draining. East Coast distillates at a 44-year seasonal low heading into October is the most dangerous inventory position in the US supply system since the 1982 energy crisis. If that number prints badly Wednesday, the political and market pressure on the administration to act escalates sharply.

My call: Brent trades $104–$113 today, biased higher into the EIA print and with significant tail risk to the upside if any new attack or Aramco statement escalates the pipeline story. The triple chokepoint is the market. Until one leg of it breaks, $100+ Brent is the floor, not the ceiling.
