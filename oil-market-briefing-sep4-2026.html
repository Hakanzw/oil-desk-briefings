⚡ Weekly Wrap — Oil's Biggest Surge Since July: US Strikes Iranian Tankers, Hormuz Transits Collapse

Brent Holds $95.67 as Oil Posts Steepest Weekly Gain Since Mid-July: US Strikes Iranian Government Tankers for the First Time, Strait Transits Fall to Six Ships, and the OPEC+ JMMC Convenes Sunday

The week ending September 4 delivered the most significant escalation in the US-Iran war since July, and the oil market priced every headline. West Texas Intermediate surged toward $92 — a weekly gain of 9.8% — while Brent gained 7.1% to settle at $95.67, its steepest weekly advance since mid-July. The catalyst was September 1's US military action: Washington launched its largest barrage since July, striking roughly 100 IRGC targets in and around the Strait of Hormuz, including — for the first time in the conflict — two Iranian government tankers under a new 'tanker for tanker' retaliation policy. Iran's IRGC claimed retaliatory strikes on US facilities in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan; Jordanian forces confirmed intercepting 10 of 13 incoming ballistic missiles. The week's final bullish catalyst arrived Thursday, when Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz threatened to "cripple all of Iran's national military and civilian infrastructure — including energy infrastructure", briefly pushing Brent to an intraday high of $97.39 before a partial reversal into Friday's close.

Beneath the headline moves, the physical picture at the Strait of Hormuz has darkened materially. Independent maritime analytics firm Kpler recorded just six vessels transiting the strait on Wednesday — against a 10-day average of 13 and a pre-war baseline of roughly 100 per day — while Lloyd's List Intelligence put the August 26–September 1 daily average at around 12. These figures stand in sharp contrast to the US government's public accounting: Energy Secretary Chris Wright stated Monday's transit totalled 17 million barrels, and Trump described 40 ships being guided through nightly under US Navy escort. Iran's Parliament Speaker offered a simpler framing: "the enemy managed to get some ships through, but we remain in complete control of the strait." Iran's Foreign Minister has separately stated the strait will not fully reopen until Washington lifts sanctions and pays war reparations.

The weekend's key scheduled catalyst is Sunday's OPEC+ Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee. Multiple sources told Reuters the group will confirm a freeze on output for Q4 2026, following September's final 188,000 bpd increase that completed the rollback of 2023 voluntary cuts. The confirmation matters because the underlying supply context has shifted since August: Venezuela is actively weighing a formal OPEC exit; Iraq's exports rebounded 73% in August to 2.34 million bpd (still less than two-thirds of the pre-war 3.7 million bpd level); and spare capacity has narrowed critically after the UAE's departure in May. What the JMMC says Sunday about energy infrastructure threats and maritime security will be as closely parsed as the production headline itself.

Iraq's Oil Exports Jump 73% in August to 2.34M bpd — Highest Since War Began, Still Less Than Two-Thirds of Pre-War Level

Iraq's crude exports rebounded sharply in August, rising roughly 73% to approximately 2.34 million barrels per day from around 1.35 million bpd in July — the highest level since the war began in late February 2026. Steep price discounts and alternative shipping arrangements through non-Hormuz routes partially restored flows disrupted by the strait crisis. Preliminary Vortexa and Kpler estimates put the figure at 2.3M and 2.17M bpd respectively, still less than two-thirds of the 3.7 million bpd Iraq exported before the war. Analysts caution that Iraq's recovery remains vulnerable to renewed Hormuz hostilities and persistent route-switch logistics constraints.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, September 4, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

The week ending September 4 has done something structurally important to this market: it has transformed what was a three-theatre supply disruption into a four-theatre problem. The Hormuz conflict was already priced; the Ust-Luga fire was largely absorbed; the Saudi Sidr crew deaths added a human-cost premium. What Thursday added — Israeli Defense Minister Katz's explicit threat to target Iranian civilian and energy infrastructure — is not yet priced. The market spiked to $97.39 intraday and then pulled back to $95.67, which is the correct initial reaction: it acknowledged the risk without committing to a scenario. That hovering is where Brent ends the week.

My read on today: Friday is a consolidation session, not a directional one. The week's move is in the books — Brent +7.1%, WTI +9.8% — and no single headline strong enough to break the $94–$97 range is scheduled before Sunday's OPEC+ JMMC. The JMMC itself is expected to be procedurally benign: a Q4 freeze is priced, the group has limited room to surprise, and spare capacity has narrowed to the point where unilateral policy moves carry limited credibility. What traders will watch heading into the weekend is whether Katz's statement was rhetorical or operational, and whether the next Kpler Hormuz transit count prints above or below the current 10-day average of 13.

The bigger picture: Venezuela's OPEC exit deliberations are a symptom, not a cause. They tell you that the cartel architecture which worked through 2022–2024 is under structural stress from multiple directions simultaneously — the UAE gone, Iraq overproducing, Venezuela pivoting toward Washington, Saudi Arabia managing price floors in a war environment it did not design and cannot control. OPEC+ has completed unwinding its voluntary cuts at precisely the moment the underlying supply system is most disrupted since 2008. The Q4 freeze is the right call given the uncertainty, but the spare-capacity cushion to enforce any floor — below 2 mb/d after the UAE exit — is thinner than at any point in the modern era.

My call: Brent trades $93–$99 today, with the most probable range $94.50–$97.00 absent a new headline shock. The $97.39 intraday high from Thursday is the first resistance level; above that, the market needs a fresh catalyst — an Israeli strike, a Hormuz transit count collapse below five per day for multiple sessions, or another major US-Iran exchange. The unpriced tail risk is a direct Israeli strike on Iranian energy infrastructure — that is the $110+ scenario, and Katz put it explicitly on the table this week. The market acknowledged it with a brief spike. It has not priced it. Watch carefully.
