Top Story

Friday, September 11 — US-Iran Tanker War Escalates: Ballistic Missiles Strike US Destroyer; Brent Tops $107 as Distillates Plunge Below Historic Floor

Brent Surges Past $107 as Iran Fires Ballistic Missiles at US Carrier Strike Group — Hormuz at Fraction of Pre-War Flow; Distillates Drop Below 100 Million Barrels for First Time in Decades

On the twenty-fifth anniversary of the September 11 attacks, global energy markets contend with a crisis less sudden but no less acute. Brent crude rose to $107.72/bbl (+0.28%) — the highest level in two months and roughly $10 above Tuesday's close — as Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired a wave of ballistic missiles at a US carrier strike group patrolling near the Strait of Hormuz overnight. US Central Command confirmed a destroyer sustained minor damage; Iran's state media claimed a direct hit on a carrier. US forces responded by permanently disabling two Iranian crude tankers and sinking a third. For a market that had spent two weeks slowly pricing the Iran-Oman shipping deal as a soft de-escalation ceiling, Thursday night's exchange has stripped that narrative away.

The physical market remains critically impaired. Strait of Hormuz transit is averaging approximately 10 commodity ships per day — versus the pre-war baseline of vessels carrying nearly 20 million barrels of oil and LNG daily. Marine analytics firm Kpler reported only 6–13 vessels crossing daily on a rolling 10-day basis, sharply contradicting US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's claim of 17 million barrels shipped recently — a discrepancy independent analysts say renders US assertions of a 'fully open' waterway at odds with observable shipping data. Murban crude — the UAE benchmark increasingly used as a Hormuz workaround indicator — surged to $122.50/bbl (+5.36%), the premium reflecting the extreme scarcity of Gulf crude actually clearing the chokepoint.

The structural backdrop has deteriorated further since Tuesday's briefing. The EIA's September Short-Term Energy Outlook, released September 9, confirmed that US distillate fuel oil inventories have dropped below 100 million barrels — below the five-year (2021–2025) average low for this time of year and forecast to remain there through much of 2027. The October ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil front-month contract — now the active contract following the September expiry on Wednesday — is trading at $1,422.75/mt (−0.38%), the extreme gasoil crack spread reflecting the severity of the heating-season distillate deficit compounded by supply disruption. EIA also reported that global oil inventories have declined by 400 million barrels since January, amplifying the market's sensitivity to any further Hormuz escalation.

Geopolitics / Hormuz

Iran's IRGC fired multiple ballistic missiles at a US carrier strike group near the Strait of Hormuz overnight, with US Central Command confirming a destroyer sustained minor damage. US forces responded by permanently disabling two Iranian crude tankers and sinking a third — the most destructive single US action against Iranian shipping since the conflict began February 28. Kpler data puts Strait of Hormuz vessel traffic at just 10 ships per day on a 10-day rolling average — far below the US Treasury's 17 million-barrel throughput claim.

Demand Destruction

Sinopec Research: China's Oil Demand to Fall 8.9% in 2026 — Gasoline Down 8.7%, Diesel Down 11.4%; Third Consecutive Annual Decline

Sinopec's research arm has forecast that Chinese oil demand will decline by 8.9% in 2026 — the sharpest single-year drop since the 1970s oil shock era and the third consecutive annual decline. Gasoline demand is projected to fall 8.7% and diesel 11.4%, reflecting record fuel prices and the rapid EV transition (now over 50% of new Chinese vehicle sales). China's crude imports already fell 32% in Q2 2026 versus Q1 as Hormuz disruption drove prices higher, before partially recovering to 8.93 mb/d in August. The Sinopec data represents the most significant demand-side counterweight to the extreme supply disruption premium currently embedded in prices, and it shapes the ceiling on how far crude can rally before accelerating its own demand destruction.

OPEC+ / Production

OPEC+ September Hike Completes Full Voluntary-Cut Rollback — Q4 Quotas Frozen; Larger 2 mb/d Cut Stays Through Year-End

OPEC+ has completed the unwinding of its 3.5 million barrels per day of voluntary cuts first announced in 2023, with September's 188,000 bpd increase finalising the rollback. For Q4 2026, the group will hold quotas flat. The larger 2 mb/d cut from 2022 remains in place through year-end, citing ongoing market volatility and the US-Iran conflict. The practical effect of the September increase remains limited: actual Gulf OPEC+ production lags quotas significantly because Hormuz disruptions prevent exports from reaching destination markets at quota-implied volumes. The group's 2027 production baseline review — via DeGolyer and MacNaughton capacity assessments — is expected in late September.

EIA / Inventories

EIA September STEO: Distillates Below 100 mb — Five-Year Low; Global Stocks Down 400 mb in 2026; Crude at 424.5 mb for Week Ending Sep 4

The EIA's September Short-Term Energy Outlook (released September 9) delivered a stark assessment: US distillate fuel oil inventories have dropped below 100 million barrels and will remain below the five-year (2021–2025) seasonal low through much of 2027. The EIA forecasts Brent averaging around $90/bbl in H2 2026 — a forecast that appeared conservative before Thursday night's missile exchange. The most recent Weekly Petroleum Status Report (week ending September 4, released September 10) showed US commercial crude at 424.5 million barrels — 1% above the five-year average. Global oil inventories have declined by 400 million barrels since January, and tightness in the global distillate market has raised domestic prices and incentivised US exporters to increase distillate exports, removing further supply from domestic markets.

LNG / Qatar

Qatar Ras Laffan LNG: 13 MT/Year Offline After Iran Strikes — $3 Billion Rebuild; 3–5 Year Recovery; EU Gas Storage Falls Behind Target

Iran-backed attacks on Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG facility have removed approximately 13 million tonnes per year of liquefaction capacity — roughly 5% of global LNG supply — with repair costs estimated near $3 billion and a timeline of three to five years, per Rystad Energy. Iran-backed forces have executed at least 172 attacks on nonmilitary infrastructure across six GCC nations since February 28, with nearly half targeting energy facilities. US oil companies' regional gas supplies are projected to drop roughly 40% this year. The Qatar damage is structurally significant for Europe: EU gas storage stands at roughly 55% capacity against an 80% November target, and TTF gas trades near €72.73/MWh, with European winter supply security deteriorating ahead of the heating season.

US-Saudi Nuclear Deal in 90-Day Congressional Review — Enrichment Path Could Free Oil Barrels; Proliferation Risk Debated at Moment of Regional Instability

The landmark US-Saudi commercial nuclear cooperation agreement — signed in July 2026 and giving Saudi Arabia a path to enriching nuclear fuel using US firms — has entered its 90-day congressional review period. The Guru's Verdict — Friday, September 11, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Twenty-five years on from the original September 11, global energy markets face a crisis less sudden but no less structural. Brent at $107.72, WTI at $102.50, and Murban at $122.50 are not the product of a single morning's shock but of six months of cumulative damage: Hormuz averaging 10 ships per day, Ras Laffan offline for three to five years, distillates below 100 million barrels heading into the northern hemisphere's heating season, and now the first confirmed ballistic missile hit on a US Navy vessel in this conflict.

The read on today: The Iran-Oman shipping deal that briefly looked like a ceiling on Brent in the high $90s has been superseded by Thursday night's missile exchange. The market is no longer pricing a de-escalation path — it is pricing the probability distribution of a US military response. The two most important variables today are the nature and timing of any CENTCOM action and whether that response targets non-oil Iranian military assets (contained escalation: $105–$112 corridor) or oil infrastructure including Kharg Island (severe escalation: $115–$130+).

The structural read: Even if today passes without fresh military escalation, the physical market's fundamentals have not improved. Distillates are now below 100 million barrels — a threshold the EIA says will hold through much of 2027. The gasoil crack embedded in the October ULS1! front-month at $1,422.75/mt is pricing a heating season without adequate supply. Global inventories are 400 million barrels lighter than at the start of the year. The EIA's own H2 2026 Brent forecast of $90/bbl, published just two days ago on Tuesday, is already looking insufficient given the overnight escalation and the structural deficit.

My call: Brent $104–$114 today, with the range entirely driven by CENTCOM and White House escalation decisions over the next several hours. The distillate market needs no new catalyst to widen further — that story is already written in the inventory data. The headline risk is asymmetrically to the upside, and today's anniversary of September 11 does not make de-escalation any more likely.
