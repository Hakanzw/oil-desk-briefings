⚡ Breaking — Iran Sanctions Reinstated, Hormuz Near-Shutdown: The Supply Crisis Deepens

Iran Sanctions Waiver Expires Today, Hormuz Vessel Transits Crash to 10 Per Day — Brent Eyes $90

The clock struck midnight and the market changed. At 12:01am EDT today, the 60-day US Treasury waiver on Iranian oil sanctions expired, and with it any legal pathway for importing nations to accept Tehran's crude. Roughly 1–1.5 million barrels per day of Iranian oil — already at sea, already loaded, already hedged — is now unsellable in an international market where buyers dare not violate primary US sanctions. This is not a threatened revocation or a political signal; it is a legal fact, effective this morning, and the tape is reading it clearly: Brent added nearly a dollar in early Friday trade to reach $85.02, representing a weekly gain exceeding 11% and capping a week in which the front-month contract touched $87 intraday — the first time at that level since June.

That intraday move to $87 came in the immediate aftermath of IRGC missile attacks on three commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz on July 7, followed by US military strikes on Iran. The IRGC struck a Qatari LNG tanker and a Saudi supertanker, sending a clear signal that the June 17 US-Iran memorandum of understanding was under severe strain. As of today, the Strait's daily transit count has collapsed to approximately 10 vessels — down from roughly 38 per day when this briefing last published on July 12, and a fraction of the ~130 vessels per day that traversed the world's most critical chokepoint before the war began. The Strait carries an estimated 20% of global seaborne oil and 20% of global LNG. At 10 transits per day, it is not a disrupted artery; it is a near-closed one.

The structural picture now features three simultaneous supply headwinds with no single political release valve. Iranian crude is fully embargoed; the Strait of Hormuz is approaching functional closure; and the OPEC+ paper increase of 188,000 barrels per day for August remains largely theoretical for Gulf members whose export infrastructure sits on the wrong side of the chokepoint. Against this backdrop, the EIA's latest inventory report — showing a 1.7-million-barrel crude draw and gasoline stocks running 8% below the five-year average — confirms the physical tightening the market has been pricing for weeks. The lone outlier in an otherwise bullish-supply picture: distillates posted a surprise +4.6-million-barrel build, suggesting that downstream diesel demand destruction may be emerging as an internal circuit breaker.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Sanctions

Iran Sanctions Waiver Expires — Full Oil Embargo Reinstated; Iranian Crude Now Unsellable

The 60-day US Treasury waiver on Iranian oil sanctions expired at 12:01am EDT on July 17, reinstating the full Iranian oil embargo and rendering Iranian crude currently at sea effectively unsellable to most international buyers. The waiver had been granted during June peace negotiations to allow limited Iranian oil transactions while diplomacy progressed. With diplomacy stalled following the July 7 tanker attacks and subsequent US military strikes, the Trump administration allowed the waiver to lapse without renewal, removing an estimated 1–1.5 million barrels per day of effective supply from accessible markets.

Geopolitics

IRGC Strikes Ships in Hormuz, US Retaliates — Ceasefire Collapses as Transits Fall to 10/Day

The Iranian IRGC fired missiles at two commercial vessels on July 7 — a Qatari LNG tanker and a Saudi supertanker — prompting US military strikes on Iran and the effective collapse of ceasefire diplomacy. A third vessel was also targeted, according to US Central Command, which stated it was "imposing heavy costs for targeting commercial shipping." Vessel transits through the Strait have since fallen from approximately 38 per day to just 10 per day as of this week, per Kpler tracking data. Bloomberg characterized the situation as a "virtual standstill" in Hormuz shipping traffic as Iranian-US skirmishes intensified.

03
OPEC+

Fifth Consecutive Monthly Hike Approved — 188k bpd for August, But Gulf Delivery Constrained

Seven OPEC+ members agreed on July 6 to raise collective output by 188,000 barrels per day beginning August 2026 — the fifth straight monthly increase as the group continues unwinding 2023-era cuts. Saudi Arabia and Russia each absorb 62,000 bpd of the increase. The decision was made when Brent was trading below $72 following the June peace MOU. However, analysts note that quota increases on paper provide limited relief when Gulf export infrastructure remains constrained by the Hormuz near-closure. The next OPEC+ ministerial review is scheduled for August 2.

04
EIA Data

Crude Stocks Down 1.7M Barrels, Gasoline 8% Below 5-Year Average — Distillates Surprise +4.6M

The EIA's weekly petroleum status report for the period ending July 10, released July 15, showed US commercial crude inventories fell 1.7 million barrels to 409.7 million barrels — now 6% below the five-year seasonal average. Gasoline stocks dropped a further 1.5 million barrels and stand 8% below the seasonal average, consistent with tight products-market conditions. The outlier: distillate stocks surged an unexpected 4.6 million barrels, a potential early signal of diesel demand destruction at elevated prices. Refinery utilization rose to 96.2%, processing 17.1 million barrels per day.

Infrastructure

Chevron Moves Into Iraq's West Qurna 2 and Evaluates Mediterranean Pipeline — A Hormuz Exit Strategy

Chevron signed memoranda of understanding with Iraq to advance its bid to develop the West Qurna 2 oilfield — currently producing approximately 460,000 barrels per day since Russia's Lukoil was sanctioned out — and joined a consortium evaluating pipeline routes to carry Iraqi crude to the Mediterranean, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz entirely. The deal reflects Baghdad's strategic imperative to diversify export routes after Hormuz closures earlier in 2026 forced deep production cuts and revenue losses. A secondary Basrah-Haditha corridor branching toward Turkey, Jordan, or Syria is also under evaluation.

Strategy

US Backs Iraq-Syria Oil Pipeline to Permanently Route Around the Strait of Hormuz

The US is actively backing reconstruction of the long-idled Kirkuk-Baniyas pipeline, which would move northern Iraqi crude to Syria's Mediterranean coast and permanently reduce Hormuz exposure for a significant volume of Middle Eastern supply. Special Presidential Envoy Thomas Barrack has facilitated talks between US energy firms and Iraqi and Syrian officials. The initiative involves Chevron and other US majors and represents a structural long-term response to Iran's demonstrated ability to weaponize the Strait. Construction timelines extend into 2028–2029, but the US political commitment marks a strategic inflection.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, July 17, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Five days ago I called Brent in a $74–$82 range for the week — sideways to higher, with the Monday open as the dominant variable. Brent broke above that resistance band decisively, touching $87 intraday mid-week before settling into this morning at $85.02, and both benchmarks are tracking a weekly gain exceeding 11%. This was not an overshoot: every structural element I cited last Sunday — the sanctions waiver, the Hormuz transit collapse, the diplomatic vacuum — has materialized in the worst-case direction simultaneously. The market did what it should when fundamentals deteriorate all at once.

The situation as of this morning: The Iranian sanctions waiver is now expired. Hormuz transit is at roughly 10 vessels per day — a number that would have constituted a market emergency in any prior year. The June peace framework is structurally over, not merely paused. And OPEC+ is delivering paper increases that Gulf exporters cannot physically ship through a near-closed strait. This is no longer a geopolitical risk premium. This is a physical supply crisis, and the market is only beginning to price it in full.

The one complicating factor — and the reason I stop short of calling a straight line to $100 — is the distillate data. A +4.6 million barrel build in distillates in a single week is not noise; it is a potential early signal of diesel demand destruction at these price levels. Demand destruction is the market's internal circuit breaker. If industrial users start cutting diesel consumption in response to $85+ crude, the physical tightening has a self-limiting dimension. But we are not there yet. Gasoline stocks are 8% below average, crude is 6% below average, and no new supply source is positioned to replace what Iran and Hormuz have removed from the market.

My call: Brent trades $82–$92 in the sessions ahead, with today's sanctions reinstatement providing a structural floor around $83. The upside case — confirmed diplomatic collapse, Hormuz at or near zero transits, or a weekend incident — puts $94–$98 in range by next week. The downside case — a surprise back-channel breakthrough via Qatari or Omani mediation — represents the only credible path below $80 in the near term. The risk is asymmetric: the floor has moved up, and the ceiling is wherever diplomacy restarts.
