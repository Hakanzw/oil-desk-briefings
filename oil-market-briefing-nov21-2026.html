⚡ Weekend Read — Brent Edges Up to $92.57 as Hormuz Tanker Attacks Intensify Against a Surplus Backdrop

Brent Gains 0.64% to $92.57 and Gasoil Adds 0.32% to $1,170.25/mt as Iran Escalates Tanker Attacks While the IEA Flags a Returning Surplus

Brent is quoted at $92.57/bbl, ▲ +0.64% (+$0.59), while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) is at $1,170.25/mt, ▲ +0.32% (+$3.75). WTI sits near $83.41, up about 1.2%, and natural gas futures near $3.41, up 4.4%, on the Oilprice ticker. The modest gains follow a week in which crude drifted lower on surplus forecasts before reversing: Oilprice noted Brent at $92.67 and WTI at $83.06 on Thursday night after an earlier slide. The market is trading as a tug-of-war between a physical glut that is forming on paper and a war that refuses to end, so rallies and dips both lack conviction heading into the Thanksgiving-shortened week.

The escalation is at sea. Oilprice reported on November 20 that Iran has stepped up tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, with incidents nearly daily in November according to Bloomberg data, after October's attacks hit the highest level since the war began at the end of February. Windward counted five tankers struck near Hormuz between November 11 and 18, and ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed one of its vessels was attacked on the evening of November 17. Windward called it a strategy of "maritime attrition" unlikely to ease absent diplomatic change. Crude volumes leaving the Strait have declined in recent days, though the cause is not certain. CNBC reported a third US carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, has arrived in the Gulf and that indirect talks via Qatar have stalled.

The bearish counterweight is supply. The IEA said in its monthly report that global supply exceeded demand for the first time since the war began, with October supply at 105.4 million bpd, up 2.3 million bpd on September, and observed global stocks rising 52 million barrels, the first build since June. It projects a 6.6 million bpd supply increase in 2027. For diesel the picture differs: the IEA says attacks on Russian energy infrastructure continue to choke off the country's diesel supplies, and the EU is drafting rules to rebuild distillate-heavy emergency stocks. That is why gasoil holds above $1,170/mt even as Brent struggles to hold the low $90s.

Hormuz

Iran Steps Up Hormuz Tanker Attacks as Oil Prices Slide

Oilprice.com reported on November 20 that Iran has intensified attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, with incidents nearly daily in November according to Bloomberg data, after October recorded the highest level of attacks since the war began in late February. Windward counted five tankers struck between November 11 and 18, and ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed one of its vessels was hit on November 17. Signal Group's Maria Bertzeletou said security risks continue to weigh on tanker operations, while Windward called it a campaign of maritime attrition. Crude volumes exiting the Strait have declined in recent days. For crude, this is a persistent risk premium that keeps dips shallow; for diesel, higher freight and insurance costs feed directly into delivered product prices in Europe and Asia.

Gulf Escalation

Iran Says Army Targeted US Drone and Missile Sites in Kuwait as Gulf Attacks Mount

CNBC reported on November 18 that Iran said its army targeted US drone and missile installations at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base, extending attacks on Gulf states hosting US forces. Kuwait's army said air defenses countered hostile drones, and Bahrain issued an all-clear after intercepting several threats. CNBC put Brent at $92.70 and WTI at $84.90 at the time. The risk for crude is spillover onto Gulf export infrastructure, which would tighten supply just as forecasters expect a surplus.

Supply / Surplus

IEA: Supply Exceeds Demand for First Time Since War Began; Russian Output Rises

The IEA's monthly report, via CNBC, showed global oil supply reached 105.4 million bpd in October, up 2.3 million bpd from September and 5.3 million bpd since August, putting supply above demand for the first time since the Iran war began. Observed global inventories rose 52 million barrels, the first increase since June, which the agency called a tentative sign of rebalancing. Russian crude output rose 210,000 bpd to 8.73 million bpd. The IEA raised its fourth-quarter supply estimate by 3.3 million bpd and projects a 6.6 million bpd supply rise in 2027. That is the core bearish case for Brent, though diesel remains tight because product exports from Russia and the Middle East are constrained.

Iran / Blockade

Iran Moves to Restart Oil Exports Despite US Naval Blockade

Oilprice.com reported on November 20 that Iran is preparing to resume crude shipments while the US plans to tighten its naval blockade of Iranian ports. Bloomberg, citing United Against Nuclear Iran and Kpler, reported a cargo moved from a sanctioned Suezmax to a non-sanctioned vessel, and US Central Command warned that ships receiving ship-to-ship transfers from Iran-linked vessels can be stopped, boarded, diverted or captured. Iran exported about 2 million bpd in March, nearly all to China, but exports have dried up since July, and output has fallen to 1.8 million bpd, about half of pre-war levels, per Kpler. IRGC-linked media floated a convoy of tankers to China. A confrontation would add risk premium; successful evasion would add modest barrels.

Europe / Diesel

EU Moves to Overhaul Emergency Oil Stockpiles to Cover Diesel and Jet Fuel Gaps

Bloomberg, via Oilprice.com on November 19, reported the European Commission is drafting rules requiring member states to align emergency reserves with national fuel consumption and refining capacity. Current rules require 90 days of net imports or 61 days of consumption. EU reserves stood at 103.5 million tons in July, including 36.5 million tons of gasoil and diesel, with 13 member states below minimums. The Iran war added an estimated €40 billion to the EU diesel import bill between March and September, and the IEA says members will release 71 million barrels through February, including about 15 million barrels of diesel. Planned approval is early December. Structural stock-building for distillates is a medium-term support for gasoil cracks.

Oil Rebounds as Houthi Attacks Reignite Middle East Supply Fears

Oilprice.com reported on November 19 that crude reversed an earlier weekly decline after media reported Houthi strikes on targets in Riyadh, including the financial district and the sovereign wealth fund headquarters, plus a petrochemicals company in Jubail. Against that, the IEA expects surplus to return this quarter and investment banks say the market is already oversupplied, while expectations of a December Fed rate hike and perceived demand destruction weigh on sentiment. Trackers say Gulf flows remain below pre-war levels and have declined since a September rebound.

The Guru's Verdict — Saturday, November 21, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that the market refuses to break either way, and today's tape confirms it. Brent at $92.57 (+0.64%) and gasoil at $1,170.25/mt (+0.32%) are small moves in a week full of large headlines: nightly tanker strikes, Houthi attacks on Riyadh, a third US carrier in the Gulf, and an IEA report declaring the first supply surplus of the war. Volatility in the news is high; volatility in price is not, which tells me both camps are well positioned.

My read on today: The bearish story is about barrels and the bullish story is about ships. The IEA numbers (105.4 million bpd supply, +52 million barrels of stocks in October) are hard data. The Hormuz attacks are real too, but so far they have reduced flows at the margin rather than closing the Strait. Until attrition turns into a sustained export loss, the surplus narrative should cap rallies near the mid-$90s.

The bigger picture: Diesel is where the two stories diverge. Crude can slide on surplus while distillates stay firm because Russian refining is impaired, the EU is rewriting its stock rules to hold more diesel and jet, and tanker risk raises delivered costs. Gasoil above $1,170/mt with Brent near $92 is a wide crack, and I would not expect it to close quickly. Natural gas, up 4.4% to about $3.41, adds a seasonal firming note.

My call: Brent trades $89–$96 on Monday, with a mild upside bias from the weekend's escalation risk but limited conviction ahead of the November 29 OPEC+ meeting. Respect the range, fade the first headline, and watch distillates for the real signal.
