Top Story

⚡ Supply Returns — Middle East Flows Rebuild While the Hormuz Deal Stays Frozen

Brent Steadies Near $96 as Saudi Pipeline Revival and Emergency US Reserve Release Take the Edge Off the Rally

Crude found its footing Wednesday after a sharp slide. Brent held around $96 a barrel, roughly flat on the day, as the physical picture improved: the 10-day average of Middle East crude exports has recovered to about 17.5 million bpd — roughly 98% of pre-war levels — according to Trading Economics' market summary. Saudi Arabia has restored flows on its East-West pipeline to around 3.5 million bpd, about half of its 7 million bpd capacity.

Satellite imagery cited by CNBC pointed to a "major operational recovery" at the Red Sea terminals of Yanbu and Muajjiz, with 12.5 million barrels loaded onto nine tankers between Saturday and Monday. Oil moving through the Strait of Hormuz averaged 13.2 million bpd over seven days, about 77% of pre-war levels per Kpler. Washington added to the supply side by authorizing the release of up to 40 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve.

The diplomatic track, however, has not moved. Iranian officials have cast doubt on a deal to end hostilities and reopen Hormuz before the US midterm elections, and President Trump rejected Tehran's latest proposal after talks in New York. Note that quoted Brent levels differ across data providers today (Trading Economics ~$96; other sources closed Tuesday near $102.6), reflecting contract and timing differences — treat levels as approximate.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, September 30, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday's slide and today's flat tape tell the same story: the market is now trading physical recovery against diplomatic paralysis. Middle East exports are near 98% of pre-war levels on a 10-day view and Saudi Red Sea loadings are back, but the strait itself is still running well below normal.

My read on today: the easy premium has come out. With the SPR adding barrels and the Saudi pipeline climbing, the burden of proof has shifted to the bulls. Gasoil's decline of roughly 1.4% to $1,423.50/mt fits that: product tightness is easing, though it remains historically elevated.

The bigger picture: risk remains two-sided. A signal from either capital that talks are alive would take another leg out of crude, while a fresh attack on infrastructure or a hard Iranian line ahead of the midterms would rebuild the premium fast. Data caution: price levels differ by provider today, so watch direction over precise levels.

My call: Brent trades $92–$100 today, biased slightly lower while supply keeps returning. The rally has stalled; the next move needs a diplomatic catalyst.
