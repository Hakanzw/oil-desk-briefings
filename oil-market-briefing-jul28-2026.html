⚡ Breaking — Abqaiq Under Fire: World's Largest Crude Processing Plant Suspends Operations as US-Iran Pause Enters Day 2

Houthis Hit Abqaiq on Monday — Brent Finds a Floor at $87 as the Market Weighs a 7 mb/d Processing Shock Against an Iran Ceasefire That Has No Formal Terms

The oil market entered Tuesday absorbing what may be the single most consequential supply-side event since the war began in February. Houthi drones struck Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq oil processing and stabilization facility on Monday, July 27, forcing operations to be suspended. Abqaiq processes approximately 7 million barrels per day — roughly 7% of global oil supply — and its disruption in the 2019 Abqaiq-Khurais attack sent prices surging 15% overnight. Satellite imagery confirmed multiple fires, including impacts on LPG tanks, with the site's flare stack emitting abnormally heavy smoke as Aramco depressurized systems to prevent a catastrophic explosion.

The strike landed in the most complicated possible market context. On Sunday, the US and Iran announced a mutual pause in military strikes — the first in 13 days of near-continuous operations — sending Brent down more than 11% to close Monday at $85.87. The Abqaiq attack then struck during that same Monday session, preventing a fuller collapse and setting an effective floor in the upper-$80s. The full scope of damage remains under active assessment; Aramco has not given a production-impact figure or timeline for restart. With Yanbu separately targeted on Sunday and Jizan refinery struck Friday, Saudi Arabia is now operating under simultaneous threats to its processing infrastructure at Abqaiq, its export corridor at Yanbu, and its Red Sea refining base at Jizan — all while Hormuz runs at ~25 ships per day.

Iran's Foreign Ministry added a new layer of complexity Tuesday morning. Spokesman Esmail Baghaei stated that Tehran "currently have no negotiations with the United States" and that Iran "will never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace." Oman's Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi is leading a separate regional diplomatic push — holding calls with counterparts from Iran, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait and Egypt — explicitly focused on Hormuz traffic management, not a broader ceasefire framework. The channel is Oman-to-Iran, not US-to-Iran, and carries no formal timeline. With the Abqaiq damage assessment running in parallel, Brent's intraday range today is the widest and most binary it has been since the war began.

Supply Shock

Abqaiq Suspended: Houthis Strike World's Largest Crude Processing Plant — Fires and Smoke Confirmed by Satellite

Saudi Aramco suspended operations at the Abqaiq oil processing and stabilization facility on Monday after Houthi drones struck the complex, setting off fires in LPG storage tanks visible from satellite imagery. Abqaiq is the world's largest crude processing facility, handling approximately 7 million barrels per day — more than the combined output of Iraq and the UAE. The 2019 Abqaiq-Khurais attack (significantly smaller in scale) caused a 15% overnight price spike and was described at the time as the most serious threat to global oil supply in decades. Aramco has not published a damage assessment or restart timeline; the full scope of the disruption will emerge over the next 24–48 hours and will set the near-term price floor.

Supply / Infrastructure

Kazakhstan Restarts CPC Oil Exports After Week-Long Black Sea Drone-Attack Shutdown

The Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC) terminal near Novorossiysk resumed operations Monday after a week-long halt triggered by repeated drone attacks on loading vessels. The SEAMAJESTY and MILOS tankers began loading crude from Chevron's Tengizchevroil project. The CPC carries approximately 80% of Kazakhstan's crude exports — roughly 1.8 million barrels per day, or nearly 2% of global supply — making it Europe's second-largest oil pipeline system by volume. The halt had reduced production at the Tengiz field to approximately 1 million barrels per day on its worst day, versus a June average of 2.16 mb/d. Resumption adds a modest bearish overlay to a market already digesting the Iran pause — though the Abqaiq attack more than offsets the incremental supply return.

LNG / Trade

China Resells First US LNG Cargo in a Year — 25% Tariff Makes Import Uneconomic; Signal of Continued Fracture

The first US LNG cargo to reach China since the tariff dispute erupted in early 2025 will not be imported — it will be resold. A tanker from Venture Global's Plaquemines LNG terminal in Louisiana docked at Yangpu, south China, where the cargo was offloaded into bonded storage to avoid Beijing's 25% retaliatory tariff. An empty tanker has since arrived to re-export the gas to another market, likely Europe. The episode underlines that the US-China LNG trade — which had been growing rapidly before the tariff war — remains structurally broken. China is not so short of gas as to pay the 25% premium, and the arbitrage is pointing buyers to Middle Eastern and Australian LNG instead. A resolution of the tariff dispute would immediately redirect multiple cargoes.

EIA Inventory

EIA Weekly Petroleum Report Due Tomorrow — First Inventory Data Into the De-Escalation Window

The EIA's weekly petroleum status report, covering the week ending July 25, is due Tuesday July 29. It will be the first inventory reading taken after the US-Iran pause and the single most important near-term demand signal available. The prior report (week ending July 17) showed crude stocks up 2.0 million barrels to 411.7 mb, roughly 6% below the five-year average, with refinery utilization at 96.1%. The question for tomorrow's print is not supply tightness — which is already established — but demand behavior: whether price-driven destruction at $85–102 per barrel is showing up in refined product drawdowns. A crude draw larger than 3 mb would suggest resilient demand; a build would accelerate the selloff and confirm the IEA's 1 mb/d demand-decline thesis.

IEA Analysis

IEA July Report: Supply Rebounded 4.1 mb/d in June — Abqaiq Attack Threatens to Reverse Those Gains in a Single Blow

The IEA's July 2026 Oil Market Report documented a sharp 4.1 mb/d supply rebound in June to 98.8 mb/d as flows through the Strait of Hormuz partially recovered following the June 18 memorandum of understanding. World output nonetheless remained roughly 9.4 mb/d below pre-war levels. The IEA also projected global oil demand to contract approximately 1 mb/d in 2026 — the first annual decline since COVID-era 2020 — driven by elevated prices and fuel substitution. The Abqaiq suspension, which occurred after the report's data cut-off, potentially erases June's entire supply recovery in a single event: Abqaiq alone processes approximately 7 mb/d, and even a partial multi-day suspension would exceed the 4.1 mb/d June gain. The report's surplus projection for 2027 depends on Hormuz normalization proceeding — a premise the Abqaiq attack now puts in serious doubt.

The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, July 28, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I wrote that the US-Iran pause had created the most two-sided market since the war began — the bearish case real, the bullish tail fat. Overnight, the Houthis answered the question of which side would move first. The Abqaiq strike is not a skirmish; it is a direct assault on the largest single node in the global oil supply chain. The 2019 version of this attack — smaller in scale — caused prices to jump 15% in a single session. That the market is not jumping today is not evidence that the attack is inconsequential. It reflects the gravitational pull of the Iran pause — a massive bearish force — absorbing a massive bullish shock simultaneously.

My read on today: This is one of the most technically difficult sessions of the war to trade. Two forces of similar magnitude are operating in opposite directions at the same time. The Abqaiq shutdown sets a hard supply floor. The pause-with-no-formal-terms sets a soft ceiling. Iran's Foreign Ministry statement — denying any US negotiations — is a meaningful negative for the bearish case: it tells you the pause is tactical (both sides catching breath), not structural (a path to a deal). That means the bullish tail just got fatter, even as the base case remains volatile range-trading.

The bigger picture: Saudi Arabia is now fighting a two-front energy war it did not anticipate in this form. It expected Hormuz pressure. It did not expect its entire downstream value chain — Abqaiq, Yanbu, Jizan — to be simultaneously under kinetic attack. Aramco's spare capacity and infrastructure redundancy was designed for one disruption at a time. The combination of Abqaiq processing suspension, Yanbu export-corridor threat, and Jizan refinery fire constitutes a scenario the market has no good historical analogue for, even including 2019. The IEA's June supply rebound of 4.1 mb/d is at risk of being fully reversed by Abqaiq alone.

My call: Brent holds $84–$95 today, anchored by the Abqaiq floor on the downside and the pause's diplomatic ceiling on the upside. The Abqaiq damage assessment — not the Iran diplomacy — is the variable that moves the tape. Watch for Aramco's statement.
