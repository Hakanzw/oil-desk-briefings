Top Story

⚡ Deadlock Confirmed — Trump Calls Iran's Hormuz Offer "Unacceptable"

Trump Rejects Iran's 7-Day Plan to Reopen Hormuz, Sending Brent Back Above $106 as Deadlock Deepens

The weekend's defining development arrived not from a bomb or a tanker — but from a podium at the United Nations and a few dismissive words outside the White House. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi unveiled at the UN General Assembly on Friday a seven-day roadmap to reopen the Strait of Hormuz: the US would release frozen Iranian funds, lift oil sanctions, and end its naval blockade; Iran, in exchange, would reopen the strait within seven days and begin final-status negotiations by day seven. Markets briefly priced in hope. Trump extinguished it on Saturday, telling reporters the plan "would not be acceptable," adding that Iran was motivated by military losses, not good faith. Brent, which had softened toward $104 on the diplomatic signal, snapped back above $106 Monday morning — recovering the entire Friday slide.

The rejection leaves the Strait of Hormuz closure — in effect since shortly after the war's February 28 start — with no visible off-ramp. Araghchi told Iranian state media that Tehran "will not back down" from its conditions and is awaiting official US counter-proposals through Qatari and Omani mediators. The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump privately expects no major movement before November's midterm elections, treating the current impasse as a position of strength. That timeline, if accurate, implies at least six more weeks of the approximately 20% of seaborne oil that transits Hormuz remaining offline.

Against that backdrop, Monday's session carries a distinct two-sidedness. The primary driver — a perpetuated Hormuz closure — is clearly price-bullish. But simultaneous headwinds are accumulating: the EIA reported a third-consecutive bearish crude build for the week ending September 18 (crude stocks up +2.97 million barrels to 426.4 million), Middle East producers are rerouting record volumes via the Cape and Yanbu, and Trump's proposed 90-day diesel export ban has injected acute policy risk into the middle distillates complex, sending US refiner shares down 6–7% and keeping gasoil cracks volatile.

Trump's 90-Day Diesel Export Ban Would Backfire — Refiners Sell Off 6–7%, Industry Warns

The White House is weighing a 90-day ban on US diesel exports to address retail diesel prices that hit $6.50 per gallon — up from $5.61 a month ago. Energy Secretary Chris Wright suggested the administration may pursue "restrictions" rather than a full ban. But Wood Mackenzie warns that a complete ban would cut US diesel production by up to 750,000 bpd as refiners lose export margins, potentially making the US a net petroleum importer by Q4 2026. Valero and Marathon Petroleum shares fell 7% and 6%, respectively, on the week.

Supply

Middle East Exports Recover to 12.8 mb/d in September — Rerouting Around Hormuz at Scale

Tanker tracking data shows crude exports from key Middle East producers recovered to 12.8 mb/d in September, the highest monthly level since the war began in February, driven by Saudi Arabia and the UAE redirecting flows via the Cape of Good Hope and through Yanbu on the Red Sea. The data demonstrates that producers have meaningfully adapted to the Hormuz closure — though at higher freight cost and longer transit times. Saudi crude loadings, which collapsed from 7.5 mb/d in January to 2.1 mb/d by mid-September, have partially rebounded as pipeline capacity ramps.

EIA: US Crude Stocks Build +2.97 mb — Third Consecutive Miss Against Consensus Draw

The EIA weekly petroleum status report for the week ending September 18 showed US crude inventories rose 2.97 million barrels to 426.4 million barrels, missing consensus expectations for a 600,000-barrel draw by a wide margin. Cushing hub stocks also built by 2.27 mb to 23.7 mb. The persistent builds reflect sluggish refinery runs and weakening industrial demand even as imports edge higher. The next EIA report, covering the week ending September 25, is due Wednesday — the tape's most important scheduled datapoint this week.

The Guru's Verdict — Monday, September 28, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Let me be direct about what happened this weekend: nothing changed, and that is the entire story. Iran made a structured offer at the UN General Assembly — frozen funds unfrozen, sanctions lifted, naval blockade ended, Hormuz open in seven days. Trump said no. The Strait stays closed. Brent bounced on Monday morning. The market is functioning exactly as it should.

My read: The risk premium that looked vulnerable on Friday — when Iran's UNGA proposal briefly implied imminent Hormuz reopening — has been re-established and then some. Trump's dismissal was blunt enough that no sophisticated trader is pricing a deal before November's midterm elections. That's at minimum six weeks of the current supply configuration: roughly 20% of seaborne oil offline through Hormuz, rerouted flows via the Cape adding freight cost and lag, and Saudi Arabia performing aerial damage control over its own energy infrastructure. The floor under crude is the diplomatic calendar.

The bigger picture: Two forces are now pulling directly against each other. The bullish side: confirmed Hormuz impasse, Houthi escalation risk (Riyadh targeted for the first time), and a US diesel export ban that could disrupt global middle distillates supply chains. The bearish side: three consecutive EIA crude builds, September Middle East export recovery to 12.8 mb/d via Cape rerouting, softening industrial demand, and natural gas under pressure from storage surpluses. Neither side is decisively winning — which is itself a message. This is a range market, not a trending one.

My call: Brent holds $103–$112 this week, biased toward the upper half so long as the diplomatic track remains frozen. The key test is Wednesday's EIA number. A fourth straight bearish build caps us below $105 and tests the patient bulls' conviction. A surprise draw — even a modest one — confirms demand absorption is real and opens a run toward $110. The Hormuz deadlock is bullish. The inventory data is bearish. Watch Wednesday for which narrative controls the week.
