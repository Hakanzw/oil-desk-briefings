Top Story

⚡ Sanctions Relief vs. Hormuz Fire — Oil Gives Back Some of Friday's Spike

Brent Slips About 1.9% to ~$107.80 From a $110 Touch as Trump Waives Russia Sanctions to Chase Diesel — But Gulf Crude Flows Have Collapsed to ~8.5 mb/d

Oil retreated on Monday after a week that took Brent to its highest level since May. HDFC Sky reported Brent down about 1.9% to roughly $107.80 and WTI off about 1% near $100.40, while Nation Thailand put Brent at $108.43 (−$1.43) in early Asian trading. Friday's session had pushed the benchmark briefly to $110, its first touch of that level since May 19. The catalyst for the pullback is political rather than physical: on Friday President Trump signed an executive order waiving key sanctions and tariffs on Russia under the Graham Act, following an arrangement with Vladimir Putin to facilitate Russian diesel exports to the United States. Traders read it as the prospect of extra middle-distillate supply ahead of the November midterms, and the move hit gasoil hardest.

The relief is tentative, and the article trail shows why. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the administration could take up to 60 days to assess the economic implications of the waiver, leaving its duration and implementation unclear. Earlier reporting put the initial Russian diesel authorisation at 500,000 tonnes in a month, small against global diesel demand of roughly 29 million barrels per day. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy criticised the decision, arguing it undermines efforts to pressure Moscow. Russia's refining capacity, battered by Ukrainian strikes, may also limit how much it can actually deliver.

Meanwhile the physical Gulf picture keeps deteriorating. Kpler data cited Monday show Gulf crude flows averaging about 8.5 million barrels per day over the seven days before Friday, versus more than 20 million the week before, with at least ten confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Egypt's stated readiness to back Riyadh add a Red Sea dimension that no Russia deal touches. With US distillate stocks at record-low seasonal levels, the sanctions waiver trims the premium at the margin, but the structural supply risk remains firmly in place.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Sanctions / Policy

Trump Signs Executive Order Waiving Russia Sanctions and Tariffs After Putin Diesel Arrangement

Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving key sanctions and tariffs on Russia under the Graham Act, following an agreement with Putin to ease Russian diesel exports to the US, Nation Thailand and HDFC Sky reported. Rubio said the administration could take up to 60 days to assess the economic implications, while Zelenskiy warned that the step would hurt Ukraine and weaken pressure on Moscow. Bloomberg earlier reported that the White House is hunting for ways to tame soaring fuel prices before the midterm election, and that Treasury also issued a waiver for previously sanctioned Russian fuel. For crude, the signal is mildly bearish; for diesel it is the most direct supply-side policy response yet, though volumes remain uncertain.

02
Gulf Supply

Kpler: Gulf Crude Flows Crash to ~8.5 mb/d as Attacks on Commercial Vessels Multiply

A Kpler note cited on Monday shows Gulf crude flows averaged about 8.5 million barrels per day across the seven days before Friday, down from more than 20 million the week before, with at least ten confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels. That follows Reuters-sourced reporting that tanker attacks around Hormuz hit their highest weekly count since the war began, with at least 12 attacks in the week of September 28 to October 5. The US Navy-led JMIC says Iran's Revolutionary Guards continue attacks, drone overflights and VHF hailing to pressure transiting ships. The data underline why the market keeps a large risk premium despite policy headlines elsewhere.

03
Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Fall 8.6M Barrels to 415.6M; Distillates Drop 2.5M to 102.6M

For the week ending October 9, commercial crude stocks fell by about 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million, roughly 3% below the five-year average. Distillate inventories fell 2.5 million barrels to about 102.6 million, around 12% below average, while gasoline stocks rose 3.5 million barrels to about 208.3 million. Refinery utilisation rose to 93.5% from 92.7%, and crude imports averaged 6.0 million bpd, down 791,000 bpd. Total commercial petroleum inventories decreased by 13.4 million barrels. The thin distillate cushion is why diesel cracks stay elevated and why any Russian barrels matter to the US market this winter. The next EIA report is due Wednesday.

04
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds November Output Targets Steady at 31.01 mb/d; Next Meeting Set for November 1

The seven core OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman — kept November production targets unchanged at a virtual meeting on October 4, extending a pause that already applied to October after four straight monthly increases. Combined output stands at 31.01 million bpd. Reuters-syndicated coverage noted that Gulf producers have been pumping well below their targets because of export disruptions tied to the war, and that the conflict has delayed the capacity review that will set 2027 quotas. The group meets again on November 1. With quotas largely academic for constrained Gulf members, the real swing factor is physical export access, not paper targets.

Analysis

Ceasefire Hopes Test Crude, but Goldman Still Pegs the Geopolitical Premium at Just Over $20

FXEmpire's mid-October analysis noted that Trump announced Russian diesel shipments of over 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes afterwards, alongside a claimed Russia–Ukraine pledge to stop hitting energy facilities that Ukraine and Russia had not verified. Gasoil fell 3% to $1,436 on the news. Goldman Sachs raised its December Brent forecast to $90 from $85 and estimates the risk premium at just over $20 per barrel. The piece cautioned that the energy deal excludes Red Sea and Gulf shipping lanes, where Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport and drone hits near Oman's Sohar anchorage keep Brent supported.

06
Natural Gas

Henry Hub Slips to $3.08, a Two-Week Low, as Mild Weather and El Niño Expectations Cap Demand

US natural gas fell 1.16% to $3.08 per MMBtu on Monday, its lowest in about two weeks, according to Trading Economics, though it remains up 8.6% over the past month. Unseasonably mild temperatures are limiting heating and cooling demand, and El Niño expectations weigh on the winter outlook. Storage is 3.9% below year-ago levels but 1.3% above the five-year average, with the surplus shrinking. LNG feedgas to the nine major export terminals averaged 17.6 bcfd in October, down from 17.9 bcfd in September, and Lower 48 output averaged 110.8 bcfd, below the August–September record of 113.3 bcfd. Gas is largely decoupled from the oil risk premium for now.

The Guru's Verdict — Monday, October 19, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

On Saturday I wrote that the risk was skewed upward into Monday's open, with Friday's $109.86 settle leaving the market exposed to a weekend gap. The gap came, but in the other direction: Brent is roughly 1.9% lower near $107.80 as traders digest Trump's Russia sanctions waiver and the promise of Russian diesel. It is a pullback inside an uptrend, not a reversal, and the source trail for the move is thinner than the headline suggests.

My read on today: The waiver is a diesel story far more than a crude story. Gasoil fell about 2.4% to roughly $1,422.63 a tonne from $1,457, which is a bigger move than crude's. Yet the volumes discussed — hundreds of thousands of tonnes against global diesel demand near 29 million barrels a day — ease the squeeze at the margin without rebalancing it, and Russia's damaged refining system may limit deliveries.

The bigger picture: Kpler's Gulf crude flow figure of about 8.5 mb/d, versus more than 20 mb/d the week before, is the number that should keep bears cautious. US distillate stocks at 102.6 million barrels are about 12% below average, OPEC+ is holding targets while Gulf members cannot even reach quota, and spare capacity is thin. Natural gas at $3.08 is a quiet sideshow, driven by mild weather rather than geopolitics.

My call: Brent trades $103–$112 today, with a mild downward bias while the waiver headlines dominate but a quick reversal likely on any new tanker strike. Policy can shave the premium; only reopened shipping lanes can remove it.
