⚡ Wednesday Open — Hormuz Flows Recover, but Iranian Tanker Attacks Keep Brent Pinned Near $100

Brent Edges Up to $101.35 as Gulf Exports Top Pre-War Levels — Yet Iran Escalates Tanker Attacks and Stockpiles Remain "Scarily Thin"

Brent crude rose 0.77% to $101.35 a barrel on Wednesday, according to Trading Economics, extending a market that has spent the week oscillating around the $100 handle. The move came after Brent briefly slipped below $100 on Tuesday, when oil futures fell about 2.5% on news that the G7 would release 100 million barrels from emergency reserves and that Hormuz volumes were normalising. Trading Economics attributed the latest bid to Iran's intensified attacks on tankers, with the UK Maritime Trade Operations reporting nine incidents so far in October. Brent remains roughly 38% above its pre-war level near $73, so the geopolitical premium is still large. Trading Economics quotes differ slightly by provider and contract, so treat the print as indicative rather than a settlement.

The supply picture has improved materially. OilPrice.com, citing Reuters and Kpler data, reported that Hormuz flows reached pre-war levels at 19.5–22.5 million barrels a day over September 27–29, roughly double the level a month earlier, while CBS News said Middle East exports topped pre-war levels at about 19.2 million bpd last week. Shell CEO Wael Sawan put flows at "80-plus percent" of pre-war, a reminder that estimates differ by methodology and by whether Iranian barrels are counted, and the divergence matters for how much more supply the market can still absorb. Trading Economics added that Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline has been restored to 5.8 million bpd, a key bypass if Hormuz or Red Sea routes are disrupted. The gap between these estimates is itself a source of volatility.

The bullish counterweight is inventories and security. Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned that global stockpiles are "scarily thin" and that replenishing them while meeting demand could take up to two years, with nearly 3 billion barrels of supply lost since the war began on February 28. Houthi strikes on Saudi targets and a Saudi-led coalition counteroffensive in Yemen keep the Red Sea in play, and UK diesel at a record 199.52 pence a litre shows the product market remains far tighter than crude. The result: crude is capped by returning barrels and G7 stock releases, but dips are being bought because the buffer is so thin, and any new disruption would land on a market with little room to absorb it, which is why traders are not yet willing to push Brent decisively below $100.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, October 7, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that Brent was a range trade, with Hormuz diplomacy as the swing factor. Today's tape confirms it: Brent is at $101.35, up 0.77%, after dipping below $100 on Tuesday. The market is balancing record-high Gulf flows against a steady drumbeat of tanker attacks, and neither side has yet won, and the range has held for several sessions. Nine incidents in the first week of October is not a trivial number, and the reaction so far has been muted because the barrels are still arriving. That can change quickly if an attack removes a cargo or forces insurers to reprice transit risk, so the calm deserves some scepticism.

My read on today: The crude side of the equation is healing, and the data keep saying so: Kpler flows at pre-war levels, Middle East exports near 19.2 million bpd, Saudi's East-West line at 5.8 million bpd. Add the G7 pledge of 100 million barrels and the case for much higher crude weakens. I would not chase a rally above $104 without verified damage to infrastructure, because the stock release and recovering flows create a cap. The natural gas tape, up to $3.13 on lower output and firmer LNG feedgas, is a side show for crude today.

Diesel is the other story. ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,384.25 per tonne is down 3.87% on the day but still more than double its level of a year ago, and UK retail diesel is at a record. Refinery damage and thin inventories mean product tightness lags crude's recovery, so the diesel crack is likely to stay elevated even if Brent drifts lower. Sharp gasoil drops have repeatedly been followed by rebounds on fresh attacks. Watch the gasoil curve structure as well: a persistently steep backwardation would signal that buyers still fear prompt shortages despite the recovery in crude flows.

The bigger picture: This is a low-inventory market priced as if supply were normal. That asymmetry argues for respecting upside tail risk even in a flat tape. My call: Brent trades $97–$104 today, flat with an upward skew if tanker attacks hit flow data. A credible Hormuz reopening timetable is the only catalyst for a sustained break below $95. Traders should also keep in mind that the week's EIA print and any G7 delivery schedule can move prompt spreads before they move flat price.
