⚡ Weekend Edition — Gulf Oil Exports Climb to Two-Thirds of Pre-War Levels as Iran–Oman Strike Hormuz Revenue Deal; Products Lane Stays Sealed

Goldman Sachs: Gulf Crude Flows Recover to 15–16 mb/d as Iran–Oman Formalise Revenue Deal — IEA Forecasts 1.6 mb/d Demand Destruction in 2026 as Products Blockade Remains Structurally Intact

As crude markets close out a week that sent Brent down more than 7% from Monday's opening — the largest weekly decline since April's war-peak reversal — two competing data points are shaping the weekend narrative. Goldman Sachs published estimates on August 28 showing Gulf crude and product exports have climbed to 15–16 million barrels per day, equivalent to roughly two-thirds of the pre-war baseline of approximately 23 mb/d. The bank attributed the recovery to a rising volume of Strait of Hormuz transits — including dark crossings with AIS transponders disabled — and increasing ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait, noting that market participants are adapting creatively to the conflict environment. Simultaneously, Iran and Oman formalised a Strait of Hormuz revenue-sharing agreement on August 26, with the IRGC framing the arrangement as going materially beyond the cautious joint foreign ministry statement issued the day prior. Tehran stressed the deal does not guarantee immediate full reopening and accused Washington of actively obstructing the negotiation process.

Against the supply recovery signal, the International Energy Agency's August 2026 Oil Market Report delivers a sobering demand-side counterweight: world oil demand is projected to contract by 1.6 mb/d in 2026 as elevated fuel prices and Hormuz-driven product shortages suppress consumption across major import economies. The agency noted global oil supply in July ran 6.3 mb/d below year-ago levels, with renewed hostilities reducing third-quarter supply estimates by a further 370 kb/d. Demand contractions are expected to ease from 4.9 mb/d in Q2 2026 to 2.8 mb/d in Q3, before a return to growth is projected in Q4 — a trajectory conditional on Hormuz normalisation proceeding without relapse. The IEA's demand outlook matters for the medium-term price floor: even a full restoration of Gulf supply must re-enter a market consuming materially less crude than it did a year ago.

The structural feature the week's diplomatic progress has not resolved remains the products lane. The Iran–Oman agreement covers crude vessel governance, maritime security monitoring, and navigation protocol for the Strait — it does not extend to refined product tankers. Europe's diesel, heating oil, and jet fuel supply chain continues to run through an effectively closed channel: US distillate inventories stand 14% below their five-year seasonal average, Low Sulphur Gasoil futures hold at $1,270.25/T, and no products-specific negotiating framework is currently under discussion. The weekend risk is further complicated by Ukraine: following President Putin's reported abandonment of peace talks and signal of military escalation on August 27, European natural gas futures surged 4.34% on Friday, and energy markets enter the weekend with the largest unresolved geopolitical tail since the Hormuz closure itself.

Supply Recovery

Goldman Sachs: Gulf Oil Exports Climb to Two-Thirds of Pre-War Levels — Dark Crossings and Ship-to-Ship Transfers Accelerating

Goldman Sachs estimated on August 28 that Gulf crude and product exports have recovered to 15–16 mb/d, against a pre-conflict baseline of approximately 23 mb/d. The bank attributed the rebound to a rising volume of Strait of Hormuz transits — with over 60% of vessels still disabling AIS transponders — and a growing network of ship-to-ship transfer points beyond the Strait itself. Prices have fallen from above $120/bbl in April to the high $80s as a result, but Goldman cautions that the recovery remains fragile and dependent on continued Iranian restraint within the nascent revenue-sharing framework now established with Oman.

Iran–Oman Formalise Hormuz Revenue Deal — IRGC Goes Beyond Joint Statement; Tehran Accuses US of Obstructing Progress

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced on August 26 that Tehran and Muscat have agreed to divide revenues from Strait of Hormuz vessel transit fees — a step framed as going materially beyond the cautious joint foreign ministry statement of August 25. Analysts view the revenue-sharing agreement as a potential foundation for more durable end-of-conflict talks, but note that its explicit exclusion of refined product tankers limits the near-term market impact on distillate and gasoil markets.

Russia / Ukraine

Ukraine Disrupts 17% of Russian Refining Capacity — Ryazan Struck August 27; Lukoil Volgograd Halted

Reuters confirmed that Ukrainian drone campaigns have disrupted the equivalent of 17% of Russia's total refining capacity, halting approximately 1.1 mb/d of throughput across ten facilities. The Ryazan refinery was struck on August 27; the Lukoil Volgograd facility has been halted since a strike on August 14. The Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Rostov Oblast was struck on August 20 and burned for more than 60 hours. Russia's domestic petrol output fell to 75,000–80,000 tonnes per day in July against demand of 115,000–120,000 tonnes per day, sustaining a nationwide fuel deficit that now affects over 90% of Russian administrative regions.

EIA Week Ending August 21: Crude +0.1 mb; Gasoline −3.2 mb; Distillates 14% Below Average; SPR at 44-Year Low

The Guru's Verdict — Saturday, August 29, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

The week ending August 28 delivered the largest weekly crude decline since April's post-ceasefire unwind, and for broadly the same reason: diplomatic progress on the Strait of Hormuz — this time a formalised revenue-sharing agreement between Iran and Oman — gave the market permission to sell the geopolitical premium. Goldman Sachs has put a number on the recovery: Gulf exports now run at 15–16 mb/d, two-thirds of the pre-war baseline, as dark crossings multiply and ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait build into a parallel routing network. That recovery, if it continues at the current pace, is structurally bearish for headline crude prices, and the tape spent most of the week expressing precisely that thesis.

My read on the weekend: Friday's reversal — Brent recovering from $86.93 to $88.22 on the Putin escalation signal — is the market telling you that the Hormuz-bearish thesis has a live counterweight it cannot fully suppress. Three Kremlin sources telling Bloomberg that Putin has abandoned peace talks and is preparing military escalation is not a routine intelligence leak — it is the kind of signal that precedes major market-moving events, and the 4.34% spike in European natural gas on the same day confirms that at least one major benchmark is taking it seriously. Crude held back because the week's Hormuz narrative dominated positioning. Monday's open will reveal which force the market decides to price into September.

The bigger picture: The IEA's 1.6 mb/d demand destruction forecast is the variable that most changes the medium-term calculus. A recovering supply base — Goldman's 15–16 mb/d and climbing — re-entering a market consuming materially less oil than 2025 compresses the price floor more than a surface supply-return headline implies. The products structural dislocation is the counterweight that the crude-bearish narrative consistently undervalues: distillates 14% below seasonal average, ULS1! at $1,270/T, and zero products-lane diplomatic progress mean the refining and distribution economics of the global oil market remain severely distorted even as the crude corridor advances. This creates a bifurcated market: crude bearish on Hormuz diplomacy, products structurally bullish on the products blockade — and gasoil remains the cleaner directional expression of the supply dislocation that is not going away until Iran agrees to negotiate a separate products framework.

My call for Monday's open: Brent trades in the $86–$92 range, with the intraday bias determined entirely by weekend Ukraine headlines. A quiet weekend allows Hormuz bearishness to reassert and Brent tests the $86–$87 zone into Tuesday. A confirmed Putin military escalation order reprices a move toward $90–$92 before European markets have finished their morning session. Watch the products trade: ULS1! will not correct meaningfully until Iran agrees to open a products-specific negotiating channel — and that conversation has not started.
