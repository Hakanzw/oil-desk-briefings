🔥 Embargo Goes Kinetic: Houthis Fire Ballistic Missiles and Drones at Saudi Tankers Encelia and Layla

Houthis Strike Two Saudi Oil Tankers With Missiles and Drones in First Kinetic Enforcement of Maritime Embargo; Brent Surges to $96; Rubio Declares Hormuz Ceasefire MOU 'Effectively Dead' as Trump Threatens to Bomb Iranian Bridges and Power Plants

The Houthi maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia crossed from declaration to detonation on Thursday. Yemen's Iran-backed rebels, operating three days after announcing a full maritime blockade on Saudi ports, struck the oil tankers Encelia and Layla using ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones — the first direct kinetic enforcement action under the embargo. Both vessels were located approximately 70 nautical miles southwest of Al Shuqaiq, Saudi Arabia, when fires broke out aboard each ship. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea stated the tankers were targeted for 'violating the embargo imposed by the group's armed forces' on vessels calling at Saudi ports. No casualties were initially reported, but the strikes mark a decisive qualitative shift: the embargo is no longer a deterrent or a transit disruption — it is an active combat operation against Saudi crude exports. At least nine tankers had already altered their Red Sea routes following Monday's blockade declaration; Thursday's missile strikes will sharply extend that number.

The tanker attacks landed on a tape already stretched by diplomatic collapse. Secretary of State Marco Rubio declared Wednesday that Iran had 'effectively killed' the memorandum of understanding meant to keep the Strait of Hormuz open, stating that Tehran was 'not being serious' about reaching an agreement with Washington — while maintaining the US remained 'committed to diplomacy.' The same day, President Trump threatened to bomb Iranian bridges and power plants if Iranian forces continued attacking commercial ships transiting Hormuz. US forces conducted their 11th consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military facilities. The convergence of Rubio's diplomatic death sentence on the MOU and Trump's infrastructure pledge removes the last plausible near-term off-ramp. Brent crude surged 2.5% to near $96 per barrel, its highest level in six weeks, while WTI rose 1.5% above $88 — the fifth consecutive daily gain for both benchmarks.

Markets also processed Wednesday's EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report, which confirmed a 2.0-million-barrel build in commercial crude stocks for the week ending July 17 — narrowly below the API's preliminary 2.6-million-barrel estimate, but still the first inventory gain in approximately 14 consecutive weeks. Commercial inventories stand at 411.7 million barrels, 6% below the five-year average, as refineries ran at a robust 96.1% utilization. The build generated zero sustained bearish pressure. Traders correctly diagnosed it as a temporal artifact — cargoes that completed transit before the Hormuz blockade fully reimposed and the Red Sea embargo began — rather than evidence of genuine supply easing. A market absorbing the first confirmed kinetic strikes on Saudi oil tankers and the official US declaration that the Hormuz ceasefire framework is dead had no appetite to sell on an inventory number that is, by definition, backward-looking.

EIA / Inventories

EIA Confirms 2.0mb Build for Week Ending July 17 — 411.7mb Total; Refinery Utilization at 96.1%; Build Has No Market Effect

The EIA's Weekly Petroleum Status Report confirmed a 2.0-million-barrel increase in commercial crude stocks for the week ending July 17 — slightly below the API's preliminary 2.6-million-barrel figure and the first build in approximately 14 weeks. Commercial inventories stand at 411.7 million barrels, 6% below the five-year average. Gasoline stocks rose 0.8 million barrels (7% below average), distillates added 1.4 million barrels (10% below average), and total commercial petroleum inventories gained 11.6 million barrels. Refinery utilization ran at 96.1%, processing 17.1 million barrels per day. The build generated no lasting bearish response: analysts are correctly reading it as a timing artifact rather than supply normalization, with the full impact of the Hormuz blockade and Houthi Red Sea embargo still feeding through to subsequent weeks' counts.

EU 21st Sanctions Package Fails for Fourth Day — $44.10 Russian Oil Price Cap Hits July 23 Deadline Unresolved

European Union member states failed to agree on the 21st Russia sanctions package for the fourth consecutive day, leaving the $44.10-per-barrel price cap on Russian crude to reach its July 23 deadline without a resolution. The cap had been preserved by emergency extension from its original July 15 expiry. Absent a deal by today, the cap rises automatically to $58 per barrel — a threshold Moscow would welcome as Brent trades near $96. The main sticking points remain restrictions on Russian LNG transport and measures targeting Austria's Raiffeisen Bank International. A cap rising to $58 would provide Russia meaningful additional oil revenue headroom, reducing the effectiveness of the West's primary energy-linked economic pressure tool at the worst possible moment for supply tightness.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, July 23, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Wednesday's briefing said the EIA build would matter only if it was large enough to overwhelm the geopolitical premium. The EIA confirmed a 2.0-million-barrel build — and the market responded by pushing Brent to $96. The Houthi embargo, which three days ago I described as the week's defining price risk, has now fired ballistic missiles into two Saudi oil tankers. The embargo is no longer theoretical. It is kinetic, and the Red Sea is an active combat zone for Saudi crude exports.

The Rubio statement is the most consequential diplomatic development of the crisis since the ceasefire collapsed in early July. By declaring the Hormuz MOU 'effectively dead' — while Trump simultaneously threatened to bomb Iranian bridges and power plants — Washington has communicated that the conflict is entering a qualitatively more destructive phase with no functional diplomatic ceiling. This is the first time in five months of war that both the diplomatic and military escalation signals have pointed in the same direction simultaneously. The market priced that signal immediately: five consecutive sessions up, a six-week high above $95, and WTI above $88 for the first time since mid-June.

The EIA build deserves a structural interpretation because it will recur and continue to look confusing. A 2.0-million-barrel build happens when blocked cargoes complete transit before their disruption window fully opens — it does not mean the supply crisis is easing. Historically, naval blockade and maritime embargo scenarios produce initial builds followed by sharp sequential draws as rerouted cargoes fail to arrive on schedule. I expect the next 3–4 weekly EIA reports to confirm that dynamic with increasing severity as the Houthi kinetic embargo closes the Red Sea transit window for Saudi crude and the Hormuz blockade prevents Iranian barrels from reaching the market.

My call for today: Brent trades $93–$100, with a strong bias toward the upper half of that range. The twin triggers of Rubio's diplomatic death sentence on the Hormuz MOU and the first confirmed Houthi missile strikes on Saudi oil tankers put $100 Brent within reach of any single additional escalation event. A Pickaxe Mountain strike — which Trump has now publicly telegraphed multiple times and which Rubio's MOU declaration removes the most obvious reason to delay — is the highest-probability single catalyst for a breach of the century mark. $100 oil is now a question of timing, not direction, unless a credible diplomatic reversal appears in the next 24 hours. None is visible.
