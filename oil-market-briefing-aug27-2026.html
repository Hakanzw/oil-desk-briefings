⚡ Hormuz — Iran–Oman Revenue-Sharing Deal Seals Fourth Consecutive Day of Brent Losses

Brent Falls to $86.93 as Iran and Oman Strike Hormuz Revenue-Sharing Deal — But Products Lane Stays Sealed, SPR Hits 44-Year Low, and Ukraine Burns Another Russian Refinery

A fourth consecutive session of crude losses — each one driven by diplomatic progress rather than genuine supply restoration — confirmed Thursday that the market is in the late stages of unwinding the Hormuz risk premium. Iran's military announced a revenue-sharing agreement with Oman covering control, navigation governance, and revenues generated by the Strait of Hormuz. Under the framework, Iran assumes authority over vessel traffic monitoring, maritime security, and environmental protection within the strait. Critically, the deal explicitly bans American and Israeli vessels and imposes fines of up to 20% of cargo value on violators — setting a structural constraint on US-flagged tonnage that will persist regardless of how quickly a broader diplomatic settlement takes shape. Tehran cautioned that full reopening of the Strait remains 'subject to other conditions,' specifically US compensation for war damages and an end to Washington's blockade of Iranian ports — language that preserves Iran's ability to slow-walk normalisation while claiming diplomatic momentum. Brent fell 1.03% to $86.93, extending a four-session losing streak that has erased more than six dollars since last Monday.

The session's clearest evidence that the geopolitical risk premium has not fully drained came from Ukraine, not the Gulf. Ukrainian forces struck the Lukoil-Nizhegorodnefteorgsintez refinery in Kstovo, Nizhny Novgorod Oblast on the night of August 25–26, igniting a large fire at one of Russia's largest refineries — a facility that processes approximately 17 million tonnes of crude per year and accounts for roughly 11% of Russia's domestic petrol production. The attack is at least the fifth time Kstovo has been hit in 2026 alone; Ukraine has now struck Russian oil-refining infrastructure nearly 200 times this year. Separately, three sources close to the Kremlin told Bloomberg that Putin is preparing to escalate military operations in Ukraine after concluding peace negotiations have reached a dead end. Crude futures briefly spiked on the Wednesday escalation report before the Iranian-Omani deal news reversed those gains — a session-within-a-session that illustrated the two counterforce trades competing for the tape in real time.

The EIA's weekly petroleum status report covering the week ending August 21 — released today — added complexity to the crude-bearish narrative. Commercial crude inventories rose just 0.1 million barrels, far below the 4.2 million barrel build flagged by the American Petroleum Institute's preliminary estimate, while gasoline inventories drew a larger-than-expected 3.2 million barrels — signalling demand resilience at lower price levels. Distillate inventories built 2.2 million barrels, a reversal of last week's draw, but remain 14% below their five-year seasonal average — the enduring structural fingerprint of zero refined product transit through Hormuz. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell a further 3.7 million barrels to 289.7 million barrels, its lowest level since November 1982. With an estimated 103 million barrels of SPR inventory operationally constrained and unavailable for drawdown, Washington is approaching a potential Hormuz relapse with an emergency buffer that is effectively at a 44-year low.

OPEC+

OPEC+ Completes Full Rollback of 2023 Voluntary Cuts — September 188k bpd Hike Was the Final Step; Quotas to Hold Through Year-End

The August 2 decision by seven core OPEC+ members to approve a 188,000 barrel-per-day September production increase completed the reversal of all approximately 3.5 million bpd in voluntary cuts introduced in 2023. Saudi Arabia and Russia each led the final round among the core seven: Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman. Delegates told Bloomberg the group plans to hold production quotas steady through the end of 2026 following this final increase — though that guidance remains conditional on market conditions. Many members remain unable to actually reach their allotted production levels due to technical constraints, meaning the headline rollback overstates the actual physical supply impact.

Energy Security

US Strategic Petroleum Reserve Falls to 289.7 mb — Below 300 mb for First Time; 103 mb Operationally Constrained

The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen below 300 million barrels for the first time in four decades, standing at 289.7 million barrels — its lowest level since November 1982. President Trump ordered a 172-million-barrel release in March as part of a coordinated IEA emergency response to the Hormuz closure. Infrastructure constraints mean approximately 103 million barrels of current SPR inventory are not available for drawdown, reducing the effective usable buffer to around 187 million barrels. Energy security analysts warn that the SPR is approaching an operational floor precisely as the Hormuz diplomatic track advances — removing a critical emergency response tool just as the next potential supply shock risk is being reassessed.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, August 27, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Four sessions. More than six dollars off last Monday's close. The Iran–Oman revenue-sharing agreement is the most concrete governance framework the Strait of Hormuz has seen since February, and the market is rationally repricing the risk premium — not all the way out, but in the direction of 'this is ending, not escalating.' The deal's explicit ban on American and Israeli vessels is the detail that separates it from prior diplomatic signals: this is not another back-channel conversation, it is a formal governance structure for a waterway that carries 20% of the world's seaborne oil — one that Iran has now formalised with legal and financial mechanisms to police.

My read on today: The dominant trade remains crude-bearish as the Hormuz premium deflates. But the session's most important moment was not the Iranian-Omani announcement — it was the brief spike on Putin's escalation report before the Hormuz news reversed it. That sequence tells you exactly how positioned the market is: the Ukraine tail risk is live, it moved the tape, and it reversed only because an even bigger bearish story landed within hours. A session without that Hormuz offset would have looked very different. The Kstovo refinery strike confirms that the supply-disruption risk in the Europe-facing barrel market remains material even as the Gulf-focused risk eases.

The bigger picture: Three structural questions now define the medium-term range. First, does the Iran–Oman deal extend to product tankers — because without that extension, distillate deficits and diesel crack spreads remain the dominant price signal in the products complex, and the structural split between crude-bearish and products-bullish persists as a defining market feature. Second, does Putin escalate in Ukraine, and if so, does it reach energy infrastructure or Atlantic shipping lanes? Third, does the SPR — now at a 44-year low with 103 million barrels operationally constrained — get replenished before the next supply shock, or does Washington enter a potential Hormuz relapse with an emergency buffer effectively at its floor?

My call: Brent holds $84–$92 today, with the intraday bias firmly lower on continued Hormuz premium unwind. The Ukraine tail is real, the SPR is at generational lows, and a products deal remains unannounced — the downside is capped more by structural risk than by diplomatic optimism, and any one of those three question marks can reprice this market $6 in either direction within a single session.
