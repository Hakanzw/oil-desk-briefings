Top Story

⚡ Peace Hopes vs. Physical Disruption — Brent Slips Again, Gasoil Sheds Nearly 8%

Brent Slips to $91.25 and Gasoil Drops 7.8% to $1,236.50 as Iran and Ukraine Diplomacy Eases Supply Fears, but Hormuz Flows Remain Far Below Normal

Crude gave back part of Wednesday's rebound. Brent (Jan '27) is quoted at $91.25 per barrel, down 1.63% or $1.51, while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) fell far harder, down 7.81% or $104.75 to $1,236.50 per tonne. Separate reports put WTI near $85.6, down about 1.1%. The move came as traders weighed signs of diplomatic progress on both the Iran and Russia–Ukraine fronts, plus a US–China summit scheduled for next week that investors hope keeps global tensions contained. The gasoil drop is notably larger than crude's, suggesting the middle-distillate risk premium built up during Wednesday's heating oil spike (+4.1% per Oilprice) is being unwound quickly.

The diplomatic picture is mixed rather than resolved. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran "would not allow any enriched nuclear material to leave the country" and warned of consequences for further US strikes, while Washington insists nuclear issues belong in any initial negotiations on ending the eight-month-old conflict. On Wednesday Rezaei had called reports of a Qatar meeting "pure lies," and Secretary of State Marco Rubio rejected Iran's reparations demand. Meanwhile Russian and Ukrainian negotiators agreed a major prisoner exchange after two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, though territorial differences remain wide. Any breakthrough there could eventually ease restrictions on Russian oil supply.

Physical supply is recovering through workarounds, not peace. OPEC crude output rose nearly 2 million bpd in October to about 27.2 million bpd, against roughly 28.6 million bpd pre-war, led by Saudi Arabia at about 10.6 million bpd (from about 8.4 million in September), per a WSJ report relayed by investingLive. Yet Kpler counts confirmed Hormuz transits at only about 4 million bpd last week versus roughly 17 million before the war (preliminary). The API reported a 9.0 million barrel US crude build for the week ended November 6; official EIA data were due Thursday after the Veterans Day delay, and we could not retrieve them at press time. Rising stocks and recovering OPEC barrels cap rallies, but Hormuz keeps a floor under prices.

Markets

Crude Falls 1% as Iran and Ukraine Talks Ease Supply Fears

Brent futures fell about 1.2% to around $91.7 and WTI about 1.1% to $85.6 in early Thursday trading, according to HDFC Sky's summary of Oilprice reporting, after both benchmarks rebounded Wednesday from two-month lows. Traders cited hopes of diplomatic progress to end the Middle East conflict and a US–China summit next week. Market strategists cautioned that the geopolitical situation does not yet justify expecting a substantial collapse in prices, since continued Hormuz shipment disruption prevents a deeper fall. Our ICE-based quote of $91.25 is slightly lower still. For crude and diesel users, the message is that the market is trading headlines day to day, with the $90 area acting as a pivot rather than a floor.

02
OPEC / Supply

OPEC Output Jumps Nearly 2 Million bpd Toward Pre-War Levels as Saudi Production Rebounds

OPEC crude output rose by nearly 2 million bpd in October to about 27.2 million bpd, versus roughly 28.6 million bpd in February before the Iran war began, according to a WSJ report relayed by investingLive. Saudi output climbed to about 10.6 million bpd from about 8.4 million in September, with exports around 7 million bpd per Kpler, above the 2025 average of 6.2 million. Aramco cut its December Arab Light price to Asia by $2 to a $7 discount to Oman/Dubai. OPEC+ agreed earlier this month to hold output steady in December, and OPEC left demand growth forecasts unchanged at 180,000 bpd this year and 2.55 million bpd next. This recovery takes the edge off shortage fears and is bearish for Brent.

03
Hormuz

Hormuz Transits Still Around 4 Million bpd Versus 17 Million Before the War

Kpler data cited by investingLive show confirmed Hormuz transits averaged about 4 million bpd over the past week, compared with roughly 17 million bpd pre-conflict, though the figures are preliminary. The article describes the supply recovery as relying on workarounds, not on peace. Oilprice added that the Washington Institute counted 37 ships attacked in October, the most in any month of the war, and LSEG Oil Research said any rise in traffic depends on mine clearing, insurance costs and shipowners' willingness to take risk. Even a deal would not restore Gulf flows quickly. This gap between recovering production and constrained exports is why Brent has not broken decisively below $90.

Geopolitics

Brent Jumps Nearly 3% as Iran Denies Any Talks With Washington

On Wednesday, Brent rose nearly 3% intraday to $93.90 and WTI nearly 2% to $87.98, while heating oil jumped 4.1%, Oilprice reported. Iran's Mohsen Rezaei denied talks with Washington and called reports of a Qatar meeting "pure lies"; President Masoud Pezeshkian accused the US of diplomacy through threats, and Rubio rejected reparations. UBS's Giovanni Staunovo cited "fading hopes of an imminent de-escalation," while Bloomberg's Javier Blas called Brent near $90 "an oddity." Since the start of November Brent had lost about 21% and WTI about 17%. Allianz Trade puts Gulf physical damage above $200 billion. The swing from Wednesday's high to Thursday's slip shows how headline-driven the tape is.

Inventories

API Reports 9-Million-Barrel US Crude Build; EIA Data Delayed to Thursday

The API reported a 9.0 million barrel build in US crude inventories for the week ended November 6, a larger-than-expected increase according to market sources, and traders largely looked past it on Wednesday. The official EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report was delayed a day by the Veterans Day federal closure, with release set for Thursday at 12:00 p.m. ET. We could not retrieve the figures at press time. For context, the previous EIA report (week ended October 30) showed crude down 865,000 barrels to about 409.8 million, with gasoline and distillate stocks also lower, per Reuters via BOE Report. A confirmed large build would weigh on WTI, while the distillate line matters most for gasoil.

06
Russia / Diesel

Russia–Ukraine Agree Prisoner Swap; Drone Strikes Still Disrupt ~30% of Russian Refining

Russian and Ukrainian negotiators agreed a major prisoner exchange after two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, though significant differences remain on territory, and President Zelenskiy hopes for concrete results before year-end, per HDFC Sky. Oilprice separately reported that a Ukrainian drone strike sparked a fire at Russia's Ust-Luga export hub, and that attacks have disrupted about 30% of Russian refining capacity since late October. A breakthrough could reduce risk premiums, especially if it eases restrictions on Russian oil, but until then diesel and gasoil supply risk persists. Gasoil's 7.8% drop today looks like a reaction to de-escalation hopes and may overshoot if strikes continue.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, November 12, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday's bounce proved fragile. With Brent at $91.25, down 1.63%, the market is again leaning on diplomatic optimism, from Iran, from Abu Dhabi, and from next week's US–China summit. But the evidence for a physical normalization is thin: Hormuz transits are about a quarter of pre-war levels, and nothing on the nuclear question has moved. This is a tape trading sentiment faster than barrels.

My read on today: The supply side is quietly turning bearish. OPEC produced about 27.2 million bpd in October, Saudi Arabia is back above 10 million bpd, and the API shows a 9.0 million barrel US build. Those are real barrels. What keeps Brent from slipping to the low $80s is the export bottleneck, not production, which is why a Hormuz incident still moves prices by several dollars.

On gasoil: A 7.8% single-day fall to $1,236.50 per tonne is large by any standard and follows Wednesday's 4.1% heating oil spike. I read it as partly a positioning flush. Distillate fundamentals have not changed: Russian refining is still about 30% disrupted, and US distillate stocks were drawing in the last EIA report we could confirm. Expect volatile cracks, with the EIA distillate line the key catalyst.

My call: Brent trades $88–$94 today, biased modestly lower on the EIA build and OPEC supply, but with upside tails from any tanker attack or Iranian hardening. Peace on paper keeps shaving the premium; the Strait keeps it from disappearing. Note: we could not verify a natural gas quote or the official EIA figures at press time.
