Top Story

Strait of Hormuz — Month Three of the Collapsed Ceasefire

Brent Steadies at $91.77 as Hormuz Attacks Persist and Diplomacy Stalls — Eight Vessels Struck This Month

Crude oil markets found a narrow equilibrium Thursday as Brent held near $91.77 per barrel and WTI clung to $84.43, both essentially flat after a five-session, 5%-plus rally driven by escalating tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. The pause is less a sign of market confidence than of exhaustion: buyers absorbed the war-premium repricing through last week; sellers are reluctant to push lower with the strait still under fire. Eight vessel strikes have been confirmed in August alone — including attacks on Qatari LNG tankers and Saudi crude carriers — since Iran's IRGC collapsed the June ceasefire memorandum on July 8, when fresh projectile strikes ended six weeks of fragile diplomacy.

The strategic picture has barely moved. Oil throughput through the Strait of Hormuz, which carried some 21.6 million barrels per day before the February 28 conflict began, has fallen to an estimated 4.9 million bpd — a loss the IEA declared the "largest disruption in the history of the global oil market," surpassing even the 1970s oil crisis. Alternative tanker routes via the Cape of Good Hope and overland pipelines have partially offset the shortfall, but freight costs remain elevated and delivery times have stretched, keeping a structural premium embedded in forward curves.

Against this backdrop, OPEC+ completed the rollback of its 2023-era voluntary cuts on August 2, approving a 188,000 bpd increase for September — the sixth consecutive monthly hike. The move closes out the 1.65 million bpd programme but leaves 2 million bpd of older 2022 cuts intact through end-2026. Analysts broadly view the September increase as symbolic: the Gulf producers most affected by the Hormuz closure cannot easily deliver the extra barrels anyway, and actual export constraints remain geopolitical rather than policy-driven. OPEC+'s next review is September 6.

Geopolitics

Ships Attacked in Hormuz — Peace Talks Remain at Impasse as Iran Demands Transit Fees

Iran's IRGC has struck 16 of 18 UKMTO-reported vessels along shipping lanes since July 6, the majority along the Southern Omani route. Key sticking points blocking fresh ceasefire talks include Iran's insistence on collecting transit fees from commercial shipping and its demand for control over shipping lane designation — terms rejected outright by the US and Saudi Arabia. Iran's foreign minister has warned negotiations cannot proceed "if threats continue," a statement widely read as diplomatic cover for continued pressure.

A US government assessment circulated August 11 projects that Strait of Hormuz disruptions will suppress global oil flows by approximately 600,000 bpd through the end of 2027, even under optimistic diplomatic scenarios. OPEC+

OPEC+ Completes Voluntary-Cut Rollback With September Hike — But Gulf Exports Still Constrained

The seven-nation OPEC+ core approved a 188,000 bpd output increase for September at its August 2 virtual meeting, closing out the 1.65 million bpd of voluntary cuts first implemented in April 2023. Saudi Arabia and Russia each absorb the largest share. Analysts caution the hike is largely symbolic: actual Gulf export capacity remains pinned by the Hormuz closure and damaged infrastructure, meaning the quota increase has minimal physical impact on global supply for now.

IEA Report

IEA: Hormuz Crisis Is Largest Disruption in History of Global Oil Markets — 8.3 mb/d Still Shut In

The IEA's August 2026 Oil Market Report declared the Strait of Hormuz crisis the worst supply disruption in the history of global oil markets, exceeding even the 1973 Arab oil embargo in scale. Global oil supply is forecast to fall 4.3 mb/d for full-year 2026 to 102 mb/d, with 8.3 mb/d of Gulf output still shut in. The global oil balance shows a deficit of 1.8 mb/d in Q3 2026, supporting prices well above pre-conflict levels despite demand destruction.

US equity markets fell sharply August 16 as Brent climbed toward $93 on fresh Iranian threats, with the Dow shedding more than 270 points. Natural Gas

EIA Slashes Henry Hub Forecast to $3.44 — Record US Gas Production Swamps LNG Demand Loss

The EIA's August Short-Term Energy Outlook cut its full-year 2026 Henry Hub average forecast to $3.44/MMBtu from $3.67 (July estimate) and trimmed its 2027 forecast to $3.31 from $3.49. The agency attributed the revision to record domestic gas production offsetting the loss of LNG feedgas demand caused by the Hormuz closure — Qatari and Gulf LNG exports have collapsed, but US production has surged to fill domestic needs. Pre-winter gas inventories are on pace for their highest levels since 2016.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, August 20, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Brent's five-session rally from the low $87s to nearly $93.60 before settling here at $91.77 tells you everything about where the market's center of gravity sits: not low enough to price a resolution, not high enough to price a full-blown supply catastrophe. The strait is still mostly closed, the ceasefire is still collapsed, and the IEA is calling this the worst disruption in energy market history — yet Brent is 27% below its March peak. Markets have found a weary equilibrium.

My read on today: The consolidation phase is healthy but fragile. The rally exhausted itself into $93 resistance; now the tape waits for a directional catalyst. There are two plausible ones: a credible diplomatic signal — even a procedural one — would trigger a rapid $4–6 sell-off as traders front-run a resolution trade. A new mass-casualty vessel attack or closure of the Omani alternative route would push crude back toward $95–$97 before the week ends.

The structural backdrop: OPEC+'s completed rollback matters less than it sounds. The Gulf producers pressing the extra barrels are largely the same ones unable to export through Hormuz. What actually matters is the IEA's Q3 deficit figure of 1.8 mb/d — that structural shortfall is the floor under this market, keeping crude from retreating to the low $80s without a physical supply improvement.

My call: Brent holds $88–$94 today, bias sideways-to-cautiously-lower in the absence of fresh escalation. The Hormuz premium is deeply embedded — but every diplomatic headline carries the risk of a fast, sharp unwind.
