Top Story

⚡ Tuesday Open — Gulf Crude Flows Heal, but Diesel and Hormuz Politics Keep the Floor Near $100

Brent Steadies Near $100.78 as Gulf Crude Shipments Recover to 98% of Pre-War Levels — Yet Refined Products Lag and Iran Keeps Hormuz Conditional

Brent crude edged up 0.46% to $100.78 a barrel on Tuesday, according to Trading Economics, recovering part of Monday's fade from the $103.06 intraday high that followed Houthi claims of missile and drone strikes on Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais. Another aggregator showed Brent near $100.7, so quotes differ by a few cents depending on provider and contract. The tape is range-bound: geopolitical headlines add a bid, while a steady recovery in physical flows and Saudi Arabia's Asia price cut hold the market back from retesting $103. Brent remains roughly 38% above its pre-war level near $73.

The supply story is improving at the crude level. Trading Economics cited analysis that Middle East crude shipments have rebounded to 17.5 million barrels a day, or 98% of pre-war levels. Iraq is hiring additional vessels and moved 2 million barrels through Hormuz on a single VLCC, while Kuwait is producing at about 75% of pre-war rates. Other tracking cited by Hormuz coverage puts the seven-day average of crude cargoes through the strait nearer 10.3 million bpd, around three-quarters of normal, so the precise recovery depends on the measure used and the route counted.

Refined products are the weak link. Diesel and gasoline flows are only about 3 million bpd, or 58% of pre-war output, because refineries in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, the UAE and Iraq remain offline after missile strikes. US retail diesel hit a record $6.53 a gallon. Politically, Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says Hormuz stays closed until Washington meets seven conditions drawn from the June memorandum, while Foreign Minister Araghchi suggests reopening within seven days if they are met. The result is a market where crude is loosening faster than diesel, and gasoil carries the scarcity premium.

Oil Slips as Saudi Cuts Asia Price to −$5; Sparta Warns Infrastructure Remains Vulnerable

Iran Says Hormuz Stays Closed Until US Meets Seven Conditions; Pezeshkian Rules Out US Talks

Iran International's live coverage reported that President Masoud Pezeshkian ruled out talks with the US as a third tanker in two days was hit near Hormuz. Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf said the strait stays closed until Washington meets seven conditions based on the June memorandum, while Foreign Minister Araghchi indicated reopening could come within seven days if they are met, and Deputy FM Gharibabadi said Tehran is evaluating an American response. The mixed signals keep a security premium in freight and insurance even as crude volumes recover, and they make a sudden policy shift a two-way risk for prices.

EIA: Crude Up 922,000 bbl to 427.3M, Distillates Down 2.3M to 14% Below Average

In the latest EIA report, for the week ended September 25, US commercial crude inventories rose 922,000 barrels to 427.3 million, 2% above the five-year average. Distillate stocks fell 2.3 million barrels to 105.2 million, 14% below the five-year norm, gasoline fell 1.7 million to 204.4 million and refinery utilisation dropped 1.5 points to 92.5%, according to BOE Report. A further distillate draw would reinforce the diesel premium and the record $6.53 retail price, while a crude build would match the bearish read from recovering Gulf flows.

The Guru's Verdict — Tuesday, October 6, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that the Saudi price cut was the more informative datapoint than the Houthi claim, and Tuesday's tape supports that. Brent is at $100.78, up a modest 0.46%, and there has been no follow-through on the Aramco headline. The market is behaving as though crude supply is recovering, with Gulf shipments at 98% of pre-war levels per Trading Economics, while security risk is priced as an option and not as lost barrels.

My read on today: The crude market is rangebound between $98 and $103 and needs a catalyst. The strongest candidate is Iran: Araghchi's seven-day reopening comment and Ghalibaf's seven conditions pull in opposite directions. I treat a formal reopening as the main downside trigger, since it would let product flows catch up, and a further tanker incident as the main upside trigger. Until one of them arrives, I expect chop.

The diesel angle: Low Sulphur Gasoil at $1,374.75 a tonne is up 2.14% on the day but down 1.6% over five days, so this looks like a bounce, not a trend change. Products are at 58% of pre-war flows, US diesel is a record $6.53 and US distillates are 14% below average. The diesel crack, not the crude price, is where scarcity is expressed, and Wednesday's EIA print is the next test.

My call: Brent trades $97–$104 today, with a flat bias. Crude is healing, products are not, and Hormuz diplomacy decides which one wins. A clean reopening timetable would take Brent toward $95, while confirmed damage to Saudi infrastructure would reopen $106–$108.
