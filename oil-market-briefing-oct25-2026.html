Top Story

⚡ Sunday Brief — Houthi Campaign Against Saudi Arabia Widens as Brent Sits at a Six-Month High Into Monday's Open

Houthis Claim Fresh Strikes on Riyadh Airport, Yanbu and the East-West Pipeline as Brent Enters the Weekend Gap at $116.72

Oil markets are shut on Sunday, so the price reference remains Friday's close: Brent for December delivery settled at $116.72 a barrel, up 3.1%, the highest settlement since April 29 and a 6.2% gain on the week, while WTI finished at $103.27. The news flow over the weekend has done nothing to cool that tape. Reuters, via France 24, reports that Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed attacks on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, an Aramco facility at Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery, crude pumping stations and the East-West Pipeline, plus airports at Dammam and Najran and an airbase at Khamis Mushait. Saudi authorities gave no immediate confirmation, and it remains unclear whether any target was actually hit.

What can be verified is still significant. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Yanbu and Taif on Saturday, and Saudi Civil Defense briefly warned residents in Taif, Yanbu, Mecca and Jeddah before declaring the threat over about ten minutes later. Yemen's government said three Houthi missiles hit coast guard sites in Aden. The fighting has ended the 2022 UN-brokered truce and, in Reuters' words, opened another front in the US–Israeli war on Iran. Yanbu and the East-West Pipeline are the kingdom's bypass around Hormuz, so even intercepted fire keeps a risk premium embedded in every Saudi barrel.

For diesel the setup is sharper still. Friday's indicative ICE low sulphur gasoil (ULS1!) quote near $1,477.50 a tonne, up about 3.1% (CMB News; we could not retrieve an official ICE settlement) shows products rallying alongside crude, and Gulf refinery exports such as Rabigh feed exactly that complex. With US refinery utilisation above 90%, a Strategic Petroleum Reserve at its lowest since 1982 and the New York Times reporting US–Israeli plans for strikes on Iran after the November 3 midterms, Monday's open carries two-way gap risk, with the skew still to the upside.

Hormuz / Shipping

Hormuz Traffic Near 80% of Pre-War Levels, but Ships Face "Indiscriminate Attacks"

CBS News' live coverage cites Vanguard, the IMO and UKMTO as counting at least 10 vessels attacked in mid-October and 23 attacks since October 1, nearly double the war's monthly average. The container ship GFS Pride sank, with Iran's IRGC saying a cargo ship hit a mine, and two seafarers are missing. Greek monitor Marisks warned ships remain exposed even with tracking off. Vanguard's Barun Gupta put traffic near 80% of pre-war levels. That explains why crude flows have largely recovered while insurance, freight and product shipments stay constrained, supporting tanker rates and diesel cracks even when crude pauses.

US Policy

Trump Leans on Diesel Diplomacy and Reserve Releases as Pump Pressure Builds

With midterms nine days away, the administration is reaching for supply-side levers. Oilprice.com reports the Energy Department is offering 315,000 barrels of diesel, one-third of its Northeast heating oil reserve, as temporary relief, and a Russian diesel tanker, the Agioi Fanendes, is heading to New York under Trump's deal with Moscow, with delivery expected around November 7. Bloomberg adds that Trump is pushing measures to lower prices before the vote. The volumes are small against a market short of distillates, so the effect is more political than physical, and the dependence on Russian cargoes complicates sanctions policy at a time when the EU is drafting a 22nd package.

05
EIA / Inventories

US Strategic Reserve at 283M Barrels, Lowest Since 1982; Crude Output Slips to 13.3M bpd

Oil & Gas Journal's read of the latest EIA weekly data shows commercial crude inventories fell by more than expected, with the Strategic Petroleum Reserve at 283 million barrels, the lowest since 1982. US crude production dropped 300,000 bpd to 13.3 million bpd from 13.6 million a year earlier. Refinery utilization is above 90%, the highest operating rates since 1998, after five months of record crack margins, and tanker rates are around $20 a barrel all-in, roughly seven times a year ago. A depleted reserve limits Washington's ability to cap prices further, while tight refining capacity, down 4% since 2019, keeps diesel margins elevated even when crude pauses.

06
OPEC+

OPEC+ Holds November Targets Flat; Next Meeting November 1 as Gulf Output Lags Quotas

OPEC+ agreed on October 4 to leave November output targets unchanged, with roughly 2 million bpd of cuts still covering most members. The group meets again on November 1. Reporting around the decision noted that Gulf producers have been pumping well below targets because of continuing export disruptions from the war, and that the capacity assessment that will shape 2027 quotas is now expected to conclude by mid-November. In practice quota decisions are largely symbolic while physical constraints bind; the real swing factors are Hormuz transit, Red Sea security and attacks on Saudi infrastructure, not the paper targets, which limits OPEC+'s ability to calm prices.

The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, October 25, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

The week closed at the highs and the weekend has not reversed the story. Brent settled Friday at $116.72, up 6.2% on the week, WTI is above $100, and the Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia now reaches Riyadh, Yanbu, Rabigh and the East-West Pipeline by the group's own account. Saudi authorities have not confirmed damage, and the coalition says it downed two missiles. That gap between claim and confirmation is what the market must price on Monday.

My read on today: Sunday is a closed market, so this is a call on the open, not on a live tape. Crude flows through Hormuz are around 80% of pre-war levels, which makes this a risk-premium rally rather than a physical crude shortage. The shortage is in products, and a Houthi threat to Rabigh and Yanbu lands directly on Red Sea product exports.

The bigger picture: Diesel remains the story underneath. Refinery utilisation above 90%, five months of record cracks, tanker rates near seven times last year's and a reserve at its lowest since 1982 leave almost no buffer. ULS1! near $1,477 a tonne, up about 3% on an indicative quote, fits that picture. Washington's small levers, 315,000 barrels of Northeast diesel and a Russian cargo due around November 7, are more political than physical.

My call: Brent trades $112–$122 around Monday's open, with upside bias on unconfirmed-but-credible weekend escalation and the November 3 midterm catalyst. Confirmed damage to Saudi export infrastructure takes it through $120 quickly; a diplomatic channel is the only thing that removes $5 or more. Respect the momentum, but size for two-way gap risk.
