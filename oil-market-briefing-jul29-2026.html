⚡ Breaking — Brent Surges 8% Past $90: Fresh Hostilities Erase the Ceasefire Rally in a Single Session

Iraqi Militias Drone Saudi Eastern Province for the Second Day; CENTCOM Intercepts Iranian Attack — Brent Crosses $90 as the Three-Day Ceasefire Trade Fully Unwinds

The three-day ceasefire rally that sent crude tumbling more than 16% is now completely erased. Brent crude surged 8.14% to $90.74 per barrel on Wednesday — its largest single-session gain since the war's early days — as fresh hostilities confirmed that the brief diplomatic window was a pause inside a war, not a prelude to peace. US Central Command confirmed intercepting an Iranian attack targeting US forces across the Middle East, a direct military signal that the pause had no strategic meaning. Iran-backed Iraqi militias launched drone strikes at Saudi oil facilities in the Eastern Region for the second consecutive day, targeting the same geographic complex where Abqaiq processing capacity — 7 million barrels per day, or roughly 7% of global oil supply — has been suspended since Monday's Houthi attack. In a single session, the market repriced from guarded optimism to full war-premium recovery.

The Eastern Province drone campaign adds a new and particularly alarming dimension to the supply-shock picture. Abqaiq, already offline since Monday, sits squarely within that complex, and repeated drone activity over the broader infrastructure cluster risks preventing any rapid restart even if Aramco's engineering teams have otherwise contained the initial blast damage. As of Wednesday's session, Aramco has still issued no official damage assessment or restart timeline. High-resolution Sentinel-2 satellite imagery has confirmed structural damage across multiple processing units with emergency flaring continuing. The 2019 Abqaiq-Khurais attack — a significantly smaller incident — required approximately five weeks to fully restore and triggered a 15% overnight price spike. The current suspension has now lasted three days with active military operations in the surrounding area, making a quick-restart scenario materially less likely with each passing hour.

Two data catalysts arrive within 36 hours that will shape the rest of the week. The EIA's weekly petroleum status report — covering the period ending July 24, 2026, the first inventory reading into the post-Abqaiq environment — will be released today and watched closely for signs of demand destruction at the $82–$102 per barrel range that defined the prior two weeks. Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh faces his first FOMC decision Thursday; his hawkish opening tone has left markets braced for tighter-than-expected guidance that would strengthen the dollar and apply a macro headwind to the crude rally. But with Brent up more than 8% on the day, Abqaiq offline, Eastern Province under drone attack, and Hormuz running at roughly 20% of normal capacity, neither the EIA print nor the Fed decision can easily overwhelm the structural supply narrative driving Wednesday's session.

CPC Terminal Resumes Kazakh Crude Exports After Week-Long Drone-Strike Halt — 1.8 mb/d Flow Restored

The Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal near Novorossiysk resumed loading operations on July 27 after a week-long halt triggered by Ukrainian drone strikes deterring tanker traffic. The facility handles approximately 1.8 million barrels per day — roughly 80% of Kazakhstan's crude exports — making it Europe's second-largest oil pipeline system by volume. The SEAMAJESTY and MILOS tankers began loading crude from Chevron's Tengizchevroil project, restoring a supply line that had cut Tengiz field output to as low as 1 million barrels per day on its worst day, versus a June average of 2.16 mb/d. The resumption had contributed to Tuesday's bearish sentiment and a portion of the three-session ceasefire drop. That partial supply return is now fully overwhelmed by Wednesday's Middle East escalation: Abqaiq alone processes nearly four times the CPC terminal's daily volume.

OPEC+

OPEC+ Approved 188,000 bbl/d for August — Fifth Consecutive Monthly Hike; Largely Theoretical Amid Hormuz Closure

Seven core OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman — agreed on July 5 to raise collective crude production by 188,000 barrels per day for August, the fifth consecutive monthly increase. The increase matches the June hike, which had itself been revised down from 206,000 bbl/d after the UAE exited the arrangement in May. The group's joint communiqué framed the adjustment as an "opportunity for the participating countries to accelerate their compensation." In practice, the output increase remains largely notional: the Strait of Hormuz closure has physically prevented Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Iraq — three of the seven signatories — from exporting at their stated quotas for much of the year. With Abqaiq now adding a processing bottleneck on top of the export-route constraints, the August hike's actual impact on global supply is expected to be minimal.

EIA Weekly Report Due Today: Week Ending July 24 — First Inventory Data Into the Abqaiq Disruption Window

The EIA's weekly petroleum status report, covering the period ending July 24, 2026, is scheduled for release today — the first inventory reading to capture any portion of the US-Iran pause, the subsequent selloff, and the early stages of the Abqaiq disruption. The prior report (week ending July 17) showed a modest crude build of 2.0 million barrels to 411.7 million barrels, leaving US stockpiles 6% below the five-year average with refinery utilization at 96.1%. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve stood at 311.4 million barrels, roughly 91 million barrels below year-ago levels after extended crisis drawdowns. Analysts will focus on whether the $85–$102 price range of the prior two weeks has begun to show demand destruction in refined product data — particularly distillate inventories, which were already running 10% below the five-year average. A surprise crude draw (greater than 3 mb) confirms resilient demand; a build would suggest the IEA's demand-decline thesis is taking hold at the pump.

The Guru's Verdict — Wednesday, July 29, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called the session the most technically difficult of the war — two forces of equal magnitude colliding in real time: the Abqaiq supply shock setting the floor, the US-Iran pause setting the ceiling. Today, that framing is resolved. The ceiling is gone. The pause lasted 48 hours and ended not with a negotiated framework but with CENTCOM confirming it intercepted an Iranian attack — which is not a de-escalation, it is a resumption of war under a different operational tempo. The ceiling traders had priced via the ceasefire trade has been removed, and the floor — Abqaiq — remains exactly where it was and is now actively hardened by militia drone activity directly over the complex.

My read on today: The directional skew is now unambiguously to the upside, and the risk profile is asymmetric. The bull case requires no new developments — the existing supply shock, active military operations, and closed Hormuz are doing all the work. The bear case requires a simultaneous resolution of at least two separate crises: Abqaiq restarting quickly and a credible new ceasefire signal. Neither is imminent. The Eastern Province drone campaign actively delays the restart scenario. Iran's explicit rejection of formal US negotiations removes the ceasefire scenario. What remains is a market biased higher, with the EIA report and the Fed the only near-term events that can temporarily redirect the tape.

The bigger picture: Three data points arrive within the next 24 hours that matter. If the EIA shows a large crude draw (demand holding at $85–102 levels), Abqaiq issues a multi-week repair timeline, and Warsh's Fed signals a hold rather than a hike — all three break bullish for crude and the $93–97 range opens. If the EIA shows a surprise build, Aramco somehow confirms quick-track restart access despite the drone campaign, and Warsh is aggressively hawkish — you get a partial retracement toward $86–88. But the probability-weighted scenario is the former: demand destruction takes time to show up in inventory data, Abqaiq access is physically constrained, and Warsh's base case is a hold. The market knows this.

My call: Brent holds $88–$97 today, biased toward the upper half of that range while the Eastern Province drone campaign is active and Abqaiq guidance is pending. The ceasefire trade is closed. The supply-shock trade is open. Watch for Aramco's damage statement and the EIA print — together they set the week's close.
