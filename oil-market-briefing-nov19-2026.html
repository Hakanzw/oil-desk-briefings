⚡ Thursday Open — EIA Confirms Big US Builds, but Hormuz Attacks Hold Brent Near $92

Brent Edges Up 0.13% to $92.07 While Gasoil Slips 0.70% to $1,171.25/mt as EIA Confirms Builds Across Crude, Gasoline and Distillates — ADNOC Vessel Attack Keeps Hormuz Risk Live

Brent is quoted at $92.07/bbl, ▲ +0.13% (+$0.12), essentially flat near the bottom of the $91–$98 range that has framed trade for weeks, while Low Sulphur Gasoil (ULS1!) fell a further ▼ −0.70% (−$8.25) to $1,171.25/mt, extending a slide that has taken the distillate benchmark from above $1,250 earlier this week to its lowest level in this run. WTI was near $83.05 on the Oilprice ticker. The divergence tells the story: crude is range-bound because supply fears and glut fears offset each other, while diesel keeps deflating as traders bet that product exports normalise and that inventories are rebuilding. Brent was a month ago above $100; it has now shed roughly 8% without any peace deal or ceasefire in the Gulf.

The official inventory data reinforced the bearish side. The EIA reported on Wednesday that US commercial crude stocks rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week ended November 13, against analyst expectations in a Reuters poll of a build of only about 58,000 barrels. Gasoline jumped 5.2 million barrels to 215.9 million versus a forecast 1.6 million build, and distillates rose 1.6 million barrels to 104.9 million, slightly above the 1.3 million expected. Cushing drew 1.2 million barrels, refinery utilisation rose 0.5 points to 95.7%, and crude exports rose 1.03 million bpd to 4.49 million bpd. Brent fell as much as $1.29 to $91.85 intraday after the release, before recovering. The distillate build is the key detail for diesel: the cushion is thickening.

The offset is a Strait of Hormuz that remains hazardous. ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels was attacked while transiting the strait on the evening of November 17, leaving one crew member unaccounted for and 11 injured, a week after another ADNOC vessel was damaged in UAE waters. Iran reports near-daily incidents of tankers striking mines in corridors it calls "unauthorized," and negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf told Al Jazeera that navigation will never return to pre-war conditions. For crude, glut headlines cap rallies and Hormuz headlines cushion dips; for diesel, freight and insurance costs are the transmission channel that keeps a floor under gasoil.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Inventories

EIA: US Crude Stocks Rise 3.4M Barrels, Gasoline Jumps 5.2M, Distillates Up 1.6M

The EIA reported that US commercial crude inventories rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week ended November 13, far above the roughly 58,000-barrel build analysts polled by Reuters had expected. Gasoline stocks rose 5.2 million barrels to 215.9 million against a forecast 1.6 million build, and distillate stocks, covering diesel and heating oil, climbed 1.6 million barrels to 104.9 million versus an expected 1.3 million. Cushing drew 1.2 million barrels, refinery runs fell 129,000 bpd even as utilisation edged up to 95.7%, and net crude imports dropped 2.15 million bpd as exports rose 1.03 million bpd to 4.49 million bpd. Brent dipped to $91.85 after the release. For crude, a third large build in a row feeds the surplus narrative; for diesel, the distillate gain slows the tightening story and helps explain gasoil's slide.

02
Hormuz

ADNOC Vessel Attacked in Hormuz — One Crew Member Missing, 11 Injured

ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of November 17. One crew member remained unaccounted for early Wednesday, 11 were injured and the rest were evacuated safely. ADNOC said its immediate priority was supporting the search and rescue effort and reiterated "the importance of protecting seafarers and safeguarding freedom of navigation and maritime security." A week earlier, on November 10, another ADNOC L&S vessel was damaged in UAE waters. Iran's IRGC Navy has warned tanker owners that the US-backed southern passage is mined and that cooperating with US military officials would put vessels at risk. For crude, repeated strikes on Gulf-owned shipping keep a geopolitical floor under Brent despite the glut forecasts. For diesel, higher war-risk premiums raise delivered product costs into Europe.

Hormuz Flows

Small Tankers Carry a Record Share of Hormuz Crude as Iran Targets Supertankers

Suezmax and Aframax tankers moved more crude out of the Strait of Hormuz in October than in any month since the war began in February, shipping about 2 million bpd, according to Vortexa data cited by Bloomberg. Most cargoes were transferred ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman, with the shift accelerating in September as supertanker attacks hit a wartime high and VLCC rates reached records. Traders said the smaller ships mostly serve routes from Kuwait, Iraq and Qatar. Iran reports nearly daily incidents of tankers hitting mines in corridors it has deemed unauthorized, and Ghalibaf says the 1982 UN Convention on the Law of the Sea does not apply. For crude, the workaround shows the market can route around the chokepoint, which explains the muted price response to attacks. For diesel, higher freight costs per barrel linger in product prices.

04
OPEC+ / Supply

Iraq Pumps 4.6M bpd, Highest Since 2019; Saudi Output Back Near Pre-War Levels

Iraq's crude output reached 4.6 million bpd in October, its highest since 2019, according to the IEA's November Oil Market Report, up 540,000 bpd from September and about 170,000 bpd above its OPEC+ quota. Kuwait raised output from 1.58 million to 2.03 million bpd, still about 650,000 bpd under its ceiling. Saudi Arabia reported October production of 10.563 million bpd to OPEC, slightly above a roughly 10.5 million bpd quota, though the IEA estimates 9.99 million bpd. Saudi officials noted part of the jump went straight into storage, so supply to the market was 9.2 million bpd. With OPEC+ due to meet November 29 on 2027 quotas, rising Gulf output complicates discipline. For crude, it adds to the surplus forecasts from the IEA and Goldman; for diesel, it adds crude feedstock for Gulf refiners.

Energy Bills

Cornwall Insight: UK Price Cap Set to Jump 26% in January on Iran War Gas Costs

Consultancy Cornwall Insight said UK household energy bills will rise another 26% from January 2027, taking the typical annual cap to £2,166, up £443 and the highest since 2023. Ofgem had already lifted the cap 4% for October to December, citing higher wholesale gas prices from the Middle East conflict. Craig Lowrey, retail markets principal at Cornwall Insight, said: "After already climbing in October, energy bills look set to rise again in the new year," adding that "the cause lies thousands of miles away." Analysts expect rises of 25% to 30% in the first quarter, and colder January weather would lift consumption further. For oil, expensive gas and power encourage fuel switching to gasoil and heating oil, a structural support for distillates this winter, even as demand destruction caps consumer spending.

US Shale / M&A

Diamondback Buys Double Eagle's 40% Midland Basin Stake for About $5 Billion

Diamondback Energy agreed on Monday to buy Double Eagle's 40% share of their Midland Basin partnership, a deal Reuters valued at about $5.03 billion in cash and stock: $2.15 billion in cash plus roughly 15.56 million shares. The assets cover about 176,000 net acres, with expected 2027 output of 92,000 boe/d including about 35,000 bpd of oil, and more than 1,200 gross horizontal locations. Diamondback expects the deal to be immediately accretive and leverage neutral, with closing planned for December 21. CEO Kaes Van't Hof called it the culmination of the partnership. The company's first full-section Barnett development peaked near 7,500 bpd of oil. For crude, consolidation of core acreage signals US operators are planning long-run growth even at $90 Brent, which supports the 2027 glut thesis; the near-term price impact is minimal.

The Guru's Verdict — Thursday, November 19, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I argued that surplus sets the direction and Hormuz sets the volatility, and that the EIA print would be the swing factor for the products complex. The data delivered: crude +3.4 million barrels, gasoline +5.2 million, distillates +1.6 million, all larger than or equal to expectations. Brent is at $92.07, up 0.13%, but gasoil has slid a further 0.70% to $1,171.25/mt. That is a market refusing to rally on risk headlines.

My read on today: the distillate complex is again the more informative tape. Gasoil is losing ground because US distillate stocks are rebuilding, refinery utilisation is at 95.7%, and Gulf producers are lifting output, with Iraq at 4.6 million bpd and Saudi Arabia reporting 10.56 million bpd. The market now believes barrels, including those moved on Suezmaxes and Aframaxes through ship-to-ship transfers, are finding a way out of the Gulf.

The bigger picture: risks remain two-sided but asymmetrical. The downside is a slow grind toward Goldman's $78 end-2027 view, but the upside is jumpy: an ADNOC vessel attack with a crew member missing, Iran insisting that no free waters exist in the strait, and a European cold snap arriving with storage near 70%. Those are reasons why dips toward $90 should keep finding buyers, even as rallies above $95 meet supply.

My call: Brent trades $89–$95 today, with a mildly lower bias and an upside tail on any verified tanker loss or cold-weather gas spike. Gasoil should keep probing $1,150–$1,200 and remains the weaker leg until the winter demand picture clarifies. The surplus is real, the chokepoint is real. Trade the range and respect the tail.
