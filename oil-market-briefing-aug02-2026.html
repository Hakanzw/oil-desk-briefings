July Wrap & August 2 Outlook — IRGC Tanker Strikes Cap a Wild Month; OPEC+ JMMC Convenes Today

IRGC Hits Two US-Escorted Tankers in Hormuz to Close Out July — Brent Holds $87.93 as OPEC+ Monitoring Committee Meets Today on September Quota

July ended the way it lived: violently. On July 31, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps claimed it struck and halted two oil tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz under US military escort, releasing imagery of one vessel on fire. Four additional tankers diverted course. US Central Command disputed Iran's claim to authority over the waterway, but the market recorded its verdict — WTI closed +1.29% at $84.67 and Brent finished +1.21% at $87.93, capping a month in which the international benchmark gained approximately 21%, the strongest single-month advance since early 2022. The July 31 IRGC action confirms what the supply data had already signalled: the Hormuz corridor remains functionally closed to normal commerce, and any residual expectation that the physical situation would normalise without a formal diplomatic breakthrough was extinguished before the calendar turned.

The backdrop for today's OPEC+ Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee meeting is therefore one of acute tension between paper quotas and physical reality. Seven core members agreed on July 5 to raise collective output by 188,000 barrels per day for August — the fifth consecutive monthly hike — but the decision exists largely in abstraction. Saudi, Iraqi, and Kuwaiti barrels earmarked for export still require Hormuz passage, and the IRGC's July 31 action demonstrates that the strait remains contested at the military level. The committee's September quota decision will reveal whether the group is pricing a corridor it does not yet control — or stepping back to acknowledge reality. A pause in the hike schedule would be unambiguously bullish for spot prices; an acceleration would test the market's confidence in physical delivery.

The US commercial crude inventory picture reinforces the structural underpinning. The EIA's report for the week ending July 24 showed stockpiles at 404.5 million barrels — a draw of 7.2 million barrels and roughly 6% below the five-year seasonal average. Refinery inputs averaged 17.3 million bpd at a utilisation rate of 97.2%, meaning downstream demand for crude is not the constraint. The constraint is incoming supply, and the July 31 tanker incident made clear that the supply-side constraint is not easing. Meanwhile, the week's second-order story — Big Oil's record Q2 earnings — provided a market-structure signal: Chevron posted its largest quarterly profit ever at $12.1 billion, ExxonMobil earned $14.5 billion, and Shell reported $10.8 billion, collectively signalling that demand destruction is not materialising at scale.

OPEC+

OPEC+ Agrees Fifth Consecutive Output Hike for August — JMMC Monitoring Committee Meets Today on September

Seven core OPEC+ members agreed on July 5 to raise collective output by 188,000 barrels per day for August — the fifth consecutive monthly increase since the group began unwinding its 2023 cuts. The group retained language that future hikes could be "accelerated, paused, or reversed" depending on market conditions. Today's Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee meeting is the first opportunity to set September's quota. With Brent at $87.93 and Hormuz throughput still at roughly 11% of its pre-war baseline — compounded by the July 31 IRGC tanker strike — analysts say paper increases are not translating into delivered barrels, making the September decision a binary market catalyst.

EIA Reports 7.2M-Barrel Crude Draw — US Stocks Fall to 404.5M bbl, 6% Below Five-Year Seasonal Average

The EIA's weekly petroleum status report for the week ending July 24 showed US commercial crude stockpiles falling 7.2 million barrels to 404.5 million barrels — roughly 6% below the five-year seasonal average. Supply Corridor

Kazakhstan CPC Terminal Resumes Black Sea Exports After Drone Strike — Ceyhan Contamination a Secondary Wrinkle

Kazakhstan's Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal at Novorossiysk resumed operations on July 27 following a suspension caused by drone strikes on the SEAMAJESTY and MILOS tankers, with Tengizchevroil loading restarting almost immediately. The resumption restores roughly 1 million bpd of Kazakh supply after the shutdown had halved output from a normal 2.16 million bpd. Commerzbank flagged that chloride contamination also triggered inspections at Turkey's Ceyhan port, affecting Azerbaijani and Kazakhstani flows of approximately 560,000 bpd combined — though both channels have since resumed. The episode illustrates how the energy-infrastructure disruption campaign has extended from the Persian Gulf into the Black Sea.

The Guru's Verdict — Sunday, August 2, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

August 2 opens with the oil market exactly where July's extraordinary arithmetic left it: structurally bid, geopolitically charged, and waiting for a catalyst that does not yet exist. The July 31 IRGC tanker strikes were not a surprise — they were the logical terminus of a month that opened with US airstrikes on Iran, saw Brent briefly touch $100, watched a ceasefire signal briefly deflate the premium, and then reasserted the supply-shock floor when diplomacy went quiet. The Strait of Hormuz is handling 14 tanker transits per day against a pre-war baseline of 88–100. That is not a market experiencing supply disruption. That is a market experiencing a supply removal — and the July 31 incident confirmed that US military escort has not changed that calculus.

My read on today's OPEC+ meeting: The JMMC meeting is the week's most important scheduled event, and the market's reaction will tell you everything about sentiment. If the committee pauses the hike cycle — acknowledging that quota increases are not translating into delivered barrels when the delivery corridor is contested at gunpoint — that is unambiguously bullish: Brent tests $92–$95 this week. If the committee presses ahead with another 188k bpd September hike, that is market neutral at best; the physical market will ignore it because the barrels cannot reach customers. The third scenario — acceleration — signals overconfidence and would likely attract selling above $91. My base case: a pause, or a heavily qualified hike with language explicitly tying further increases to Hormuz normalisation.

The bigger picture after July: Big Oil's record earnings season puts the structural dynamics in the sharpest possible relief. Chevron's $12.1 billion quarterly record and ExxonMobil's $14.5 billion Q2 net income are not aberrations — they are receipts from a supply shock that the market can now price with quarterly precision. And the CEO commentary was the most telling signal of the week: no demand destruction at current prices. The global consumer is absorbing $85–$90 crude without capitulating. That removes one of the few credible bearish arguments still standing — that high prices would self-correct via demand erosion. They are not. The market is tight, the consumer is paying, and the supply corridor is still closed.

My call: Brent $85–$93 this week, biased toward the upper half following OPEC+'s likely pause and the continued Hormuz physical constraint. The floor — anchored by a 6%-below-average inventory reading and 14-per-day Hormuz throughput — is real and tested. The ceiling is the diplomatic variable, and diplomacy was set back by Friday's tanker strikes. Respect the bid below $86; sell the spike above $93 only with confirmed de-escalation signals from both sides.
