Top Story

⚡ Escalation — Brent Reaches a Five-Month High as Houthi Strikes Spread Across Saudi Arabia

Brent Pushes Toward $114 as Houthi Attacks Hit Saudi Targets and Aramco Evacuates Dhahran Headquarters — Gasoil Jumps 2.9%

Crude extended its climb on Friday, with Trading Economics showing Brent near $113.91, up about 0.65%, its highest level in more than five months. Thursday's session did the heavy lifting: Reuters reported Brent was up $2.42 to $111.24 by mid-morning New York time and set for its highest close since May 18, and Bloomberg noted the contract settled above $113 with WTI near $99 after a 2.4% gain. The driver is an expanding Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities said two people died when debris from an intercepted drone fell near a processing plant at Aramco's Shaybah field, and an attack on Dammam's King Fahd airport killed one and injured eight. Reuters added that Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters.

Diesel is moving faster than crude. Investing.com's London gasoil page showed the November contract at 1,486.63 a tonne, up 2.90% from 1,444.75, though the page's own text cites a 2.4% gain and warns data may not be real-time, so treat it as indicative. Meanwhile the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Navy claimed seven vessels were hit in the Persian Gulf, four tankers struck during oil transfers and three that hit mines west of Hormuz, and warned of a wider response against US bases. Morgan Stanley estimates Middle East crude exports near 17 million barrels a day, against roughly 18 million before the war, so the physical gap is narrower than the headlines imply.

PVM's Tamas Varga argued that export gains are "more than countered by attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz" and Houthi strikes on Saudi infrastructure, adding that no de-escalation is expected before the US midterm elections. Trading Economics also cited reports of possible renewed US and Israeli strikes on Iran after the midterms, which we could not independently confirm. The asymmetry is clear: damage to the 7 million bpd East-West pipeline or Yanbu would add several dollars quickly, while a credible diplomatic channel could remove $5 or more just as fast.

Today's Key Headlines

01
Saudi / Houthis

Shaybah Drone Debris Kills Two as Dammam Airport Attack Adds Casualties

The National reported that a Houthi drone was intercepted near Aramco's Shaybah field, which produces about 1 million barrels a day, with debris killing two people near a processing plant. The Saudi Energy Ministry said operations were unaffected. Hours later an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed one and injured eight, according to the civil aviation authority. An earlier strike on Riyadh's main airport this month was the deadliest in the kingdom in over a decade, and the East-West pipeline, capacity about 7 million bpd, was reportedly halted on October 5 after a pumping station was hit. For crude, the issue is the Red Sea bypass around Hormuz; for diesel, it is Saudi refined-product exports.

Hormuz / Iran

IRGC Navy Says Seven Vessels Hit in Persian Gulf and Threatens US Bases

Oilprice.com, citing Reuters, reported that the IRGC Navy said four tankers conducting oil transfers in the northern Gulf were hit by Iranian forces, and three more vessels struck mines west of the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC warned that further US provocations could bring a wider response against American bases. The report noted Saudi Arabia has largely restored exports via both Hormuz and the Red Sea, and that the Houthis say they are answering a Saudi-led coalition offensive aimed at the Red Sea coast and Bab el-Mandeb. Shipping insurance and tanker availability, not just barrels, now drive the risk premium, and that tends to feed diesel faster than crude.

Market Reaction

Brent Overbought for a Third Time in a Week; PVM Sees No Relief Before Midterms

Reuters reported Brent entered technically overbought territory for the third time in a week and was heading for its highest close since May 18. PVM Oil analyst Tamas Varga said Middle East export gains are more than offset by ship attacks and Houthi strikes, and that no de-escalation is anticipated until the US midterm elections. Morgan Stanley put Middle East crude exports near 17 million bpd last week, versus about 18 million bpd in the 12 months before the Iran war per Kpler. Valero separately posted its highest-ever quarterly profit as disruptions lifted refining margins, a signal that product tightness, not crude, is where the squeeze is most visible.

04
Aramco / Yanbu

Houthis Claim Strikes on Yanbu, Riyadh Defence Ministry; Saudi Cannot Confirm

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed strikes on Aramco facilities in Yanbu, the Saudi defence ministry in Riyadh and Dammam airport. Saudi authorities had not immediately confirmed damage to Yanbu or the ministry, and there was no independent verification. Yanbu is the Red Sea terminal at the end of the East-West pipeline and the kingdom's main route around Hormuz, so even a modest confirmed hit would matter. Saudi Arabia has responded with strikes on missile launch sites. Trading Economics cited the Yanbu claims as a driver of Friday's push toward $114. Unverified claims move prices now, and confirmation or denial is the next catalyst.

05
OPEC+

OPEC+ Core Group Holds November Targets Flat; Next Meeting Nov 1

Gulf News reported that seven core OPEC+ members, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman, agreed on October 4 to keep November production targets unchanged, with changes unlikely before 2027 because a capacity review is needed to allocate increases. Sources said Gulf exports have fluctuated at roughly 60% to 80% of normal in recent months, so actual output sits well below quotas. The group meets again on November 1 to consider December plans. The practical effect is minimal for barrels, since war-constrained supply, not quotas, is the binding limit, but it leaves no spare-capacity cushion if Saudi infrastructure is damaged.

Natural Gas / Supply

Henry Hub Slips to Two-Month Low Near $2.72 on Mild Weather; Libya Valve Trims Output

Trading Economics showed US natural gas near $2.72 per MMBtu, down 2.56%, the lowest in two months, as milder forecasts cut heating-demand expectations, offsetting smaller-than-usual storage builds and strong LNG feedgas demand. Separately, Reuters reported a pipeline valve carrying crude from Libya's Hamada and Tahara fields to Zawiya port was shut, trimming output, per the National Oil Corporation chief. Russia also said it is ready to resume US-mediated peace talks next month, which would matter for sanctioned barrels. Gas remains a sideshow to crude, but weak US gas keeps associated-gas drilling economics unaffected while European gas stays sensitive to the EU's Russian LNG debate.

The Guru's Verdict — Friday, October 23, 2026

Oil Trading Intelligence — Independent Market Opinion

Yesterday I called Brent $107–$116 with an upward bias, and the market delivered: Thursday settled above $113 and Friday quotes sit near $113.91, a five-month high. The range held, but we are in its upper third, and the character of the news is worse, because Houthi claims now reach Yanbu, the very terminal that lets Saudi crude bypass Hormuz.

My read on today: Diesel is again the sharper instrument. Gasoil at roughly $1,486.63 a tonne, up about 2.9% on Investing.com's quote, is outrunning crude, and US distillates, even after a 1.7 million barrel build, remain about 9% under the five-year average. Valero's record quarter tells you where the scarcity is. I expect cracks to stay wide while Gulf product flows and tanker insurance are impaired.

The bigger picture: Morgan Stanley's 17 million bpd export estimate suggests barrels are mostly flowing, which is why this rally is a risk-premium story, and three overbought readings in a week say positioning is stretched. PVM does not expect de-escalation before the midterms. That makes dips shallow until a verified diplomatic channel appears, but any such channel, or Saudi denial of Yanbu damage, could unwind $5 or more quickly. Natural gas at $2.72 is a quiet sideshow.

My call: Brent trades $108–$118 today, with an upward bias while Saudi infrastructure risk dominates and high odds of volatile two-way trade into the weekend. The premium is large and fragile; only confirmed safety for Saudi export routes and Gulf shipping can truly remove it.
